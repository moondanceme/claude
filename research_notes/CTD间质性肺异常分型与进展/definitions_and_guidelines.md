# Definitions and guidelines: can the "ILA" framework be applied to CTD, and how do CTD-ILD guidelines handle minimal, early or subclinical CT changes?

Retrieval note: full texts were read from PMC for Fleischner 2020 (PMC7970441), ACR/CHEST 2023 (PMC12646460), ERS/EULAR 2025 (ERJ version, PMC12824641) and ATS 2025 (PMC12264694). Everything else comes from PubMed abstracts only. Quoted text is verbatim from those sources. The Chinese consensus documents could be read only at abstract level (see Gaps).

## Q1. Does Fleischner 2020 exclude CTD and other at-risk groups from "ILA"? Is the exclusion contested?

### Takeaway
Yes. Fleischner 2020 explicitly excludes abnormalities found while screening high-risk groups (RA, SSc/scleroderma, familial ILD, occupational exposure). It says these "might be referred to as preclinical ILD" and leaves them out of scope. In July 2025 the ATS Clinical Statement deliberately removed that exclusion. ILA is now defined purely by imaging, it can apply in CTD, and the ATS suggests baseline CT screening "for ILAs/ILD" in high-risk CTD. So the terminology is contested, and the latest pulmonology statement disagrees with the original Fleischner framing. Neither the rheumatology-led guidelines (ACR/CHEST 2023, ERS/EULAR 2025) nor the RA studies that use the term have reconciled the two.

### Cited Findings
- Fleischner 2020 definition (Panel 1): ILA = "Incidental identification of non-dependent abnormalities, including ground-glass or reticular abnormalities, lung distortion, traction bronchiectasis, honeycombing, and non-emphysematous cysts; Involving at least 5% of a lung zone ...; In individuals in whom interstitial lung disease is not suspected." — Hatabu H et al., Lancet Respir Med 2020;8:726-737, PMID 32649920, [DOI 10.1016/S2213-2600(20)30168-5](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2213-2600(20)30168-5)
- Fleischner 2020, main text (verbatim): "Abnormalities identified during screening for ILD in high-risk groups (eg, those with rheumatoid arthritis, systemic sclerosis, or familial ILD) are not considered as ILAs because they are not incidental; these might be referred to as preclinical ILD and their management is beyond the scope of this Position Paper." — [Hatabu 2020, PMID 32649920](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2213-2600(20)30168-5)
- Fleischner Panel 1, "What are not ILAs?", includes: "Preclinical interstitial abnormalities identified during screening of high-risk individuals (eg, those with rheumatoid arthritis, scleroderma, occupational exposure, familial interstitial lung disease)" and "Findings in patients with known clinical interstitial lung disease". — [Hatabu 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2213-2600(20)30168-5)
- Fleischner on terminology: earlier literature had used "interstitial lung changes at an early phase, early ILD, ILD, subclinical ILD, and preclinical ILD". "ILAs are not synonymous with subclinical ILD because a subset of patients with ILAs has symptoms and lung function impairment without suspected ILD." When clinically significant findings are present, "ILAs are likely to represent mild ILD rather than subclinical abnormalities." The paper also says HRCT "is highly sensitive for detecting subclinical interstitial abnormalities in high-risk populations, such as patients with connective tissue disease (eg, systemic sclerosis)". — [Hatabu 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2213-2600(20)30168-5)
- Fleischner on the 5% threshold: "Although the 5% threshold is arbitrary and imprecise, it is retained to exclude minimal opacities and to conform to previous published literature." Subcategories are non-subpleural, subpleural non-fibrotic and subpleural fibrotic. Panel 3 ("Key uncertainties") lists "Reproducibility of radiological criteria", "Natural history of non-fibrotic ILAs" and "Optimal extent thresholds for predicting significant physiological progression...". — [Hatabu 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2213-2600(20)30168-5)
- Hata et al. (Radiology State of the Art 2021, Fleischner-aligned authors): "Clinically significant interstitial lung disease should not be considered ILA." — PMID 34374589, [DOI 10.1148/radiol.2021204367](https://doi.org/10.1148/radiol.2021204367)
- **ATS 2025 Clinical Statement reverses the exclusion.** Abstract: "The updated definition removes the prior exclusion of high-risk populations." Table 1: "The need for findings to be incidental and exclusion of high-risk populations has purposefully been removed from the definition." The ILA definition is otherwise "nondependent bilateral parenchymal abnormalities ... involving ≥5% of a lung zone", "but without meeting the criteria for ILD". — Podolanczuk AJ et al., Am J Respir Crit Care Med 2025;211:1132-1155, PMID 40387336, [DOI 10.1164/rccm.202505-1054ST](https://doi.org/10.1164/rccm.202505-1054ST)
- ATS 2025 rationale (verbatim): "First, a definition of ILA that is independent of pretest probability is more practical for clinical use, particularly because radiologists frequently lack the necessary patient details to confirm whether these nonimaging criteria are met. Second, ILAs can still be present in high-risk populations, and incorporating these groups within the definition of ILAs allows the committee to provide guidance for evaluation and management of these patients. Finally, in the absence of compelling data to suggest otherwise, it is simpler to create a relatively broad definition..." — [Podolanczuk 2025](https://doi.org/10.1164/rccm.202505-1054ST)
- ATS 2025 working ILD definition (Table 3). A person with ILA-type CT features has ILD if **any one** of the following is present:
  - any dyspnoea or cough a clinician attributes to ILD;
  - FVC, TLC or DLCO below the LLN, or physiological PPF criteria;
  - fibrotic abnormality (honeycombing and/or reticulation with traction bronchiectasis) involving ≥5% of **total** lung volume;
  - progressive fibrotic abnormality on serial CT;
  - a major fibrotic ILD pattern (UIP/probable UIP, fibrotic HP, fibrotic NSIP) on CT or pathology.

  Unilateral abnormality "may be sufficient to characterize as ILA in some populations, e.g., with a family history of familial pulmonary fibrosis." — [Podolanczuk 2025](https://doi.org/10.1164/rccm.202505-1054ST)
- ATS 2025 conceptual premises: "although normal lung, ILAs, and ILD likely represent a spectrum"; definitions should balance sensitivity and specificity "for disease progression and the need for treatment, recognizing that not all 'disease' requires treatment"; "Although the 5% threshold needs additional supporting data, there was insufficient evidence or rationale to suggest an alternative." CTD is listed in Table 4 as a demographic or clinical feature associated with increased risk of ILA progression. — [Podolanczuk 2025](https://doi.org/10.1164/rccm.202505-1054ST)
- A 2026 narrative review says the ATS statement "has reshaped the definition of ILAs proposed by the Fleischner Society in 2020, introducing substantial changes to the populations in whom ILAs may be identified and proposing new boundaries to distinguish ILAs from ILD. Nevertheless, several key aspects remain debated." It names CTD patients specifically as a relevant population. — Balbi M et al., Semin Respir Crit Care Med 2026, PMID 42863827, [DOI 10.1055/a-2969-5614](https://doi.org/10.1055/a-2969-5614)
- RA literature used "ILA" in CTD both before and after Fleischner 2020:
  - Doyle 2014 (BRASS) graded "ILAs" on clinically indicated CTs in RA using a sequential reading method. — PMID 24305643, [DOI 10.1378/chest.13-1394](https://doi.org/10.1378/chest.13-1394)
  - An accompanying editorial was titled "Interstitial lung abnormalities in rheumatoid arthritis are common and important" (no abstract available). — Fischer A, Cosgrove GP, Chest 2014, PMID 25010956, [DOI 10.1378/chest.14-0030](https://doi.org/10.1378/chest.14-0030)
  - Kawano-Dourado 2020 split RA patients into "ILA" vs "ILD" using clinical record review. 29% of ILA cases progressed over 4.4 years, and subpleural distribution and higher extent predicted progression. — PMID 32428513, [DOI 10.1016/j.chest.2020.04.061](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chest.2020.04.061)
  - McDermott 2023 (COPDGene): ILA in 16.9% of RA vs 5.0% of non-RA. RA with ILA or indeterminate ILA had HR 3.16 for mortality. The authors frame this as "preclinical ILD in RA". — PMID 37871923, [DOI 10.1093/rheumatology/kead277](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/kead277)
  - Juge 2022 used the term "subclinical RA-ILD" (prevalence 19.0% and 16.9% in two cohorts of asymptomatic RA screened by HRCT). — PMID 35583934, [DOI 10.1002/art.42162](https://doi.org/10.1002/art.42162)
  - Leone & Richeldi 2020 commentary: "ILA/ILD extension and subpleural ILA localisation are risk factors for disease progression in RA subjects." — Breathe 2020, PMID 33664837, [DOI 10.1183/20734735.0223-2020](https://doi.org/10.1183/20734735.0223-2020)
- A 2021 expert survey (ILA Study Group) framed ILA as possibly "undiagnosed early-stage or subclinical ILD" and reached consensus that "patients with systemic sclerosis should be screened with pulmonary function tests for early-stage ILD". It uses "early-stage ILD" rather than ILA wording for SSc. — Hunninghake GM et al., Chest 2022, PMID 34197782, [DOI 10.1016/j.chest.2021.06.035](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chest.2021.06.035)

### Inferences
- Under Fleischner 2020, "Does Fleischner ILA subtyping predict progression in CTD?" is strictly a category error. Screening-detected findings in CTD are by definition not ILA ("preclinical ILD"). Under ATS 2025 the question is legitimate, but only for findings that fail the ATS ILD criteria: no attributable symptoms, normal PFTs, fibrotic extent <5% of total lung volume, no UIP/NSIP/fHP pattern, and no progression.
- In practice many CTD patients with "minimal" CT changes will meet the ATS ILD definition. Reasons include an NSIP pattern, a low DLCO from pulmonary vascular disease, or symptoms. The ILA (ATS) subgroup in CTD is therefore a residual, narrower group than "all minimal abnormalities", and the denominator depends on which definition is used.
- A defensible reframing is "early/limited CTD-ILD and CTD-ILA (ATS 2025) imaging phenotyping". Fleischner/ATS subcategories (non-subpleural, subpleural non-fibrotic, subpleural fibrotic) would be used as descriptive imaging features, and the paper would report sensitivity analyses under both the Fleischner 2020 and ATS 2025 definitions. Stating which definition is used is mandatory, because the two definitions are now in direct conflict.

### Gaps
- No published editorial or letter was retrieved that explicitly debates the ATS 2025 removal of the high-risk exclusion as it applies to CTD; only the 2026 narrative review says "several key aspects remain debated". The Fischer 2014 editorial had no abstract and its content was not verified.
- It was not verified whether the ACR, EULAR or any rheumatology society has formally responded to or adopted the ATS 2025 ILA definition.

## Q2. ACR/CHEST 2023 guideline (screening and monitoring ILD in SARDs)

### Takeaway
ACR/CHEST covers RA, SSc, IIM, MCTD and SjD (SLE is excluded). It conditionally recommends PFTs plus HRCT for screening at-risk patients. It never uses the term "ILA" and gives no extent threshold for "ILD present". It openly accepts that screening "may identify mild, asymptomatic ILD that is not progressive" and justifies screening by patient preference. Once ILD is found, the monitoring advice (PFT intervals) applies regardless of extent.

### Cited Findings
- Scope and recommendations: 15 recommendations.
  - Screening: PFTs and HRCT are conditionally recommended. 6MWD, chest radiography, ambulatory desaturation and bronchoscopy are conditionally recommended against. Surgical lung biopsy is strongly recommended against.
  - Monitoring: PFTs, HRCT and ambulatory desaturation testing are conditionally recommended.
  - Source: Johnson SR et al., Arthritis Care Res 2024;76:1070-1082, PMID 38973729, [DOI 10.1002/acr.25347](https://doi.org/10.1002/acr.25347) (simultaneously in Arthritis Rheumatol and Chest)
- Who to screen: "Although most people with SSc and many people with IIM warrant screening, it is not necessary to screen all people with RA or SjD." Table 1 lists risk factors:
  - SSc: anti-Scl-70, nucleolar ANA, diffuse subtype, male sex, African American race, first 5–7 years of disease, raised acute-phase reactants.
  - RA: high-titre RF or anti-CCP, smoking, older age at onset, high disease activity, male sex, higher BMI.
  - IIM: anti-synthetase, MDA-5, Ku, PM/Scl and Ro52 antibodies, mechanic's hands, arthritis.
  - MCTD: dysphagia, Raynaud's, SSc features.
  - SjD: Ro52, ANA, Raynaud's, older age, lymphopenia, dental caries.
  - Source: [Johnson 2024](https://doi.org/10.1002/acr.25347)
- On mild or subclinical findings (verbatim): "The Patient Panel expressed a preference for identifying ILD early, even if there was a risk for identifying either subclinical disease that might not progress or incidental findings requiring additional testing. If there is uncertainty regarding whom to screen, clinicians should proceed with screening." Also: "We acknowledge that, in some cases, screening may identify mild, asymptomatic ILD that is not progressive. The high morbidity of ILD once symptomatic, emergence of more effective treatments, and general preference of the Patient Panel for screening to identify ILD early contributed to our recommendations." — [Johnson 2024](https://doi.org/10.1002/acr.25347)
- Heterogeneity: "people with RA have low frequency (ie, 3%–5% of people with RA) of progressive ILD, and a larger proportion have asymptomatic or stable disease ... a subset of people with long-standing limited cutaneous SSc can have mild, stable ILD." — [Johnson 2024](https://doi.org/10.1002/acr.25347)
- Accuracy evidence uses a 20% extent reference: "Very low-certainty evidence suggests that the HRCT chest has a sensitivity of 95.7% and specificity of 63.8% for the detection of ILD (≥20% extent of lung involvement)." HRCT should include prone images "to differentiate mild dependent lung atelectasis from early fibrosis". — [Johnson 2024](https://doi.org/10.1002/acr.25347)
- Rescreening: "people with SARDs and associated greater risk of ILD be considered for rescreening with PFTs annually", with more frequent screening early for high-risk antibodies (Scl-70, MDA-5, anti-synthetase). — [Johnson 2024](https://doi.org/10.1002/acr.25347)
- Monitoring intervals:
  - IIM-ILD and SSc-ILD: PFTs every 3–6 months in year 1, then less often once stable (every 6–12 months).
  - RA-ILD, SjD-ILD and MCTD-ILD: PFTs every 3–12 months in year 1 (6–12 months suggested for RA-ILD).
  - Ambulatory desaturation: every 3–12 months.
  - No routine HRCT frequency is given; HRCT is advised "when clinically indicated".
  - Source: [Johnson 2024](https://doi.org/10.1002/acr.25347)
- Evidence base: 10 studies addressed screening and 13 addressed monitoring. "No publications were found to address 4 screening questions ... and 10 of the monitoring questions." — [Johnson 2024](https://doi.org/10.1002/acr.25347)
- Stated research gaps (verbatim): "First, the frequency of screening and monitoring requires further investigation to ascertain the most cost-effective strategy. Second, on HRCT chest, it is uncertain whether monitoring should differ by histologically based patterns (eg, nonspecific interstitial pneumonia, UIP). Similarly, an improved understanding of risk factors, including expanded autoantibody profiling ... is needed. Together, radiographic patterns, autoantibody profiles, and/or other features may allow for the development of specific screening and monitoring recommendations for subsets of patients with SARD-ILD." — [Johnson 2024](https://doi.org/10.1002/acr.25347)
- Full-text search found no occurrence of "ILA" in the ACR/CHEST guideline (string search of PMC12646460). — [Johnson 2024](https://doi.org/10.1002/acr.25347)

### Inferences
- In ACR/CHEST, anything detected by screening is "ILD", whether mild or subclinical. The guideline gives no rule for distinguishing minimal or indeterminate changes from ILD. That missing rule is the space a phenotyping study could address, and it ties directly to the stated gap that "radiographic patterns ... may allow ... specific screening and monitoring recommendations for subsets".

### Gaps
- The companion ACR/CHEST treatment guideline was not retrieved. It may contain rules on whether to treat mild, asymptomatic ILD (the screening guideline cites it as reference 37).

## Q3. ERS/EULAR 2025 CTD-ILD guideline (PMIDs 40907995 / 40912974)

### Takeaway
ERS/EULAR uses HRCT as the screening reference. It strongly recommends screening all SSc, all MCTD, and IIM with risk factors; RA and SjD with risk factors "could be screened"; it makes no recommendation for SLE. It gives no extent threshold for "ILD present", does not use "ILA" or "subclinical", and states it cannot recommend a screening frequency. Its research agenda explicitly includes impact of screening asymptomatic patients, incidence after negative screening, how to define progression, HRCT (including quantitative) scoring, AI trajectory prediction, and "MMF versus placebo in patients with very limited ILD".

### Cited Findings
- Methods and scope: 25 PICO and 28 narrative questions across SSc, RA, IIM, SjD, SLE and MCTD. There was insufficient evidence for 4 narrative questions (screening and progression-risk assessment in MCTD, SjD and SLE). — Antoniou KM et al., Eur Respir J 2026;67(1), PMID 40907995, [DOI 10.1183/13993003.02533-2024](https://doi.org/10.1183/13993003.02533-2024); same guideline in Ann Rheum Dis 2025;85:22-60, PMID 40912974, [DOI 10.1016/j.ard.2025.08.021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ard.2025.08.021)
- Screening recommendations:
  - "We recommend that all patients with SSc should be screened for ILD using HRCT (strong recommendation, low certainty)."
  - RA with risk factors (older age, smoking, RF, anti-CCP, raised inflammatory markers, male sex, high articular activity) "could be screened" (conditional, low certainty).
  - IIM with risk factors "should be screened" (strong). IIM without risk factors, except inclusion body myositis, "could be screened".
  - All MCTD "should be screened" (strong).
  - SjD with risk factors "could be screened".
  - SLE: "unable to provide a recommendation".
  - PFTs and lung ultrasound should not replace HRCT for screening.
  - Source: [Antoniou 2026 ERJ](https://doi.org/10.1183/13993003.02533-2024)
- SSc screening rationale: strong despite low certainty "based on the high risk of missing ILD if screening by HRCT is limited to patients with certain risk factors ... none of the risk factors are definitively linked to the presence or absence of ILD." — [Antoniou 2026](https://doi.org/10.1183/13993003.02533-2024)
- Screening frequency (verbatim): "Given the very low certainty of evidence for SSc, the significantly reduced incidence of ILD over the course of SSc, and the lack of evidence for other CTDs, we cannot recommend how frequently patients with CTD should be screened for ILD." Evidence: 1,454 SSc patients, 46% with ILD at baseline, and 39 incident cases over 12 months. — [Antoniou 2026](https://doi.org/10.1183/13993003.02533-2024)
- Monitoring: PFTs every 3–6 months during the first years (SSc: the first 3–5 years), then at least every 6–12 months. Repeat HRCT after 1–2 years in SSc-ILD, RA-ILD and other CTD-ILD; after 3–6 months in IIM at risk of rapid progression, then annually for 2 years. Repeat PFTs and HRCT if progression is suspected. — [Antoniou 2026](https://doi.org/10.1183/13993003.02533-2024)
- Baseline risk stratification:
  - SSc: global assessment including ILD extent on HRCT, FVC, DLCO, age, sex, mRSS and CRP/ESR. "More extensive ILD" was the most frequently cited predictor of progression and mortality.
  - RA: UIP pattern and extent on HRCT, plus FVC and DLCO.
  - IIM: HRCT, PFTs, and MDA-5, anti-synthetase and Ro52 antibodies.
  - Source: [Antoniou 2026](https://doi.org/10.1183/13993003.02533-2024)
- Treatment selection: "We suggest using the inclusion criteria of RCTs to guide treatment decisions for CTD-ILD (conditional, very low certainty)". Combined with the research item below, this implies that very limited ILD falls outside the evidence base. — [Antoniou 2026](https://doi.org/10.1183/13993003.02533-2024)
- Research agenda (Table 1d), verbatim items:
  - "Assess impact of screening of asymptomatic patients using clinical characteristics and/or biomarkers"
  - "Assess impact of early screening and diagnosis of ILD on outcomes"
  - "Conduct multicentre, prospective investigations to determine the incidence of ILD in each CTD after initial negative screening"
  - "further assess how to define CTD-ILD progression"
  - "Develop a risk stratification tool to assess risk of CTD-ILD progression and to tailor differential upfront treatment strategies"
  - "Further evaluate HRCT scores (including quantitative scores) to assess severity and predict risk of ILD progression" (IIM)
  - "Assess the role automated quantification of changes on HRCT"
  - "Assess the role of artificial intelligence for disease trajectory prediction"
  - "Explore genetic testing (i.e. MUC5B promoter risk variant) as a screening tool in RA patients"
  - "MMF versus placebo in patients with very limited ILD in RCTs"
  - "Identify and develop markers/tools for early detection and prediction of progression to enable timely treatment in patients at risk"
  - Source: [Antoniou 2026](https://doi.org/10.1183/13993003.02533-2024)
- Full-text string search: "ILA" 0 hits; "interstitial lung abnormalit" appears only in reference titles (Doyle 2014; Kawano-Dourado 2020); "subclinical" appears only in reference titles; "any extent", "early ILD" and "mild ILD" 0 hits. The SLE section notes that the source studies "did not define ILD precisely". — [Antoniou 2026](https://doi.org/10.1183/13993003.02533-2024)

### Inferences
- ERS/EULAR treats any HRCT-detected ILD in CTD as ILD and does not grade "how little is ILD". Its own agenda (very limited ILD RCT, progression definition, quantitative HRCT, AI trajectory, risk tools) directly supports a study on progression risk in limited or early CTD-ILD by imaging phenotype. It does not support framing such a study as "ILA".

### Gaps
- The supplementary EtD tables were not reviewed. They might contain an operational ILD-presence definition used in the evidence review.

## Q4. SSc-, RA-, IIM-specific statements and Chinese/Japanese consensus

### Takeaway
SSc guidance (Hoffmann-Vold 2020 Delphi; US Delphi 2023) says screen all SSc with HRCT and allows "no pharmacological treatment" for some (typically limited) disease. Goh 2008 remains the standard limited/extensive staging: about 20% HRCT extent, with FVC 70% used when extent is indeterminate (10–30%). New 2026 RA-ILD consensus documents (Lancet Respir Med expert group, ALAT) and the 2025 Japanese JRS/JCR guide add screening strategies. An IIM-ILD definition effort (MCTC 2026) explicitly states that no standardised definition of IIM-ILD exists. The Chinese 2018 CTD-ILD consensus covers "early screening" but could only be read at abstract level.

### Cited Findings
- Hoffmann-Vold 2020 European SSc-ILD Delphi (27 experts; 52 primary and 6 supplemental statements). Key statements: "all patients with systemic sclerosis should be screened for systemic sclerosis-associated ILD using high-resolution CT; high-resolution CT is the primary tool for diagnosing ILD in systemic sclerosis; pulmonary function tests support screening and diagnosis; ... severity should be measured with more than one indicator; it is appropriate to treat all severe cases; no pharmacological treatment is an option for some patients; follow-up assessments enable identification of disease progression." Funded by Boehringer Ingelheim. — Lancet Rheumatol 2020;2:e71-e83, PMID 38263663, [DOI 10.1016/S2665-9913(19)30144-4](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2665-9913(19)30144-4)
- Goh 2008 staging (n=215): "Increasingly extensive disease on HRCT was a powerful predictor of mortality (P<0.0005), with an optimal extent threshold of 20%. In patients with HRCT extent of 10-30% (termed indeterminate disease), an FVC threshold of 70% was an adequate prognostic substitute." Limited vs extensive staging had HR 3.46. With "minimal and severe disease on HRCT defined as clearly <20% or clearly >20%", prognostic separation was higher for practitioners (HR 3.39–3.82) than trainees (HR 1.87–2.60). — Am J Respir Crit Care Med 2008;177:1248-54, PMID 18369202, [DOI 10.1164/rccm.200706-877OC](https://doi.org/10.1164/rccm.200706-877OC)
- US SSc-ILD Delphi 2023 (25 experts): all SSc patients to be screened by auscultation, spirometry with DLCO, HRCT and/or autoantibodies. Treatment decisions are influenced by PFTs, "extent of ILD on HRCT", and duration and degree of dyspnoea. — Rahaghi FF et al., Respir Res 2023;24:6, PMID 36624431, [DOI 10.1186/s12931-022-02292-3](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12931-022-02292-3)
- SSc natural history after screening:
  - Flemish cohorts (n=722, Goh-rated ILD): prevalence 34%, incidence 20.3/1000 person-years. 60% of baseline ILD showed functional progression at 5 years. "Early" SSc: prevalence 6%. — Vandecasteele E et al., Semin Arthritis Rheum 2021, PMID 34403812, [DOI 10.1016/j.semarthrit.2021.07.018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.semarthrit.2021.07.018)
  - Canadian cohort: 21% of 969 developed incident ILD over a median of 2.4 years. Incidence in late SSc (>7 years) was 3.7/100 person-years, lower than in earlier SSc (RR 0.68). Progression rates were similar. — Hoa S et al., Arthritis Rheumatol 2025, PMID 39492683, [DOI 10.1002/art.43051](https://doi.org/10.1002/art.43051)
- Early SSc CT sign: "faint amorphous nodular opacity" (FANO) was seen in 64% of 28 biopsied SSc-ILD cases. It corresponded to perivenular fibrosis and peribronchiolar metaplasia and often progressed to reticulation. It is proposed as an early-stage CT finding (small, biopsy-selected series). — Fukuda T et al., Jpn J Radiol 2026, PMID 41407994, [DOI 10.1007/s11604-025-01922-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11604-025-01922-2)
- RA-ILD expert consensus 2026: a multidisciplinary group gave consensus statements on risk factors, screening, and treatment initiation. The abstract states there is "no agreement on how to screen for rheumatoid arthritis-associated ILD, when to initiate treatment, and the optimal treatment regimen". Specific statements were not accessible (abstract only). — Solomon JJ et al., Lancet Respir Med 2026;14:820-836, PMID 42248165, [DOI 10.1016/S2213-2600(26)00094-9](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2213-2600(26)00094-9)
- ALAT 2025 RA-ILD guideline: emphasises early recognition through screening. A comparative review says ALAT "proposed a broader approach that included lung ultrasound as an adjunct, without replacing HRCT", while ERS/EULAR and ACR/CHEST "favored risk-based assessment"; guidelines "differ in screening, monitoring intervals, and treatment priorities." — Alberti ML et al., Arch Bronconeumol 2026, PMID 42034552, [DOI 10.1016/j.arbres.2026.04.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.arbres.2026.04.001); Garcia-Salinas R et al., Rheumatol Int 2026, PMID 42888356, [DOI 10.1007/s00296-026-06322-x](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00296-026-06322-x)
- Japanese JRS/JCR 2025 CTD-ILD guide (2nd edition): adds "a conceptual framework for understanding ILD in CTD, practical clinical flowcharts, screening strategies, and risk factors" and a provisional RA-ILD algorithm. The details of how it handles minimal changes were not accessible. — Kondoh Y et al., Respir Investig 2026, PMID 42085904, [DOI 10.1016/j.resinv.2026.101412](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resinv.2026.101412)
- IIM: ERS/EULAR strongly recommends HRCT screening for IIM with risk factors (see Q3). The Myositis Clinical Trials Consortium states (verbatim) "A major barrier to progress in this field is the lack of standardized disease nomenclature and uniform diagnostic or classification criteria". Its systematic review will map how IIM-ILD is "defined and operationalized across studies", ahead of a Delphi process. — Triantafyllias K et al., Syst Rev 2026, PMID 42363307, [DOI 10.1186/s13643-026-03230-w](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13643-026-03230-w)
- Chinese consensus: the 2018 Chinese expert consensus on CTD-ILD (41 experts in rheumatology, pulmonology and radiology) "covered the early screening, diagnosing, disease monitoring and assessing, immunosuppressive and anti-fibrotic therapy with a treat-to-target approach." — Zhonghua Nei Ke Za Zhi 2018;57:558-565, PMID 30060326, [DOI 10.3760/cma.j.issn.0578-1426.2018.08.005](https://doi.org/10.3760/cma.j.issn.0578-1426.2018.08.005)
- A 2022 Taiwan consensus covers RA comorbidities including ILD (not read in detail). — Yu KH et al., Medicine (Baltimore) 2022, PMID 35029907, [DOI 10.1097/MD.0000000000028501](https://doi.org/10.1097/MD.0000000000028501)

### Inferences
- Goh 2008 "limited" disease (<20%) is far broader than ILA (≥5% of a zone, no ILD criteria). A study of "minimal or early" CTD changes therefore needs its own explicit extent strata, for example <5% of a zone, ILA-range, and <10% / 10–20% of total lung. Goh-limited is too coarse to stand in for "early".
- Several bodies (MCTC for IIM; ERS/EULAR for SLE, where "studies did not define ILD precisely") acknowledge non-standard ILD definitions. A definitional or phenotyping contribution is therefore recognised as needed.

### Gaps
- A specific "Chinese CTD-ILD expert consensus 2022" and a "Chinese RA-ILD consensus" were **not found in PubMed** with the searches run (Zhonghua Nei Ke Za Zhi and other queries). The only Chinese CTD-ILD consensus located was the 2018 one, read at abstract level, so its handling of minimal changes is unverified. Chinese journals outside PubMed (CNKI or Wanfang) were not searched.
- The full texts of the Solomon 2026 RA-ILD consensus, the ALAT 2025 guideline and the Japanese 2025 guide were not accessible, so their treatment of "subclinical" or "mild" ILD is unverified.
- A formal EUSTAR/World Scleroderma Foundation document defining "subclinical ILD" was not located.

## Q5. How is "ILD present" thresholded in CTD research, and how reliable are minimal changes?

### Takeaway
No CTD guideline sets a minimum extent for "ILD present". ACR/CHEST and ERS/EULAR both treat ILD found on HRCT as present, without a threshold. The thresholds that do exist are:
- Fleischner/ATS ILA: ≥5% of a lung zone.
- ATS ILD criterion: fibrosis ≥5% of total lung volume.
- Goh: 20% total extent, with 10–30% treated as indeterminate.
- ACR/CHEST diagnostic-accuracy evidence: ILD defined as ≥20% extent.

Specific interobserver data for minimal changes in CTD were not found. Fleischner lists "reproducibility of radiological criteria" as a key uncertainty, and Goh showed lower prognostic discrimination when trainees did the reading.

### Cited Findings
- Fleischner: the 5% threshold is "arbitrary and imprecise", and "Reproducibility of radiological criteria" is a key uncertainty. — [Hatabu 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2213-2600(20)30168-5)
- ATS 2025: the 5% threshold "needs additional supporting data". Quantitative CT "potentially provide[s] a more objective and reproducible metric of disease extent compared with visual assessment", but how QCT metrics compare and how much they depend on scanner and reconstruction "are unknown". — [Podolanczuk 2025](https://doi.org/10.1164/rccm.202505-1054ST)
- ATS 2025 meta-analysis of 52 studies of CTD patients not suspected of ILD: pooled prevalence of "ILAs/ILD" was 40% (95% CI 37–43%). By disease: RA 23%, SSc 45%, SjD 39%, DM/PM 44%. The authors did not separate ILA from ILD in this pooled estimate. — [Podolanczuk 2025](https://doi.org/10.1164/rccm.202505-1054ST)
- ATS 2025 CTD screening: "We suggest a baseline chest HRCT scan to screen for ILAs/ILD in adults with CTDs that are associated with an increased risk of ILD" (36/37 votes; RA, SSc, PM/DM, antisynthetase syndrome, MCTD, SjD, overlap). There was no consensus on repeat screening frequency, and SLE without overlap was not recommended. ILA monitoring by CT every 2–3 years. — [Podolanczuk 2025](https://doi.org/10.1164/rccm.202505-1054ST)
- ACR/CHEST accuracy evidence used ILD defined as "≥20% extent of lung involvement". — [Johnson 2024](https://doi.org/10.1002/acr.25347)
- Goh 2008: 20% threshold; 10–30% indeterminate; trainee vs practitioner HR 1.87–2.60 vs 3.39–3.82. — [Goh 2008](https://doi.org/10.1164/rccm.200706-877OC)
- Semi-quantitative SSc fibrosis extent scoring (Oslo/Zurich, n=155): inter-rater κ=0.891 and intra-rater κ=0.996. The authors note "the presence of distinct imaging abnormalities by HRCT defines ILD" but that validated quantification methods are lacking. Those κ values apply to extent scoring in established SSc-ILD, not to detecting minimal changes. — Tschalèr L et al., RMD Open 2025, PMID 40021203, [DOI 10.1136/rmdopen-2024-004938](https://doi.org/10.1136/rmdopen-2024-004938)
- RA ILA studies use the Hunninghake/Washko sequential reading method (ILA present, indeterminate, or absent). This method originated in COPDGene smokers, where ILA was present on 8% of HRCTs. — Washko GR et al., NEJM 2011, PMID 21388308, [DOI 10.1056/NEJMoa1007285](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1007285); applied in RA by [Doyle 2014](https://doi.org/10.1378/chest.13-1394) and [McDermott 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/kead277)

### Inferences
- Because "ILD present" has no agreed lower threshold in CTD, and ILA and ILD were pooled in the ATS meta-analysis, any study of "minimal" changes in CTD must:
  - pre-specify extent cut-offs;
  - report inter-reader agreement for presence and for subtype;
  - consider quantitative CT as an anchor.
- An "indeterminate" category, as in the sequential reading method, is probably needed.

### Gaps
- No kappa values were retrieved for interobserver agreement on ILA presence or subtype (Fleischner subcategories) in CTD cohorts. Kappa values from the original ILA reading method papers (e.g. Washko 2011 full text) were not extracted, because the abstract does not report them.
- No CTD study was retrieved that directly compares outcomes under "any extent" vs ≥5% vs ≥10% thresholds for defining ILD presence.
