# Interstitial lung abnormalities (ILA) in the general / non-CTD population: Fleischner 2020 classification, outcomes by subtype, and post-2020 updates

Sources were retrieved from PubMed: abstracts through NCBI E-utilities, and full text from PMC where available. Each entry gives the PMID and DOI. Full text was read for: Fleischner 2020 (PMC7970441), the ATS 2025 Clinical Statement (PMC12264694), the 2022 PPF guideline (PMC9851481, ILA passages only) and the MESA cause-of-death letter (PMC13624791). All other entries are based on abstracts.

## Q1. Fleischner 2020 definition of ILA, its three subcategories, and its position on CTD and other high-risk groups

### Takeaway
Fleischner 2020 defines ILA by imaging alone. ILA are incidentally detected, non-dependent CT abnormalities (ground-glass or reticular abnormality, traction bronchiectasis, honeycombing, non-emphysematous cysts) that affect more than 5% of any lung zone in a person with no prior clinical suspicion of ILD. The paper recognises three subcategories: non-subpleural, subpleural non-fibrotic and subpleural fibrotic. It explicitly excludes abnormalities found by screening high-risk groups, including RA, SSc and familial ILD; it calls these "preclinical ILD" and places them outside its scope. The 2025 ATS Clinical Statement removed that exclusion. This difference between the two documents is the main framing point for a CTD grant.

### Cited Findings
**Fleischner Society Position Paper.** Hatabu H, et al. *Lancet Respir Med* 2020;8:726–737. PMID 32649920; DOI 10.1016/S2213-2600(20)30168-5; PMCID PMC7970441. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2213-2600(20)30168-5)

- **Definition.** ILA are "CT scan findings that are potentially compatible with ILD in patients who have partial (eg, abdominal CT including the lower lung zones) or complete chest CT examinations without previous clinical suspicion of ILD." "The definition of ILAs is purely radiological and is based on the incidental identification of CT abnormality. Differentiation between ILAs and clinical and subclinical ILD must be on the basis of clinical evaluation." [Source](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2213-2600(20)30168-5)
- **Extent threshold and zones.** ILA are "non-dependent abnormalities affecting more than 5% of any lung zone." The upper, middle and lower zones are divided at the inferior aortic arch and the right inferior pulmonary vein. The paper concedes that "the 5% threshold is arbitrary and imprecise" and says it was kept "to exclude minimal opacities and to conform to previous published literature." [Source](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2213-2600(20)30168-5)
- **Included and excluded findings.**
  - Included: ground-glass or reticular abnormalities, traction bronchiectasis, honeycombing, non-emphysematous cysts.
  - Removed: centrilobular nodularity, because it reflects smoking-related respiratory bronchiolitis, is common, is typically non-progressive and is not associated with fibrosis.
  - Classified as equivocal: focal or unilateral patchy GGO, and dependent abnormalities unless they persist on prone imaging.
  - Not ILA: pleuroparenchymal fibroelastosis.
  [Source](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2213-2600(20)30168-5)
- **Three subcategories.**
  1. Non-subpleural: GGO or reticulation without a predominant subpleural distribution.
  2. Subpleural non-fibrotic: GGO or reticulation with a predominant subpleural distribution and no evidence of fibrosis.
  3. Subpleural fibrotic: traction bronchiectasis, architectural distortion or honeycombing.

  Fibrotic ILA can then be classified with the 2018 Fleischner/ATS-ERS-JRS-ALAT UIP categories (typical, probable or indeterminate for UIP). [Source](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2213-2600(20)30168-5)
- **Prognosis by subcategory as stated in the paper.**
  - Non-subpleural ILA are "usually non-progressive and not associated with increased mortality."
  - Fibrotic ILA are "associated with a higher rate of progression and death on 5-year follow-up."
  - About 2% of the AGES-Reykjavik cohort had a probable or definite UIP pattern; these participants were more likely to show subpleural ILA progression and had worse survival.
  - "Fibrotic ILAs might be an important precursor to IPF or other progressive fibrotic ILDs."
  [Source](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2213-2600(20)30168-5)
- **CTD and other high-risk groups.** "Abnormalities identified during screening for ILD in high-risk groups (eg, those with rheumatoid arthritis, systemic sclerosis, or familial ILD) are not considered as ILAs because they are not incidental; these might be referred to as preclinical ILD and their management is beyond the scope of this Position Paper." The future-research section adds: "We have excluded preclinical interstitial abnormalities identified during the screening of individuals at high risk (eg, those with rheumatoid arthritis, scleroderma, occupational exposure, and familial interstitial lung disease) from the scope of this Position Paper to simplify the approaches. However, future studies and discussions are needed to investigate the role of ILAs in preclinical interstitial abnormalities identified during screening of individuals at high risk." [Source](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2213-2600(20)30168-5)
- **Work-up of a person with ILA.** Evaluation should look for explanations including "systemic disease (eg, occult connective tissue disorders)". Referral for pulmonary evaluation is advised if any of the following are present: respiratory symptoms or signs, physiological or gas-transfer abnormality, or extensive CT abnormality (3 or more of 6 lung zones). [Source](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2213-2600(20)30168-5)
- **Prevalence.** In people over 60, ILA are seen in 4–9% of smokers and 2–7% of non-smokers. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2213-2600(20)30168-5)
- **Follow-up.**
  - Risk factors for progression: smoking, other inhaled exposures, medications, subthreshold physiological abnormality, fibrosis on CT, and a subpleural basal-predominant distribution.
  - Suggested schedule for higher-risk individuals: first clinical follow-up at 3–12 months; follow-up CT at 12–24 months.
  - Non-subpleural ILA "seldom progress" and can be followed expectantly.
  - The optimal intervals are acknowledged to be unknown. "Optimal management of progressive ILAs is unknown," which makes them a candidate population for treatment trials.
  [Source](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2213-2600(20)30168-5)
- **Pathology (cited within Fleischner).**
  - Miller 2018 (n = 424 nodule resections): 19 of 26 patients with ILA (73%) had histological fibrosis, and 2 (8%) had UIP. 52% of patients without ILA also had fibrosis.
  - Hung: 51% of specimens without radiological ILA showed fibrotic changes.

  The paper concludes that most ILA in smokers probably represent smoking-related interstitial fibrosis rather than UIP. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2213-2600(20)30168-5)

### Inferences
- In the strict Fleischner 2020 framework, imaging abnormalities found by deliberately screening CTD patients are by definition not ILA. A grant that applies the Fleischner subcategories to CTD cohorts is therefore borrowing a classification that was designed for, and validated in, a different pretest-probability setting. Fleischner itself names this as a research need ("future studies ... are needed to investigate the role of ILAs in preclinical interstitial abnormalities identified during screening of individuals at high risk").

### Gaps
- Fleischner 2020 gives no numerical progression or mortality estimates for each of its three subcategories, and no validation data for the subcategory scheme itself.

## Q2. Prevalence, progression rates and mortality by subtype in the major cohorts

### Takeaway
- **Prevalence:** about 7–10% in Western cohorts and roughly 2–4% in Asian health-screening cohorts.
- **Progression:** pooled imaging progression is about 34–47% overall, rising to about 64–80% with 5 or more years of follow-up.
- **Strongest predictors of progression:** fibrotic features, namely the definite-fibrosis/UIP pattern (OR 8.4), traction bronchiectasis, subpleural reticulation, lower-lobe predominance and honeycombing (all cases progressed).
- **Mortality:** adjusted all-cause mortality HRs are 1.3–2.7 for ILA overall. In MESA, excess ILD/respiratory mortality was largely confined to fibrotic ILA (adjusted SHR 4.55), whereas non-fibrotic ILA showed no significant independent association.

### Cited Findings

|---|---|---|---|---|
| FHS | 2,633 | 177 (7%) | 7% vs 1% | 2.7 (1.1–6.5) |
| AGES-Reykjavik | 5,320 | 378 (7%) | 56% vs 33% | 1.3 (1.2–1.4) |
| COPDGene | 2,068 | 156 (8%) | — | 1.8 (1.1–2.8) |
| ECLIPSE | 1,670 | 157 (9%) | 11% vs 5% | 1.4 (1.1–2.0) |

In AGES-Reykjavik, the excess mortality was explained by respiratory deaths, specifically pulmonary fibrosis. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2016.0518)

**AGES-Reykjavik, imaging patterns.** Putman RK, et al. *AJRCCM* 2019;200:175–183. PMID 30673508; DOI 10.1164/rccm.201809-1652OC. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1164/rccm.201809-1652OC)
- Design: ILA assessed in 5,320 participants; progression assessed in 3,167 with CT about 5 years apart.
- Over 5 years, 327 participants (10%) had ILA on at least one CT. Of these, 238 (73%) progressed and 89 (27%) were stable or improved.
- Older age and number of MUC5B copies were associated with progression.
- The definite-fibrosis pattern carried the highest risk of progression: OR 8.4 (2.7–25).
- Mortality versus the UIP-indeterminate pattern: probable UIP HR 1.7 (1.2–2.4); UIP HR 3.9 (2.3–6.8).

The Fleischner paper summarises this study as follows. Subpleural reticular change, lower-lobe predominance or traction bronchiectasis gave more than 6-fold odds of progression; all cases of honeycombing progressed over 5 years; and centrilobular nodules predicted non-progression. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2213-2600(20)30168-5)

**FHS, development and progression.** Araki T, et al. *AJRCCM* 2016;194:1514–1522. PMID 27314401; DOI 10.1164/rccm.201512-2523OC. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1164/rccm.201512-2523OC)
- Design: 1,867 participants with serial CT about 6 years apart.
- Outcomes: 118 (6%) had ILA with progression and 37 (2%) had stable or improving ILA.
- Age and MUC5B were associated with progression.
- FVC decline with progression was greater than in participants without ILA (20 mL, p = 0.0005) and greater than in non-progressive ILA (25 mL, p = 0.03).
- Progression and death: HR 3.9 (1.3–10.9) over a median of about 4 years.

Fleischner adds context: excess FVC decline was about 30 mL/yr (about 60 mL/yr in total), compared with about 200 mL/yr in IPF. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2213-2600(20)30168-5)

**AGES-Reykjavik, traction bronchiectasis.** Hino T, et al. *Eur J Radiol Open* 2021;8:100334. PMID 33748349; DOI 10.1016/j.ejro.2021.100334. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejro.2021.100334)
- Design: 327 participants with ILA and CT about 5 years apart.
- All 5 participants with severe traction bronchiectasis or honeycombing at baseline progressed, as did 46 of 51 (90%) with mild-to-moderate traction bronchiectasis.
- Progression of traction bronchiectasis was associated with mortality: adjusted HR 1.68 (p < 0.001).

**COPDGene, visual and quantitative progression.** Putman RK, et al. *AJRCCM* 2025;211:1794–1801. PMID 40720797; DOI 10.1164/rccm.202501-0247OC. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1164/rccm.202501-0247OC)
- Design: 4,373 smokers with CT about 5 years apart; 544 (12%) had ILA on at least one CT.
- Of those with ILA, 391 (72%) progressed visually.
- Traction bronchiectasis predicted progression: OR 3.1 (1.3–7.3).
- Baseline quantitative interstitial abnormality (QIA) score and FVC did not predict progression.
- An increase in QIA was associated with mortality: HR 1.05 per unit (1.01–1.09).

**MESA, incidence.** McGroder CF, et al. *Eur Respir J* 2023;61:2201950. PMID 37202153; DOI 10.1183/13993003.01950-2022. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1183/13993003.01950-2022)
- Design: 13,944 cardiac CTs in 6,197 participants, 2000–2012.
- Incidence: 13.1 per 1,000 person-years for ILA and 3.5 per 1,000 person-years for fibrotic ILA.
- Risk factors:

| Factor | Incident ILA, HR (95% CI) | Incident fibrotic ILA, HR (95% CI) |
|---|---|---|

| IPF polygenic risk score | not reported as associated | 2.09 |

- Reader agreement for ILA: intra-reader 92.0% (AC1 0.912); inter-reader 83.5% (AC1 0.814).

**MESA, cause of death by subtype.** Kassapidis V, et al. *AJRCCM* 2026;212:2506–2509 (research letter). PMID 42446123; DOI 10.1093/ajrccm/aamag347. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1093/ajrccm/aamag347)
- Design: 6,185 participants with 276 ILA cases (76 fibrotic, 200 non-fibrotic); median follow-up 16 years. ILA were defined by the ATS 2025 definition, with fibrotic ILA meaning honeycombing, traction bronchiectasis or architectural distortion in at least 5% of a lung zone.

| Group | All-cause death | ILD death | Respiratory death | Cardiovascular death |

**NLST single site.** Jin GY, et al. *Radiology* 2013;268:563–571. PMID 23513242; DOI 10.1148/radiol.13120816. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1148/radiol.13120816)
- n = 884 smokers. ILA prevalence 9.7%, with another 11.5% equivocal. Pattern: fibrotic 2.1%, non-fibrotic 5.9%, mixed 1.7%.

**Queensland lung cancer screening.** Mackintosh JA, et al. *Intern Med J* 2019;49:843. PMID 30350396; DOI 10.1111/imj.14148. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1111/imj.14148)
- n = 256. ILA prevalence 7.8%. Only 1 of 20 progressed over 2 years.
- This is an example of low short-term progression.

**US clinical lung cancer screening.** Patel AS, et al. *Respir Res* 2023;24:49. PMID 36782326; DOI 10.1186/s12931-023-02359-9. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12931-023-02359-9)
- n = 1,699. ILA prevalence 2.4%, plus 5.9% indeterminate.
- 24.4% of those with ILA were later diagnosed with ILD, at a mean of 4.5 years.
- About 50% progressed. Mortality HR 3.87 (2.07–7.21).

**UK lung cancer screening.** Selvarajah B, et al. *BMJ Open Respir Res* 2025;12:e003298. PMID 40935564; DOI 10.1136/bmjresp-2025-003298. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjresp-2025-003298)
- Population: 11,635 people screened; 417 had ILA.
- Interobserver kappa: 0.64 for the Fleischner classification versus 0.76 for a new scheme based on traction bronchiolectasis.
- Mortality was about 3-fold higher for both fibrotic and non-fibrotic ILA, and 6-fold higher for undiagnosed ILD.

**Korea, health-screening cohort (3 centres).** Lee JE, et al. *Radiology* 2023;306:e221172. PMID 36219115; DOI 10.1148/radiol.221172. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1148/radiol.221172)
- n = 2,765 aged 50 or over. ILA prevalence 3% (35 non-fibrotic, 59 fibrotic), plus 4% equivocal.
- 80% (48 of 60) progressed over a median of 8 years of CT follow-up.
- Fibrotic ILA versus no ILA: progression HR 10.3 (6.4–16.4); lung cancer HR 4.4 (2.1–9.1); disease-specific mortality HR 6.7 (3.7–12.2); all-cause mortality HR 2.5 (1.6–3.8).
- Non-fibrotic ILA also had higher mortality than no ILA (p = 0.01).

**Korea, lung cancer screening-eligible individuals with preserved FVC.** Ahn Y, et al. *Eur Radiol* 2026;36:111. PMID 40715650; DOI 10.1007/s00330-025-11874-w. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00330-025-11874-w)

- CT progression predicted mortality (HR 8.74). Fibrotic subtype did not (p = 0.254), and CT progression did not predict FVC falling below 80%.

**Korea, younger health-screening examinees.** Choe J, et al. *J Korean Med Sci* 2025;40:e237. PMID 40985850; DOI 10.3346/jkms.2025.40.e237. [Source](https://doi.org/10.3346/jkms.2025.40.e237)
- n = 2,589, mean age 49. Baseline prevalence 0.2% ILA and 0.3% equivocal.
- 83.3% of baseline ILA progressed over a median of 7 years.
- Risk factors for progressive ILA: age, smoking, white cell count, ESR and rheumatoid factor.

**Korea, automated quantification.** Oh JH, et al. *Respir Res* 2024;25:78. PMID 38321467; DOI 10.1186/s12931-024-02715-3. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12931-024-02715-3)
- n = 2,890 healthy screenees, mean age 49. ILA defined by a quantitative score of 3 or more: prevalence 8.6%.
- Progression 23.5% over a median of 6.5 years.
- Predictors: age, BMI, ESR.

**Large health check-up population (subpleural non-fibrotic ILA).** Zhang Y, et al. *AJRCCM* 2022;206:178–185. PMID 35426779; DOI 10.1164/rccm.202110-2412OC. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1164/rccm.202110-2412OC)
- n = 155,539 on low-dose CT. ILA prevalence 2.1%, of which 81.7% were subpleural non-fibrotic. Prevalence rose linearly with age.
- 43.6% (198 of 454) of subpleural non-fibrotic ILA progressed over 4 years. Reticulation predicted progression: OR 1.9 (1.2–3.0).
- Subpleural non-fibrotic ILA with extensive reticulation progressed at a rate similar to subpleural fibrotic ILA (73.0% vs 68.8%).

**Japan, health check-up (St Luke's, Tokyo), equivocal ILA.** Imai R, et al. *Ann Am Thorac Soc* 2025;22:387. PMID 39531620; DOI 10.1513/AnnalsATS.202408-867OC. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1513/AnnalsATS.202408-867OC)
- n = 20,896. ILA prevalence 2.0%; equivocal ILA 0.4%.

**Sweden, SCAPIS.** Pesonen I, et al. *ERJ Open Res* 2023;9:00035-2023. PMID 37753274; DOI 10.1183/23120541.00035-2023. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1183/23120541.00035-2023)

**Meta-analysis, Grant-Orser A, et al.** *AJRCCM* 2023;208:695–708. PMID 37534937; DOI 10.1164/rccm.202302-0271OC. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1164/rccm.202302-0271OC)
- 22 studies, n = 88,325.
- Pooled prevalence: 7% in lung cancer screening, 7% in the general population, 26% in familial cohorts.

- 19 studies for prevalence (n = 241,541): pooled 9.7%; general population 6.8%.
- 10 studies for progression (n = 1,317): pooled 47.1%; 64.2% with follow-up of 4.5 years or more versus 31.0% with shorter follow-up.
- Fibrotic ILA and progression: combined OR 5.55 (1.95–15.82).

**Meta-analysis, Seok J, et al.** *Sci Rep* 2024;14:7330. PMID 38538680; DOI 10.1038/s41598-024-57831-3. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-57831-3)
- Overall mortality RR 2.62 (1.94–3.54; I² = 90%).
- Lung cancer RR 3.85.
- ICI-related ILD RR 3.05.

**Individual patient-level meta-analysis, Chae KJ, et al.** *Ann Am Thorac Soc* 2026;23:1179–1189. PMID 41871447; DOI 10.1093/annalsats/aaoag071. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1093/annalsats/aaoag071)
- 14 studies, n = 31,739. Pooled prevalence 5.6%, rising from 2.5% under age 55 to 14.6% at age 80 or over.
- Pooled progression 34%, higher in fibrotic ILA. Age did not affect progression once ILA was present.
- Progression-free survival: 76% at 3 years and 55% at 5 years (n = 202).

**ATS 2025 evidence synthesis.** Podolanczuk AJ, et al. PMID 40387336; DOI 10.1164/rccm.202505-1054ST. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1164/rccm.202505-1054ST)
- 15 serial-CT studies: pooled radiologic progression 46% (38–55%).
- Progression and mortality: HR 1.9 (1.3–2.8) and HR 1.69 (1.21–2.34) in two studies; a third study found no association.
- ILA/ILD prevalence among smokers: 8% (24 studies).
- "Median times to progression to UIP as long as 11 years."

**2022 ATS/ERS/JRS/ALAT IPF/PPF guideline.** Raghu G, et al. *AJRCCM* 2022;205:e18–e47. PMID 35486072; DOI 10.1164/rccm.202202-0399ST; PMCID PMC9851481. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1164/rccm.202202-0399ST)
- "These incidentally identified interstitial lung abnormalities (ILA) are an independent risk factor for mortality."
- "At least 40% of subjects with ILA show progression of CT changes when followed over 4–6 years."
- The former term "early UIP pattern" was eliminated "to avoid confusion with interstitial lung abnormalities."
- Machine and deep learning tools are noted as potentially useful for characterising incidentally detected ILA.

**Radiologic–pathologic correlation (Korea, surgical lung biopsy).** Chae KJ, et al. *Eur Radiol* 2022;32:2713–2723. PMID 34984519; DOI 10.1007/s00330-021-08378-8. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00330-021-08378-8)
- n = 45 incidental ILA with normal pulmonary function, all biopsied.
- 69% (25 of 36) of subpleural fibrotic ILA showed definite or probable UIP, compared with 11% of subpleural non-fibrotic ILA.
- Fibrotic ILA: progression HR 2.42; death HR 9.22. Median progression-free time 40 months.

**Radiologic–pathologic correlation (Japan, surgical lung biopsy).** Fukuda T, et al. *Respir Investig* 2026;64:101374. PMID 41579529; DOI 10.1016/j.resinv.2026.101374. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resinv.2026.101374)
- n = 30, of which 90% were fibrotic ILA.
- Dominant patterns: UIP 43.3%, NSIP 20%, bronchiolocentric interstitial pneumonia 20%.
- 96.7% would be reclassified as subclinical ILD under the 2025 ATS criteria.

### Inferences
- **Subtype gradient.** The evidence is consistent for fibrotic versus non-fibrotic features. Fibrosis or traction bronchiectasis carries about 3- to 10-fold odds or hazard of progression, and fibrotic ILA in MESA carries about 4.5-fold adjusted respiratory mortality. Evidence that cleanly separates all three Fleischner subcategories is thinner. Asian data (Zhang 2022) show that subpleural non-fibrotic ILA is not benign: 44% progressed over 4 years, and the reticulation-rich form behaves like fibrotic ILA.
- **Follow-up length.** Progression estimates depend heavily on how long people are followed: about 11–37% at 2 years (NLST), 31% under 4.5 years, and 64–80% at 5–8 years or more.
- **Mortality.** All-cause mortality HRs in general cohorts are modest (1.3–2.7). Much of the excess death is non-respiratory. Fleischner notes that respiratory deaths made up less than 15% of deaths in ILA in AGES-Reykjavik, which points to accelerated ageing or comorbidity. A CTD cohort, with its competing mortality from the systemic disease, may show a different mix of causes of death.

### Gaps
- No retrieved Western general-population study reports progression and mortality separately for all three Fleischner subcategories with the 2020 criteria applied as written. The AGES and FHS analyses mostly used older pattern definitions. Hata 2021 (*Radiology*, PMID 34374589; DOI 10.1148/radiol.2021204367) states that subcategories are "informative for predicting progression and mortality", but its full text was not retrievable, so its subcategory tables could not be checked.
- UK Biobank: no ILA-specific UK Biobank publication was found in PubMed title searches.
- Chinese general-population cohorts: Zhang 2022 is a very large check-up cohort, but the country was not confirmed in the retrieved abstract and should be verified before citing it as "Chinese".
- ECLIPSE and COPDGene have no subtype-specific mortality beyond what is reported above.

## Q3. Genetic and biological correlates: general-population ILA is related to age, smoking and IPF genetics

### Takeaway
In general cohorts, ILA are driven by age (the strongest determinant), smoking, other inhalational exposures (occupational exposures, traffic-related elemental carbon) and IPF-type genetic risk. The MUC5B rs35705950 promoter variant raises ILA odds about 2.8-fold per allele and fibrotic-ILA odds 5- to 6-fold. ILA also share the DPP9, DSP, FAM13A and IVD loci with IPF and are associated with shorter telomeres. This biological profile matches IPF, not autoimmunity, which is the key contrast with CTD.

### Cited Findings
- **MUC5B in FHS.** Hunninghake GM, et al. *NEJM* 2013;368:2192–2200. PMID 23692170; DOI 10.1056/NEJMoa1216076. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1216076)
  - n = 2,633; ILA in 7%.
  - Per minor allele of rs35705950: ILA OR 2.8 (2.0–3.9); definite CT fibrosis OR 6.3 (3.1–12.7).
  - The association was stronger above age 50 and was not modified by smoking.
- **GWAS meta-analysis of six cohorts (AGES, COPDGene, FHS, ECLIPSE, MESA, SPIROMICS).** Hobbs BD, et al. *AJRCCM* 2019;200:1402–1413. PMID 31339356; DOI 10.1164/rccm.201903-0511OC. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1164/rccm.201903-0511OC)
  - 1,699 ILA cases and 10,274 controls.
  - MUC5B: P = 2.6×10⁻²⁷ for ILA and 1.6×10⁻²⁹ for subpleural ILA.
  - 5 of 12 IPF loci were associated with ILA (DPP9, DSP, FAM13A, IVD, MUC5B).
  - Novel ILA loci (IPO11, FCF1P3, HTRE1) were not associated with IPF, which suggests ILA include some non-IPF biology.
- **Telomere length (COPDGene, AGES, FHS).** Putman RK, et al. *Eur Respir J* 2022;60:2101814. PMID 35115336; DOI 10.1183/13993003.01814-2021. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1183/13993003.01814-2021)
  - Shortest versus longest telomere quartile and ILA: OR 2.2 (1.5–3.4) in COPDGene; OR 2.6 (1.4–4.9) in AGES.
  - In FHS, telomeres were 767 bp shorter in people with ILA.
  - There was no consistent association between telomere length and mortality.
- **Risk-factor summary in Fleischner 2020.** [Source](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2213-2600(20)30168-5)
  - Among smokers, about 2.2-fold odds of ILA per 10 years of age.
  - Male sex about 1.7-fold (an inconsistent finding across cohorts).
  - Current versus former smoking about 1.8-fold.
  - MUC5B 1.5- to 2.7-fold per allele across FHS, COPDGene and AGES, stronger for subpleural ILA.
  - MESA: occupational vapours, gases, dusts and fumes, and nitrogen oxides, were associated with ILA.
- **Air pollution (FHS).** Rice MB, et al. *Thorax* 2019;74:1063. PMID 31391318; DOI 10.1136/thoraxjnl-2018-212877. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1136/thoraxjnl-2018-212877)
  - Per IQR increase in elemental carbon: ILA OR 1.27 (1.04–1.55); ILA progression OR 1.33 (1.00–1.76).
  - PM2.5 and ozone were not associated.
- **MESA incidence.** McGroder 2023 (PMID 37202153). [Source](https://doi.org/10.1183/13993003.01950-2022)
  - MUC5B: HR 4.96 for incident fibrotic ILA.
  - IPF polygenic risk score: HR 2.09 for incident fibrotic ILA.
  - Smoking was associated only with fibrotic ILA.
- **Age as the dominant determinant.** Chae 2026 individual patient-level meta-analysis (PMID 41871447). [Source](https://doi.org/10.1093/annalsats/aaoag071)
  - Age was the most influential factor for prevalence, amplified in men and in people with more than 30 pack-years.
  - Age did not affect progression once ILA was present; progression was driven by fibrotic features.
- **RA autoimmunity markers in people without known RA (MESA, COPDGene).** Kim JS, et al. *Ann Am Thorac Soc* 2025;22:64–71. PMID 39405163; DOI 10.1513/AnnalsATS.202403-238OC. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1513/AnnalsATS.202403-238OC)
  - RA-risk HLA-DRB1 alleles were not associated with ILA.
  - In MESA, higher IgA-RF and anti-CCP levels were associated with ILA: ORs 1.20 and 1.19.
  - Among smokers, each doubling of IgM-RF was associated with ILA 10 years later: OR 1.25.
  - Associations did not differ by MUC5B status.
  - This is a direct bridge to CTD biology.
- **Korean screenees.** Choe 2025 (PMID 40985850) identified ESR, white cell count and rheumatoid factor as risk factors for progressive ILA. [Source](https://doi.org/10.3346/jkms.2025.40.e237)
- **ATS 2025 position on genetic testing.** Routine MUC5B or telomere testing is not suggested for people with ILA. For MUC5B and radiologic progression, the pooled OR was 2.6, but the evidence was "inconsistent". Among relatives of people with pulmonary fibrosis, MUC5B predicted ILA/ILD with sensitivity 56% and specificity 60%. The MUC5B variant is rare in African and Asian ancestry. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1164/rccm.202505-1054ST)

### Inferences
- General-population ILA, especially the subpleural fibrotic subtype, look biologically like "pre-IPF": they track with age, MUC5B, telomere length and IPF loci. CTD-ILD, especially SSc-ILD (typically NSIP), has a different driver (autoimmunity) and a different dominant pattern. Prognostic weights from the general population may therefore not transfer to CTD. One partial exception is RA-ILD, where MUC5B is also a known risk factor (not retrieved here; to be verified by the CTD researcher).
- MUC5B is uncommon in East Asian populations (ATS 2025), so the genetic contribution may differ in Chinese cohorts.

### Gaps
- No retrieved study tests whether MUC5B or telomere length modify ILA progression inside CTD populations. This is out of scope here and is left to the CTD-focused researcher.

## Q4. Post-2020 updates: 2022 PPF guideline, 2024–2026 statements and reviews, AI/quantitative ILA

### Takeaway
The most important post-2020 development is the **2025 Official ATS Clinical Statement on ILA** (Podolanczuk et al.). It:
- keeps the ≥5%-of-a-lung-zone threshold and the three subcategories;
- removes Fleischner's exclusion of high-risk populations, including CTD;
- creates an explicit ILA→ILD boundary (symptoms, physiology, fibrosis ≥5% of total lung volume, progression, or a major fibrotic pattern);
- suggests baseline HRCT screening for high-risk CTDs and CT follow-up every 2–3 years;
- lists CTD itself as a high-risk feature for ILA progression.

The 2022 PPF guideline mentions ILA only briefly. The AJR expert panel (Kanne 2024/2025) and an Austrian 2026 position paper add practical surveillance guidance. AI and quantitative tools carry prognostic signal but have reproducibility problems.

### Cited Findings

**ATS 2025 Clinical Statement.** Podolanczuk AJ, Hunninghake GM, ... Ryerson CJ. *AJRCCM* 2025;211:1132–1155. PMID 40387336; DOI 10.1164/rccm.202505-1054ST; PMCID PMC12264694. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1164/rccm.202505-1054ST)
- **Definition.** "ILAs [are] nondependent bilateral parenchymal abnormalities detected on CT, including ground-glass or reticular abnormalities, lung distortion, traction bronchiectasis, and/or honeycombing involving ≥5% of a lung zone by visual estimate ... but without meeting the criteria for ILD." [Source](https://doi.org/10.1164/rccm.202505-1054ST)
- **Changes from Fleischner 2020.**
  - "The updated definition removes the prior exclusion of high-risk populations."
  - "The Fleischner Society definition of ILA required findings to be incidental and excluded high-risk populations." The committee's reasons for removing this: a definition independent of pretest probability is more practical, since radiologists often lack clinical details; and "ILAs can still be present in high-risk populations, and incorporating these groups ... allows the committee to provide guidance."
  - Non-emphysematous cysts and centrilobular nodularity without other findings are no longer counted as ILA.
  - Bilaterality is now required, with the note that unilateral findings may matter in some high-risk groups: "Patients with CTD and/or occupational exposures may also present with unilateral abnormalities, but data on the risk of progression in such cases is limited."
  [Source](https://doi.org/10.1164/rccm.202505-1054ST)
- **Subcategories.** The committee considered collapsing to fibrotic versus non-fibrotic but kept all three Fleischner subcategories because "there is prognostic significance for each of the three previous categories." [Source](https://doi.org/10.1164/rccm.202505-1054ST)
- **The ILA→ILD boundary.** In a person with ILA, ILD is defined by any one of:
  - dyspnoea or cough attributed to ILD;
  - FVC, TLC or DLCO below the lower limit of normal, or meeting physiological PPF criteria;
  - fibrotic abnormality (honeycombing and/or reticulation with traction bronchiectasis) in ≥5% of **total lung volume**;
  - progressive fibrotic abnormality on serial CT;
  - a major fibrotic ILD pattern (UIP/probable UIP, fibrotic HP, fibrotic NSIP) on CT or pathology.

  "Progressive abnormalities are not a normal part of aging." [Source](https://doi.org/10.1164/rccm.202505-1054ST)
- **CTD screening suggestion.** "We suggest a baseline chest HRCT scan to screen for ILAs/ILD in adults with CTDs that are associated with an increased risk of ILD." The qualifying CTDs are RA, SSc, PM, DM, antisynthetase syndrome, MCTD, Sjögren's, undifferentiated CTD and overlap syndromes. SLE without overlap is not recommended for baseline screening because prevalence is low. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1164/rccm.202505-1054ST)
  - Pooled ILA/ILD prevalence across 52 studies of CTD patients not suspected of ILD:

| Population | Studies | Pooled ILA/ILD prevalence (95% CI) |
|---|---|---|
| All CTD | 52 | 40% (37–43%) |
| RA | 13 | 23% (17–29%) |
| SSc | 22 | 45% (42–49%) |
| Sjögren's | 5 | 39% (18–59%) |
| DM/PM | 10 | 44% (37–52%) |

  - The prevalence figures pool ILA and ILD together and do not separate them.
- **High-risk features for ILA progression (Table 4).**
  - Clinical: family history of pulmonary fibrosis, older age, smoking, other inhaled exposures, **CTD**.
  - Genetic: MUC5B variant, telomere length below the age-adjusted 10th percentile.
  - Imaging: definite fibrosis (honeycombing, traction bronchiectasis, architectural distortion), **the subpleural fibrotic and subpleural non-fibrotic subtypes**, subpleural reticulation, greater extent.
  - Physiological: abnormal or borderline FVC, TLC or DLCO.
  [Source](https://doi.org/10.1164/rccm.202505-1054ST)
- **Follow-up.** A follow-up chest CT 2–3 years after baseline is suggested, with earlier imaging (about 12 months) for high-risk features. Symptoms, PFTs and CT are reassessed every 2–3 years. Routine MUC5B or telomere testing is not suggested. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1164/rccm.202505-1054ST)
- **Thresholds.** "Although the 5% threshold needs additional supporting data, there was insufficient evidence or rationale to suggest an alternative." [Source](https://doi.org/10.1164/rccm.202505-1054ST)

**Published responses to ATS 2025.**
- **Egashira R, Yamada D, Jacob J.** "Patterns, features and extents: radiological dilemmas in the ATS statement approach to interstitial lung abnormalities." *AJRCCM* 2026;212:378–379 (correspondence). PMID 41052463; DOI 10.1164/rccm.202506-1442LE. Only the title and metadata were retrieved; the text is embargoed, so its specific arguments are unverified. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1164/rccm.202506-1442LE)
- **Fukuda 2026** (PMID 41579529). 96.7% of Fleischner-defined ILA with biopsy were reclassified as "subclinical ILD" under ATS 2025, which shows that the new boundary shifts many fibrotic ILA into the ILD category. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resinv.2026.101374)

**2022 ATS/ERS/JRS/ALAT IPF/PPF guideline** (Raghu 2022, PMID 35486072). It mentions ILA only in passing: ILA are an independent mortality risk; at least 40% progress over 4–6 years; the term "early UIP pattern" was dropped to avoid confusion with ILA. It gives no ILA management recommendations. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1164/rccm.202202-0399ST)

**AJR Expert Panel Narrative Review.** Kanne JP, et al. "Progressive Pulmonary Fibrosis and Interstitial Lung Abnormalities." *AJR* 2025;224(3):e2431125 (epub 24 April 2024). PMID 38656115; DOI 10.2214/AJR.24.31125. [Source](https://doi.org/10.2214/AJR.24.31125)
- Per the abstract, professional-society guidance leaves the role of imaging surveillance unclear for asymptomatic people with ILA who are at risk of progression. The panel describes the range of current practice and offers surveillance suggestions where no guideline exists, "where such decisions are currently driven primarily by local pulmonologists' preference."
- Only the abstract was retrieved; the specific intervals it suggests are not verified.

**Austrian Society of Pneumology and Austrian Radiological Society position paper (2026).** Kamenski-Rathmanner T, et al. *Wien Klin Wochenschr* 2026;138(Suppl 1):1–10. PMID 41636796; DOI 10.1007/s00508-025-02687-4. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00508-025-02687-4)
- Prevalence 3–10%.
- Risk of progression to ILD "20–80% depending on subtype and risk factors".
- Fibrotic, subpleural-basal ILA of greater extent carry higher risk.
- Individualised follow-up at 6–36 months.

**Narrative reviews (2022–2025), listed for completeness and not read in full.**
- Hata A, et al. *Radiology* 2021 (PMID 34374589; DOI 10.1148/radiol.2021204367). Mortality HRs of 1.3–2.7 across large cohorts; AI-based analysis "may be helpful, but further validation and improvement are needed." [Source](https://doi.org/10.1148/radiol.2021204367)
- Hata A, et al. *Radiographics* 2022 (PMID 36083805; DOI 10.1148/rg.220073). Lists imaging pitfalls that cause ILA to be overcalled: centrilobular nodularity, dependent abnormality, suboptimal inspiration, osteophyte-related lesions, apical cap and PPFE-like lesions, aspiration, infection. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1148/rg.220073)
- Kattih Z, et al. *Chest* 2025 (PMID 39393485). Narrative review; not read.
- Jin GY. *Tuberc Respir Dis* 2023 (PMID 38111097; DOI 10.4046/trd.2023.0117). In the Asian population ILA prevalence is about 4%, versus 7–10% in Western cohorts. A Korean quantitative threshold of 5% in at least one zone gave sensitivity 67.6% and specificity 93.3%; a 1.8% area threshold gave sensitivity 100% and specificity 99%. [Source](https://doi.org/10.4046/trd.2023.0117)

**AI and quantitative ILA.**
- **Baraghoshi D, et al.** *AJRCCM* 2026 (epub; PMID 42811099; DOI 10.1093/ajrccm/aamag525). Deep-learning UIP classifier (MIL-UIP) in COPDGene and AGES. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1093/ajrccm/aamag525)
  - MIL-UIP above 0.5 was associated with a 13–14% relative annual increase in the quantitative fibrosis score (DTA).
  - Mortality: HR 1.61 in COPDGene and 1.62 in AGES.
- **Lee JE, et al.** *Sci Rep* 2026 (PMID 41876612; DOI 10.1038/s41598-026-45108-w). Korean health screening, n = 3,363. Deep-learning thresholds for mortality: total ILA ≥2.89% (HR 5.15) and fibrotic ILA ≥0.26% (HR 2.71). [Source](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-45108-w)
- **Putman 2025 (COPDGene).** Baseline quantitative interstitial abnormality did not predict visual progression, but its change over time did. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1164/rccm.202501-0247OC)
- **Fleischner 2020 on quantitative methods.** High-attenuation area is "neither sensitive nor specific" for later ILA, and the paper advises against using "subclinical ILD" as a synonym for it. Sources of variation include scanner, inspiration, noise, dose, vendor and segmentation. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2213-2600(20)30168-5)
- **Ahn Y, et al.** *Eur Radiol* 2026 (PMID 42091660; DOI 10.1007/s00330-026-12595-4). Prospective same-day supine and prone deep-learning quantification in n = 38. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00330-026-12595-4)
  - Supine overestimated extent: 1.76% vs 1.39%.
  - Agreement between positions: CCC 0.770 for the fibrotic component and 0.431 for the non-fibrotic component, worst in dependent zones.

### Inferences
- For a CTD grant, ATS 2025 is an essential citation. It formally brings CTD patients into the ILA definition and recommends HRCT screening in high-risk CTDs. However, its CTD evidence is about prevalence (ILA and ILD pooled), not subtype-specific progression. That gap is the one the grant can claim.
- Under ATS 2025, many "subpleural fibrotic ILA" become "ILD" by the ≥5%-of-total-lung fibrosis or major-pattern rule. Any CTD study must state which definition it uses. Fleischner 2020 versus ATS 2025 will change subtype counts.

### Gaps
- No Japanese Respiratory Society ILA statement or updated Fleischner ILA document was found in PubMed. The Selvarajah 2025 abstract refers to a "joint statement by a European multidisciplinary task force led by the ERS" on lung-cancer-screening ILA, but that statement was not retrieved.
- The specific surveillance intervals recommended by Kanne 2024 are not verified (abstract only).

## Q5. Controversies and limitations of the ILA concept

### Takeaway
- The 5% threshold is acknowledged as arbitrary by both Fleischner and ATS.
- Interobserver agreement is moderate to good for ILA presence but only fair to moderate for subcategory assignment. Fleischner kappa was about 0.64 in a UK lung-cancer-screening study, and non-fibrotic ILA kappa was as low as 0.25 on supine CT alone.
- Dependent opacity and supine/prone differences inflate non-fibrotic ILA in particular.
- Sub-threshold ("equivocal") changes also progress, especially in high-risk groups.
- Histology is heterogeneous: smoking-related fibrosis, UIP, NSIP and BIP.
- Clinical reports overcall ILA compared with research reads.

### Cited Findings
- **Threshold.** Fleischner calls the 5% threshold "arbitrary and imprecise." [Source](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2213-2600(20)30168-5) ATS 2025: it "needs additional supporting data, [but] there was insufficient evidence ... to suggest an alternative." [Source](https://doi.org/10.1164/rccm.202505-1054ST)
- **Sub-threshold changes progress.**
  - In relatives at risk for familial pulmonary fibrosis (n = 273, mean follow-up 6.2 years), progression occurred in 65% with "mild" ILA (under 5% of a zone, or unilateral; OR 9.15) and 77% with moderate ILA (OR 17.14), compared with 15% of those without ILA. Salisbury ML, et al. *AJRCCM* 2024;210:1441. PMID 39137317; DOI 10.1164/rccm.202403-0524OC. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1164/rccm.202403-0524OC)
  - In a Japanese health check-up cohort, 23% of equivocal ILA progressed and FVC declined faster than in people without ILA (Imai 2025). [Source](https://doi.org/10.1513/AnnalsATS.202408-867OC)
- **Interobserver agreement.**
  - MESA: inter-reader agreement for ILA presence 83.5% (AC1 0.814). [Source](https://doi.org/10.1183/13993003.01950-2022)
  - UK lung cancer screening: Fleischner classification kappa 0.64 versus 0.76 for a traction-bronchiolectasis-based scheme. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjresp-2025-003298)
  - Five-reader study: Fleiss kappa for non-fibrotic ILA rose from 0.25 on supine CT to 0.51 with prone CT; for fibrotic ILA it was 0.63 rising to 0.72. Supine CT overestimated extent by 1–2%. Song J, et al. *Eur Radiol* 2025;35:3021. PMID 39694885; DOI 10.1007/s00330-024-11259-5. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00330-024-11259-5)
- **Dependent opacity and position.** Fleischner treats dependent abnormalities as equivocal unless they persist on prone imaging and recommends prone views. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2213-2600(20)30168-5) Quantitative agreement between supine and prone is poor for non-fibrotic ILA (CCC 0.431), worst in dependent zones (Ahn 2026). [Source](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00330-026-12595-4)
- **Clinical overcalling.** In the tertiary-centre practice-pattern study, almost one-third of cases labelled ILA on clinical reads were reclassified as no ILA on research reads. The term was used almost exclusively by thoracic-trained radiologists. Clin Imaging 2023. PMID 37862912; DOI 10.1016/j.clinimag.2023.109996. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clinimag.2023.109996)
- **Histological heterogeneity.**
  - Most ILA in smokers' resection specimens represent smoking-related fibrosis rather than UIP (Fleischner pathology section). [Source](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2213-2600(20)30168-5)
  - Biopsied fibrotic ILA in Japan showed UIP 43%, NSIP 20% and BIP 20% (Fukuda 2026). [Source](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resinv.2026.101374)
  - In Korea, 69% of subpleural fibrotic ILA were definite or probable UIP (Chae 2022). [Source](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00330-021-08378-8)
- **Mortality not specific to the lung.** Respiratory deaths were under 15% of deaths among people with ILA in AGES-Reykjavik. Fleischner suggests "accelerated physiological ageing or other causes of death" contribute. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2213-2600(20)30168-5)
- **Progression definitions vary across studies.** Visual "any increase", quantitative score increases and composite clinical definitions are all used. Pooled estimates are highly heterogeneous: Seok 2024 reported I² = 90% for mortality, and Hwang 2025 found progression rates differing by follow-up length. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-57831-3); [Source](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00330-024-10952-9)
- **Fibrotic subtype as a predictor is not always independent.** In Korean lung-cancer-screening participants with preserved FVC, fibrotic subtype did not independently predict mortality (p = 0.254), whereas CT progression did (HR 8.74) (Ahn Y 2026, PMID 40715650). [Source](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00330-025-11874-w)

### Inferences
- Subcategory assignment is the least reproducible step, particularly separating subpleural non-fibrotic ILA from dependent opacity or early fibrosis. A CTD study applying Fleischner subtypes should include prone imaging (or a protocol to handle dependent opacity), multiple blinded readers with kappa reported, and a pre-specified definition of progression.
- Because sub-threshold changes progress in high-risk groups (familial pulmonary fibrosis relatives), the general-population 5% threshold may be too insensitive for CTD screening. ATS 2025 itself notes that unilateral or limited findings may matter in high-risk groups.

### Gaps
- No retrieved study directly quantifies interobserver kappa for the three-way Fleischner subcategory scheme in a general-population cohort, separately from fibrotic versus non-fibrotic.
- The first author of PMID 37862912 (*Clin Imaging* 2023) was not captured; cite it by PMID and DOI.
- No general-population study was found that compares how well Fleischner 2020 and ATS 2025 definitions predict outcomes.
