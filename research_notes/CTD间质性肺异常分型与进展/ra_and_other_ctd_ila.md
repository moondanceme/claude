# ILA / subclinical ILD in RA and other non-SSc CTDs (IIM/ASS/anti-MDA5, MCTD, SLE, pSS, UCTD/IPAF)

Search method: PubMed MCP (about 30 calls, search date 2026-10-28). Queries combined "interstitial lung abnormalit*", "subclinical/preclinical interstitial", "Fleischner", "subpleural fibrotic", MUC5B, China[Affiliation], with each CTD. All PMIDs/DOIs below come from records I retrieved. Where only the abstract was read (no full text), that is stated. A "negative" result means nothing turned up in these PubMed searches. It does not prove that no such paper exists.

## Q0. Framework: the ILA definition has changed and now covers CTD populations

### Takeaway
The 2025 ATS clinical statement removed the Fleischner 2020 rule that excluded high-risk populations from the ILA definition. It also set criteria that separate ILA from ILD, and it suggests screening adults with CTD. "ILA in CTD" is therefore now a sanctioned concept. A study that applies the new rules to CTD cohorts is timely, and no study has done so yet.

### Cited Findings
- ATS Clinical Statement 2025 (PMID 40387336). ILA is defined as nondependent bilateral GGO/reticulation/distortion/traction bronchiectasis/honeycombing involving ≥5% of a lung zone. **"The updated definition removes the prior exclusion of high-risk populations."** ILD is separated from ILA by attributable symptoms, abnormal or declining lung function, fibrotic change (honeycombing and/or reticulation with traction bronchiectasis ≥5% of total lung volume), progressive imaging, or a specific fibrotic pattern. The statement suggests screening adults with CTD and repeating CT every 2–3 years in people with ILA. — [Am J Respir Crit Care Med 2025](https://doi.org/10.1164/rccm.202505-1054ST)
- A 2026 multidisciplinary review notes that the ATS statement "reshaped the definition… proposed by the Fleischner Society in 2020, introducing substantial changes to the populations in whom ILAs may be identified", and names CTD as a key at-risk group (PMID 42863827). — [Semin Respir Crit Care Med 2026](https://doi.org/10.1055/a-2969-5614)
- A Radiology 2025 review states that "subpleural fibrotic ILA is a prognostic imaging biomarker, predictive of worse prognosis" (general populations, not CTD) (PMID 40358445). — [Radiology 2025](https://doi.org/10.1148/radiol.241252)
- The 2023 ACR/CHEST guideline on ILD screening and monitoring in SARDs (PMID 38973714) and the 2025 ERS/EULAR CTD-ILD guidelines (PMIDs 40912974 / 40907995) were retrieved by title only. I did not read their content on subclinical ILD. — [ACR/CHEST](https://doi.org/10.1002/art.42860); [ERS/EULAR ARD](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ard.2025.08.021)

### Inferences
- Under the 2025 ATS rules, many "subclinical CTD-ILD" cases in older studies would count as ILD rather than ILA, for example fibrotic reticulation with traction bronchiectasis ≥5% of total lung, or any progression. Prevalence figures from older cohorts are not directly comparable with figures under the new definitions. A prospective study could quantify this reclassification as a primary methods contribution.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve the full text of the ATS 2025 statement. Its exact handling of CTD patients (for example, whether ILA in CTD should automatically be called CTD-ILD) needs confirmation from the full text.

## Q1. RA: prevalence, progression, predictors and mortality of ILA / subclinical RA-ILD

### Takeaway
RA has the deepest evidence base. Subclinical ILD or ILA appears in about 17–33% of patients by HRCT in referral and research cohorts, but in only about 4–6% of an unselected Danish early-RA cohort. CT progression occurs in roughly 30–57% of those with abnormalities over 1–4.4 years (86% in one small pilot). Predictors are subpleural distribution, greater baseline extent, MUC5B rs35705950, male sex, age, smoking, RF/ACPA, inflammatory markers and disease activity. ILA in RA roughly triples mortality among smokers. **No retrieved RA study classifies ILA by Fleischner 2020 subtypes (non-subpleural / subpleural non-fibrotic / subpleural fibrotic) and tracks outcomes by subtype prospectively.** Most use the older Hunninghake/Washko "sequential reading" (ILA present/indeterminate/absent; ILA 0–3) or ad hoc definitions.

### Cited Findings
**Prevalence and progression cohorts**
- **Gochuico 2008** (PMID 18227362). NIH, n=64 RA patients without dyspnea or cough. Preclinical ILD on HRCT in 21/64 (**33%**). HRCT abnormalities **progressed in 12/21 (57%)**. Associations: smoking history (P<.001), crackles, A-a gradient. Methotrexate use was more frequent in progressors (P=.046). Follow-up duration was not given in the abstract. — [Arch Intern Med 2008](https://doi.org/10.1001/archinternmed.2007.59)
- **Doyle 2014, BRASS** (PMID 24305643). Brigham RA Sequential Study, 91/1,145 subjects with clinically indicated CT. Using the validated sequential reading method: 12 radiologically severe ILA, 34 ILA, 38 no ILA. Severe ILA was associated with older age, cough and dyspnea, higher RF and swollen joints, and a trend toward lower FVC (77% vs 94%) and DLCO (52% vs 77%). Cross-sectional design. — [Chest 2014](https://doi.org/10.1378/chest.13-1394)
- **Kawano-Dourado / Doyle 2020** (PMID 32428513). Retrospective, Brazil and Brigham, 293 RA patients with clinically indicated CT in 2014–2016. Interstitial changes in 64 (22%). Of 56 with sequential CT, **38% progressed over 4.4 years; 29% of ILA progressed**. Risk factors for progression: **subpleural distribution and higher baseline extent**. About half of progressive ILA had been found on CT done for non-pulmonary reasons. — [Chest 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chest.2020.04.061)
- **Dong 2018** (PMID 31657702). Prospective pilot, n=18 ACPA+ early RA (diagnosed within 1 year), HRCT at 0 and 1 year. ILA in 7/18 (39%). **6/7 (86%) progressed at 1 year**, while 10/11 without ILA stayed stable. CRP was higher in progressors (3.5 vs 1 mg/L, p=0.024). Smoking and autoantibodies were not associated. — [Eur J Rheumatol 2018](https://doi.org/10.5152/eurjrheum.2019.19044)
- **Hyldgaard 2023** (PMID 37066633; DOI 10.1080/03009742.2023.2194105). Prospective, single centre, Denmark, n=150 newly diagnosed RA (137 had CT). 59% were ever-smokers. 2 had clinically significant ILD and **6 had mild reticulation suggestive of preclinical ILD** (about 4–6%). **No ILD progression at 2-year follow-up.** Nodules (42%), bronchiectasis (29%) and emphysema (20%) were common. The authors favour symptom-based screening plus smoking cessation over ILD screening at diagnosis. — [Scand J Rheumatol 2023](https://doi.org/10.1080/03009742.2023.2194105)
- **Hyldgaard 2024** (PMID 39445640). Same cohort, 5-year follow-up, n=115. Three risk algorithms each flagged about one-third as high risk, but only 3 patients had clinical or subclinical ILD. Agreement between the two clinical models was kappa 0.43. — [Scand J Rheumatol 2024](https://doi.org/10.1080/03009742.2024.2408867)
- **Vermant 2026** (PMID 41930622). Prospective pilot, KU Leuven, n=81 RA with HRCT at baseline and 12–18 months, plus 72-zone lung ultrasound. **Radiologic progression (blinded 5-point Likert) in 31% (25/81).** Baseline B-lines and their change predicted progression (AUC 0.78; 0.82 with ΔDLCO), outperforming symptoms (AUC 0.58) and PFTs (AUC 0.59). The term "ILA" is used, but the abstract mentions no Fleischner subtyping. — [Arthritis Rheumatol 2026](https://doi.org/10.1002/art.70161). A comment on statistical issues followed (PMID 42112593). — [Arthritis Rheumatol 2026 comment](https://doi.org/10.1002/art.70219)
- **Mochizuki 2026** (PMID 42735675). Japan (Kamagaya General Hospital). Letter titled "Radiological change in ILA and ILD in patients with RA at 1 year". **No abstract available, so the data are unknown.** — [Int J Rheum Dis 2026](https://doi.org/10.1111/1756-185x.70864)
- **Chai 2023** (PMID 37809328). **China, Beijing Chaoyang Hospital.** n=371 RA. "Preclinical ILD (pILD)" plus ILD in 32.3%. Among 98 RA-ILD/pILD patients followed a median 19.1 months, **51.0% showed fibrotic progression on HRCT**. Predictors of progression: diabetes (HR 2.03), DAS28-ESR >5.1 (HR 2.21), baseline HRCT score >5 (HR 2.30). Risk factors for prevalence: age >60, smoking, diabetes, LDH >250, anti-CCP+. The abstract does not separate pILD from ILD in the progression analysis. — [Front Med 2023](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmed.2023.1265355)
- Older pilot data: a prospective cohort of 40 early RA patients without respiratory symptoms followed for 5 years. 13 had lung involvement, 3 developed ILD, and DLCO decline correlated with baseline CRP/ESR. Methotrexate and smoking were not associated (PMID 32973236). — [Sci Rep 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-020-72768-z)
- A small Turkish cross-sectional study of 30 asymptomatic RA patients found "ILD" by CT visual score in 73.3%. This is likely an over-inclusive definition (PMID 35848433). — [Turk Thorac J 2022](https://doi.org/10.5152/TurkThoracJ.2022.21263)

**Predictors (genetic, serologic, quantitative CT)**
- **Juge/ESPOIR–TRANSLATE2 risk score** (PMID 35583934). Two prospective RA cohorts without pulmonary symptoms (n=163 and n=89). Subclinical RA-ILD in 19.0% and 16.9%. Independent risk factors: **MUC5B rs35705950 T allele OR 3.74**, male sex OR 3.93, age at onset OR 1.10/yr, DAS28-ESR OR 2.03/unit. Score AUC 0.82 (discovery) and 0.78 (replication). — [Arthritis Rheumatol 2022](https://doi.org/10.1002/art.42162)
- **Matson 2022** (PMID 34874815). Research letter: in a prospectively screened RA cohort, subclinical ILD was associated with the MUC5B promoter variant. No abstract was available, so I could not extract effect sizes. — [AJRCCM 2022](https://doi.org/10.1164/rccm.202109-2087LE)
- **Quantitative densitometry** (PMID 34747452). n=193 RA, 106 rescanned after a mean 39 months. **%HAA increased in 49%.** Predictors of increase: higher baseline %HAA, **MUC5B minor allele**, and absence of HLA-DRB1 shared epitope (AUC 0.69). Anti-CCP level was associated with the size of the increase. — [Rheumatology 2022](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/keab828)
- **COPDGene, RA smokers** (PMID 37871923). 83 RA vs 8,725 non-RA. **ILA 16.9% vs 5.0%; adjusted OR 4.76 (2.54–8.92).** RA with ILA or indeterminate ILA had higher all-cause mortality: **HR 3.16 (2.11–4.74)** vs non-RA without ILA, and **HR 3.02 (1.36–6.75)** vs RA without ILA. — [Rheumatology 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/kead277)
- COPDGene 2026 (PMID 42753287). 76 RA vs 8,175 non-RA: ILA 17.3% vs 7.1%. RA was associated with more acute respiratory exacerbations over 10 years (aHR 1.68). — [Semin Arthritis Rheum 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.semarthrit.2026.153080)
- COPDGene QCT (PMID 38048611). RA was associated with a higher percentage of interstitial changes (β=1.7, P=0.0008). — [Rheumatology 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/kead645). COPDGene PRS (PMID 40090204): the IPF PRS was associated with ILA (OR 1.88/unit) and the RA PRS was not. — [Semin Arthritis Rheum 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.semarthrit.2025.152708)
- **Population cohorts (no RA diagnosis), MESA/COPDGene.** RF IgA and anti-CCP were associated with ILA, mainly in ever-smokers (ILA prevalence +13–14% per doubling) (PMID 27609750). — [Thorax 2016](https://doi.org/10.1136/thoraxjnl-2016-208932). Among smokers without baseline ILA, each doubling of IgM RF gave **OR 1.25 for ILA 10 years later**. RA-risk HLA alleles were not associated (PMID 39405163). — [Ann Am Thorac Soc 2025](https://doi.org/10.1513/AnnalsATS.202403-238OC). A broader ACPA repertoire was associated with *lower* ILA odds (OR 0.83/log-unit), modified by race (PMID 41821027). — [Respir Res 2026](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12931-026-03616-3)
- **Korean health-screening cohort** (not RA-specific; PMID 40985850). n=2,589, median 7.0-year CT interval. **Higher baseline RF** independently predicted progressive ILA, along with age, smoking, WBC and ESR. ILA aHR for mortality 4.01; progressive ILA aHR 3.92. — [J Korean Med Sci 2025](https://doi.org/10.3346/jkms.2025.40.e237)
- Review data: the Chest 2025 review (PMID 41354403) states that prospective studies show subclinical ILD in about 25% of RA patients. Reported risk factors: age, male sex, smoking, CRP/ESR at diagnosis, DAS28, RF/anti-CCP, MUC5B, MMP7, PARC, SP-D. "The optimal treatment approach for subclinical disease is currently unknown." — [Chest 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chest.2025.11.033). J Rheumatol 2026 (PMID 41326168): "Clinical trials are needed to identify an intervention that alters the natural history" of screen-detected subclinical RA-ILD. — [J Rheumatol 2026](https://doi.org/10.3899/jrheum.2025-0797)
- **Retraction warning.** Yoo et al., "CTD-ILD and ILA: evolving concept", Eur J Radiol Open 2020 (PMID 33364263) is tagged **"Retracted Publication"** in PubMed. It is often cited for "ILA in 20–60% of RA, 35–45% progress". A 2022 re-publication of the same title exists (PMID 35445144, DOI 10.1016/j.ejro.2022.100419). Do not cite the 2020 version. — [Retracted 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejro.2020.100311); [2022 version](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejro.2022.100419)

### Inferences
- Progression rates vary widely (0% at 2 years in Danish early RA; 29–57% in referral or symptom-enriched cohorts). The variation tracks case mix, ILA definition and CT indication. This is the gap that standardized Fleischner/ATS subtyping is meant to address.
- The Kawano-Dourado finding that subpleural distribution and extent drive progression fits the Fleischner logic that subpleural fibrotic ILA carries higher risk. No one has tested the three Fleischner subtypes formally in RA.

### Gaps
- No retrieved RA study reports outcomes by Fleischner 2020 ILA subtypes. The Vermant 2026 and Mochizuki 2026 papers may use them, but this could not be verified from the abstracts. Their full texts should be checked.
- I did not retrieve Juge et al. NEJM 2018 (MUC5B in RA-ILD) or the full Matson 2022 data, so I cannot quote their effect sizes.
- RA-specific Korean or Japanese prospective ILA cohorts were not found beyond the Mochizuki letter and KEIO-RA (PMID 40192850, which concerns AE-ILD and is not relevant to ILA).

## Q2. IIM / ASS / anti-MDA5: natural history of subclinical or mild ILD at diagnosis

### Takeaway
I found **no study applying ILA terminology or Fleischner subtypes to IIM cohorts with follow-up.** The IIM literature calls the early stage "subclinical", "early" or "non-RP" ILD. Natural-history data come from phenotype-clustering cohorts (mostly Chinese anti-MDA5) and from ASS cohorts. These show heterogeneity: some anti-MDA5 patients have indolent or asymptomatic ILD, but high-risk clusters progress rapidly regardless of how mild the initial CT looks. ILA appears only incidentally in IIM papers, for example as one HRCT category in an Italian cohort and in case reports.

### Cited Findings
- **Xu 2023** (PMID 35849805). **China (Nanjing).** n=265 anti-MDA5+ DM, cluster analysis. Cluster 1 (n=108): cumulative non-RPILD 85.2%, mortality 3.7%. Cluster 2 (n=72): non-RPILD 73.6%, mortality 9.7%. Cluster 3 (n=85): non-RPILD 32.9%, mortality 60%, enriched for high anti-MDA5 titre plus anti-Ro52. Classification algorithm built from 8 variables (age >50, course <3 months, weakness, arthritis, CRP, CK, Ro52 titre, MDA5 titre); accuracy 78.5% in development and 70% in external validation. — [Arthritis Rheumatol 2023](https://doi.org/10.1002/art.42308)
- **Nanjing anti-MDA5 course study 2025** (PMID 40764133). Chinese journal. n=249 anti-MDA5 DM. A short disease course (≤6 months, n=207) had more ILD and RP-ILD and higher mortality (29.9% vs 4.8%) than a longer course (>6 months, n=42). — [Zhonghua Jie He He Hu Xi Za Zhi 2025](https://doi.org/10.3760/cma.j.cn112147-20241229-00761)
- Case series of three Latino patients with **asymptomatic or slowly progressive anti-MDA5 ILD**, including normal PFTs at 18 months on MMF and stability without ILD therapy (PMID 39040087). — [Respir Med Case Rep 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rmcr.2024.102072)
- Case report: anti-MDA5 DM with "mild interstitial lung abnormalities" on HRCT, treated pre-emptively with PSL+CNI+IVCY because of the RP risk (PMID 42664192). This illustrates that clinicians do not watch and wait in anti-MDA5. — [Mod Rheumatol Case Rep 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/mrcr/rxag085)
- **TYPASS (ASS)** (PMID 41376133). n=132 ASS-ILD. RP-ILD at diagnosis in 39%, associated with fever (aOR 128), OP pattern (aOR 66.8), pleural effusion and male sex. Cluster 1 (n=62, anti-Jo-1, non-RP) had a good prognosis. — [J Intern Med 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/joim.70058)
- **British Columbia IIM cohort** (PMID 41610110). n=111 (ASS/anti-MDA5/scleromyositis), median follow-up 35.5 months. ILD prevalence 87.5% in ASS, 84.6% in anti-MDA5 and 65.5% in scleromyositis. "Post-myopathic progressive pulmonary fibrosis" in 13.5% overall (23.1% anti-MDA5, 16.1% ASS). OP/NSIP overlap was more common in progressors. — [ACR Open Rheumatol 2026](https://doi.org/10.1002/acr2.90010)
- **Brescia IIM cohort** (PMID 33141387). n=165 IIM. HRCT patterns were centrally classified as NSIP/OP/UIP/indeterminate/**ILA**. ILD in 31.5%. Anti-Ro52 and dyspnea predicted ILD. Most ILD began within 12 months of IIM onset. ILA was used only as a descriptive category, without outcome data in the abstract. — [Clin Rev Allergy Immunol 2020](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12016-020-08814-5)
- PM/DM *without* ILD: quantitative CT showed subclinical pulmonary vascular loss (PMID 42111821). This is relevant to a "pre-ILA" baseline. — [PeerJ 2026](https://doi.org/10.7717/peerj.21259)
- The Lancet Respir Med 2024 review (PMID 39622261) states that IIM pulmonary manifestations range "from subclinical ILD to rapidly progressive respiratory failure". — [Lancet Respir Med 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2213-2600(24)00267-4)
- The MCTC systematic review protocol (PMID 42363307) says IIM-ILD lacks standardized nomenclature and definitions. This supports a standardized-terminology study. — [Syst Rev 2026](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13643-026-03230-w)

### Inferences
- In anti-MDA5, the severity of the baseline CT is not a reliable guide to safety: high-risk serologic and clinical clusters progress fast. A Fleischner-ILA-based watchful-waiting framework may therefore be unsafe or ethically constrained in anti-MDA5. A multi-CTD study should stratify or exclude anti-MDA5, or treat it as a separate high-risk arm.
- In ASS, non-RP anti-Jo-1 phenotypes may have a more ILA-like indolent course. I found no data on untreated mild ASS-ILD trajectories.

### Gaps
- No cohort documents untreated mild or subclinical IIM-ILD with serial CT. Nearly all IIM patients receive immunosuppression for muscle or skin disease, which confounds natural history.
- Large Japanese multicentre cohorts (e.g., JAMI) were not retrieved in these searches.

## Q3. SLE, pSS, MCTD, UCTD/IPAF: subclinical-ILD screening cohorts and progression

### Takeaway
Most data are cross-sectional or retrospective prevalence studies. Progression data on *subclinical* disease are sparse. A 2026 SLE meta-analysis gives a pooled ILA/radiographic-ILD prevalence of **12.4%** against 2.9% for clinical ILD. A Chinese pSS study used ILA 0–3 grading (50% ILA 2–3) but was cross-sectional. MCTD has the best longitudinal data, from the Norwegian and international cohorts, but these use "ILD" rather than ILA. **No UCTD/IPAF ILA study was found.**

### Cited Findings
**SLE**
- **SLE meta-analysis** (PMID 42565363). 40 studies. Pooled prevalence of **ILA/radiographic ILD 12.39% (95% CI 9.68–15.36%)** vs clinical ILD 2.92% (1.50–4.46%). Among SLE-ILD, NSIP 52.5% and UIP 12.1%. The authors suggest targeted HRCT screening. — [Arthritis Care Res 2026](https://doi.org/10.1002/acr.80132)
- Small Indian SLE study (n=38). HRCT abnormalities in 55% of patients, many asymptomatic and with normal radiographs or PFTs. Cross-sectional (PMID 18405129). [J Assoc Physicians India 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18405129/)
- **I found no SLE study with prospective CT follow-up of subclinical ILD or ILA.**

**pSS**
- **Weng 2022** (PMID 36217184). **China (Xiamen).** n=69 consecutive pSS patients graded with **ILA 0–3 categories** (the Hunninghake-type scale, not Fleischner subtypes). ILA 2–3 ("pSS-ILD") in 50%. Age, disease duration, KL-6 and TNF-α discriminated ILA 2–3 from ILA 0–1 (AUC 0.883). Cross-sectional, no follow-up. — [Eur J Med Res 2022](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40001-022-00828-3)
- Retrospective cohort, India (PMID 38736967). ILD in 33/550 (6%), 15% of them asymptomatic. — [Mediterr J Rheumatol 2024](https://doi.org/10.31138/mjr.230323.cca)
- Turkish cohort (PMID 36422505). ILD on CT in 13.3% of 120; ILD was associated with ESSDAI; some cases were asymptomatic. — [Turk J Med Sci 2022](https://doi.org/10.55730/1300-0144.5513). Polish registry (PMID 39691174): 34/64 with ESSDAI pulmonary-domain involvement had ILD; 9% of those with lung changes were asymptomatic. — [Ther Adv Musculoskelet Dis 2024](https://doi.org/10.1177/1759720X241305218). Tunisia, n=30 (PMID 38188883): pulmonary involvement was clinically silent in one-third. — [Pan Afr Med J 2023](https://doi.org/10.11604/pamj.2023.46.42.36534)
- **I found no pSS study with prospective follow-up of subclinical ILD or ILA.**

**MCTD**
- **Boleto 2023** (PMID 37696231). International, 14 centres, n=300 MCTD, mean follow-up 7.8 years. Baseline ILD was associated with older age, skin thickening, upper GI symptoms, FVC/DLCO <80%, anti-topo, SSA, cryoglobulinemia and CRP. **Digital ulcers predicted FVC decline >10%.** MCTD-ILD had higher mortality. "ILD" was defined by HRCT; subclinical disease was not separated. — [Semin Arthritis Rheum 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.semarthrit.2023.152258)
- **Juvenile-onset MCTD, Norway** (PMID 30068767). n=52, mean disease duration 16 years. ILD on HRCT in 27%, mostly <10% extent. **HRCT findings did not progress significantly over a mean of about 8.8 years between scans, although FVC declined.** — [J Rheumatol 2018](https://doi.org/10.3899/jrheum.180019)
- Norwegian nationwide MCTD cohort: HLA-B*18 and DRB1*03:01 are risk factors for lung fibrosis (PMID 25187641). — [Rheumatology 2014](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/keu310)
- The adult Norwegian MCTD ILD-progression paper (Reiseter et al.) was not retrieved, so I cannot cite its numbers.

**UCTD / IPAF**
- My searches found only reviews of IPAF (PMIDs 32936097, 29950308). **I found no study applying ILA terminology to UCTD/IPAF and none with screening-detected subclinical ILD follow-up.**

### Inferences
- In SLE and pSS, "ILA" mainly appears in prevalence pooling (SLE meta-analysis) or ILA 0–3 grading (Chinese pSS). Prospective natural history of screen-detected ILA in these diseases is essentially unstudied, which makes this a clear novelty space.

### Gaps
- No SLE, pSS or UCTD/IPAF prospective ILA progression data.
- Adult MCTD progression data limited to "ILD"; ILA-level data absent.

## Q4. Studies explicitly using "ILA" with Fleischner subtypes and prospective follow-up in any non-SSc CTD

### Takeaway
**None found.** A PubMed search for "Fleischner" AND "interstitial lung abnormalit*" combined with any CTD term returned only two records, both reviews or statements (ATS 2025; the 2026 narrative review). A search for the subtype names ("subpleural fibrotic", "non-subpleural") plus CTD terms returned only a Radiology 2025 review.

### Inferences
- A prospective multi-CTD cohort that applies Fleischner 2020 subtyping together with the ATS 2025 ILA-vs-ILD criteria, with serial CT and PFT, appears **novel** for RA, IIM, SLE, pSS, MCTD and UCTD/IPAF. In RA, the novelty lies in the subtyping and in head-to-head comparison across diseases, not in studying ILA progression itself. In the other CTDs, even basic ILA natural-history data are missing.

### Gaps
- Embase, Web of Science, CNKI/Wanfang and conference abstracts (ACR/EULAR/ATS/ERS) were not searched. Unpublished or abstract-only Fleischner-subtyping CTD studies may exist. Registry searches (ClinicalTrials.gov, ChiCTR) were not done.

## Q5. Chinese PubMed-indexed studies on subclinical ILD/ILA in CTD

### Takeaway
A search combining China[Affiliation] with "ILA / subclinical / preclinical / early interstitial" and CTD terms returned only 8 records. The relevant ones are the Beijing RA "preclinical ILD" progression cohort (Chai 2023), the Xiamen pSS ILA 0–3 biomarker study (Weng 2022), and the Nanjing anti-MDA5 phenotype and disease-course studies. **None uses Fleischner ILA subtypes.**

### Cited Findings
- Chai 2023, Beijing Chaoyang: RA pILD/ILD 32.3%; 51% fibrotic progression over median 19.1 months; predictors diabetes, DAS28-ESR >5.1, HRCT score >5 (PMID 37809328). — [Front Med 2023](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmed.2023.1265355)
- Weng 2022, Xiamen: pSS ILA 2–3 in 50%; KL-6, TNF-α and age were discriminators (PMID 36217184). — [Eur J Med Res 2022](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40001-022-00828-3)
- Xu 2023, Nanjing: anti-MDA5 three clusters, RPILD risk 14.8% / 26.4% / 67.1% (PMID 35849805). — [Arthritis Rheumatol 2023](https://doi.org/10.1002/art.42308)
- Nanjing 2025: anti-MDA5 short vs long course, mortality 29.9% vs 4.8% (PMID 40764133). — [Chinese J Tuberc Respir Dis 2025](https://doi.org/10.3760/cma.j.cn112147-20241229-00761)
- Chinese RA proteomics: MMP3 + VWF + KL-6 detected RA-ILD in newly diagnosed RA (AUC 0.90, n=110). This is a case-control diagnostic study, not natural history (PMID 41143689). — [Ann Med 2025](https://doi.org/10.1080/07853890.2025.2576636)

### Inferences
- Chinese PubMed-indexed literature has no prospective, Fleischner-subtyped, multi-CTD ILA cohort. A Chinese multi-CTD prospective study would fill both a regional and a global gap.

### Gaps
- CNKI/Wanfang (Chinese-language databases) were not searched. Chinese-language papers using "间质性肺异常" may exist outside PubMed.
