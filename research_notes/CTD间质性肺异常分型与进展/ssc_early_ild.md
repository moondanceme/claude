# Natural history of subclinical / mild / limited / early ILD on CT in systemic sclerosis (SSc)

Search scope and method: PubMed (MCP tool; abstracts retrieved for every paper cited below) plus web search, run on 2026-10-28. Priority was 2015–2026. Goh 2008 is included because it is the source of the "limited vs extensive" staging. Every figure below comes from the PubMed abstract unless the entry says otherwise. Full texts were not read, so some numbers stop at what the abstract reports.

---

## Q1. Prevalence of ILD / subclinical ILD on screening HRCT in SSc, including patients with normal PFTs

### Takeaway
Screening HRCT finds ILD in about half of unselected SSc patients. The figure is 50% in the Norwegian nationwide cohort and 54–71% in referral or early-diffuse cohorts. A large share of these patients have "normal" PFTs: 31–62.5% of those with HRCT-ILD have FVC ≥80%. When ILD is first diagnosed, most of it is limited (Goh): pooled estimate 63.5%. New ILD keeps appearing after a negative baseline HRCT, at about 3.7–4.4 per 100 person-years, for at least 10 years.

### Cited Findings
- **Nor-SSc nationwide cohort** (Hoffmann-Vold, AJRCCM 2019; PMID 31310156; DOI 10.1164/rccm.201903-0486OC). Population-based, n=815 (all SSc patients in Norway, 2000–2012). HRCT available for 650. At baseline 50% (n=324) had ILD on HRCT. Mortality rose with fibrosis extent: SMR 2.2 with no fibrosis vs 8.0 with >25% fibrosis. In patients with **normal-range FVC (80–100%)**, 5- and 10-year survival was 83%/80% without fibrosis vs **69%/56% with fibrosis** (p=0.03). The authors read this as showing that "the mere presence of ILD" affects outcome. — [Hoffmann-Vold 2019](https://doi.org/10.1164/rccm.201903-0486OC)
- **Suliman, Arthritis Rheumatol 2015** (PMID 26316389; DOI 10.1002/art.39405). Prospective, n=102 (Zurich). 63% had "significant" ILD on HRCT, but only 26% had FVC <80%. **40/64 (62.5%) of patients with HRCT-ILD had normal FVC.** 5 of those 40 had severe, functionally occult fibrosis, and in 2 of them every PFT was normal. Occult ILD with normal FVC was associated with anti-Scl-70, diffuse SSc and ACA negativity. — [Suliman 2015](https://doi.org/10.1002/art.39405)
- **Showalter, J Rheumatol 2018** (PMID 30275265; DOI 10.3899/jrheum.171362). n=265 (Northwestern). 71% had radiographic ILD. **31% of those with ILD had FVC ≥80%**, and 43% had DLCO ≥60%. The best single thresholds were FVC <80% (sensitivity 0.69, specificity 0.73) and DLCO <62% (sensitivity 0.60). Every FVC/DLCO combination had NPV <0.70. NPV of FVC <80% was 0.05 in Scl-70+ vs 0.57 in Scl-70− patients. — [Showalter 2018](https://doi.org/10.3899/jrheum.171362)
- **Bernstein, Arthritis Rheumatol 2020** (PMID 32583956; DOI 10.1002/art.41415). PRESS registry of early diffuse SSc, 11 US sites, n=212. 54% had radiographic ILD. Sensitivity was 63% for FVC <80% and 85% for FVC <80% or DLCO <80%. NPV was 61% and 70%. — [Bernstein 2020](https://doi.org/10.1002/art.41415)
- **Early / sine scleroderma** (Torres Villarreal, ARP Rheumatol 2025; PMID 40170470; DOI 10.63032/RZDO2574). Cross-sectional, n=353. HRCT-ILD in **38% of SSc sine scleroderma (n=65) and 20% of VEDOSS / very-early SSc (n=75)**. Associations: ACA OR 0.17; anti-Scl-70 OR 35.8. — [Torres Villarreal 2025](https://doi.org/10.63032/RZDO2574)
- **Extent at diagnosis** (Rubio-Rivas, Rev Clin Esp 2024; PMID 38387499; DOI 10.1016/j.rceng.2024.02.008). Systematic review and meta-analysis (17 non-overlapping studies, n=2,149). **At SSc-ILD diagnosis 63.5% (95% CI 55.3–73) was limited and 34.3% extensive by Goh criteria.** Mean FVC was 80.5%. — [Rubio-Rivas 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rceng.2024.02.008)
- **Incident ILD after a negative baseline HRCT** (EUSTAR CP133; Ann Rheum Dis 2026; PMID 41549016; DOI 10.1016/j.ard.2025.12.008). n=5,331 with negative baseline HRCT. Incidence was **3.83 per 100 person-years**, with continuous detection up to 10 years. Predictors: NYHA ≥2, muscle weakness, raised inflammatory markers, SSc-specific antibodies (not disease duration). Incident ILD carried a *lower* progression risk than prevalent ILD, but mortality was about double that of ILD-negative patients. — [EUSTAR 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ard.2025.12.008)
- **CSRG (Canada)** (Hoa, Arthritis Rheumatol 2025; PMID 39492683; DOI 10.1002/art.43051). n=969 without prevalent ILD. 21% developed incident HRCT-ILD over a median of 2.4 years. Incidence was 3.7/100 PY in late SSc (>7 years), RR 0.68 vs earlier SSc. At onset, incident ILD was functionally mild (FVC 88% / 87%; DLCO 64% / 62%). Progression rates were similar for late- and early-onset ILD (HR 1.11). — [Hoa 2025](https://doi.org/10.1002/art.43051)
- **CSRG** (Hurtubise, Respir Med 2021; PMID 34089970; DOI 10.1016/j.rmed.2021.106482). n=798. 158 new clinically apparent ILD cases over a median of 4.4 years, a crude incidence of **4.4/100 PY**. — [Hurtubise 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rmed.2021.106482)
- **Australian Scleroderma Cohort** (Morrisroe, Semin Arthritis Rheum 2025; PMID 40974697; DOI 10.1016/j.semarthrit.2025.152826). n=2,198. 17.7% developed incident ILD over a median of 5.8 years. This is HRCT on clinical suspicion, not universal screening. — [Morrisroe 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.semarthrit.2025.152826)
- **Guideline position.** The 2023 ACR/CHEST guideline (PMID 38973714; DOI 10.1002/art.42860) conditionally recommends PFTs **and** HRCT for screening at-risk SARD patients, including SSc. — [ACR/CHEST 2023](https://doi.org/10.1002/art.42860). The European consensus (Lancet Rheumatol 2020; PMID 38263663; DOI 10.1016/S2665-9913(19)30144-4) says all SSc patients should be screened with HRCT. — [Hoffmann-Vold 2020 consensus](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2665-9913(19)30144-4)
- **Practice survey** (Hoa, Rheumatology 2022; PMID 34918033; DOI 10.1093/rheumatology/keab929). n=198 respondents. Only 59% routinely order HRCT at SSc diagnosis (40% in Canada and Australia). 48% would not treat subclinical SSc-ILD. — [Hoa 2022](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/keab929)

### Inferences
- PFT-based screening misses roughly one-third to two-thirds of HRCT-detectable SSc-ILD, so "subclinical" CT disease is common.
- Prevalence depends on how ILD is defined. Some studies count any interstitial change, others "significant" ILD. Cohorts also differ in referral bias and antibody mix.
- The prevalence of minimal (<5%) changes is not consistently separated from that of clinically meaningful ILD.

### Gaps
- No study found that reports prevalence split by a minimal threshold such as <5% of lung (the Fleischner ILA-like range) vs ≥5% in SSc.
- Prevalence of "indeterminate" or equivocal CT findings in SSc screening cohorts was not found.

## Q2. Progression of mild / limited SSc-ILD: rates and time course

### Takeaway
Most mild or limited SSc-ILD stays stable or progresses slowly, but a meaningful minority progresses. In Oslo, about 7% of patients with 1–20% fibrosis moved to >20% over about 3 years; these were patients with short disease duration, and their fibrosis grew about 5.9% per year. EUSTAR shows 23–27% FVC-defined progression in any 12-month period, with most decliners slow or episodic. Among untreated patients, who were mostly limited, cumulative 3-year progression was about 40%. Most progression happens early in the disease course. Progression is not autocorrelated year to year, which argues for treating on risk rather than waiting for progression.

### Cited Findings
- **Oslo serial HRCT** (Hoffmann-Vold, Arthritis Rheumatol 2015; PMID 25916462; DOI 10.1002/art.39166). Prospective, n=305, mean HRCT interval 3.1 years. Baseline groups: >20% fibrosis (n=40), **1–20% fibrosis (n=157)**, none (n=108).
  - All 108 with no fibrosis were still fibrosis-free at follow-up.
  - Of the 157 with 1–20% fibrosis, **146 (93%) stayed at 1–20%** and **11 (7%) progressed to >20%**. The progressors had a mean disease duration of 1.3 years.
  - Annual fibrosis progression: 0.7%/year in stable limited, **5.9%/year in progressors**, 0.9%/year in >20%, 0% in none.
  - Total FVC decline: 5.7% in stable limited, 8.7% in progressors, 7.1% in >20%, 2.9% in none.
  - Predictors of >20% at follow-up: baseline fibrosis (OR 1.3 per unit) and FVC (OR 0.96). ACA (OR 4.7) and DLCO predicted staying fibrosis-free.
  — [Hoffmann-Vold 2015](https://doi.org/10.1002/art.39166)
- **EUSTAR progressive ILD** (Hoffmann-Vold, Ann Rheum Dis 2021 [epub 2020]; PMID 32988845; DOI 10.1136/annrheumdis-2020-217455). n=826 SSc-ILD.
  - At 12±3 months, 27% progressed: 5–10% FVC decline (moderate) or >10% (significant).
  - Over a mean of 5 years (n=535), **23–27% progressed in each 12-month period**, but few progressed in consecutive periods.
  - 58% of progressors had slow decline with more stable or improving periods than declining ones. Only 8% had rapid, continuous decline. **33% never had an FVC-decline episode.**
  - Predictors: male sex, higher mRSS, reflux/dysphagia.
  — [Hoffmann-Vold 2021 EUSTAR](https://doi.org/10.1136/annrheumdis-2020-217455)
- **Oslo / Zurich / Michigan, "progression does not predict progression"** (Hoffmann-Vold, Lancet Rheumatol 2025; PMID 40381640; DOI 10.1016/S2665-9913(25)00026-8). Prospective, n=231 main cohort plus an enriched cohort of n=121 with <3 years duration.
  - Between visits 1 and 2, progression was 31% by FVC ≥5%, 16% by ≥10%, 17% by PPF and 39% by PF-ILD.
  - Prior progression did **not** raise the risk of next-year progression (FVC ≥5%: OR 0.28; ≥10%: OR 0.57, NS). The same held in the enriched cohort (OR 0.22).
  - FVC ≥5% decline was associated with mortality (HR 1.66).
  — [Hoffmann-Vold 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2665-9913(25)00026-8)
- **Untreated SSc-ILD, two centres** (RMD Open 2024; PMID 38199606; DOI 10.1136/rmdopen-2023-003658). n=386; 74% untreated at baseline.
  - Non-treatment was associated with ACA (OR 6.75), **limited ILD extent (OR 2.39)**, longer disease duration and higher DLCO.
  - Among 234 untreated patients, **3-year cumulative progression was 39.9% (95% CI 32.9–46.2)**. The composite included FVC/DLCO decline, ILD-extent increase or start of treatment.
  - Diffuse SSc and **extensive fibrosis** predicted progression in untreated patients.
  — [RMD Open 2024](https://doi.org/10.1136/rmdopen-2023-003658)
- **SPAR model for mild ILD** (Wu, Ann Rheum Dis 2018; PMID 29875097; DOI 10.1136/annrheumdis-2018-213201). Two prospective cohorts (Zurich derivation n=98; multinational validation n=117) of SSc patients with **mild ILD on HRCT**.
  - **1-year progression in 25/98 (25.5%) and 25/117 (21.4%)**, defined as relative FVC decline ≥15%, or ≥10% plus DLCO ≥15%.
  - Predictors: SpO2 after 6MWT <94% and arthritis ever; AUC 0.83.
  — [Wu 2018](https://doi.org/10.1136/annrheumdis-2018-213201)
- **Goh staging** (Goh, AJRCCM 2008; PMID 18369202; DOI 10.1164/rccm.200706-877OC). n=215. **20% HRCT extent was the optimal mortality threshold.** For an extent of 10–30% ("indeterminate"), FVC 70% is used. Limited vs extensive: HR 3.46 for mortality. — [Goh 2008](https://doi.org/10.1164/rccm.200706-877OC)
- **PPF timing** (Roeser, Respir Res 2025; PMID 41188867; DOI 10.1186/s12931-025-03375-7). Single centre, n=79, 279 HRCTs centrally reviewed.
  - 5-year cumulative PPF was 22.7% using the 2022 1-year-window criteria vs **45.6% with no time limit** (2024 criteria).
  - Only short-term progressors had worse survival; slow progressors did not differ from non-progressors.
  — [Roeser 2025](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12931-025-03375-7)
- **Incident (new-onset) ILD**, which is by definition early and usually mild: 38.8% of 389 incident-ILD patients progressed over a median of 5.8 years (Australian; PMID 40974697). — [Morrisroe 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.semarthrit.2025.152826). In EUSTAR, incident ILD had lower progression risk than prevalent ILD (PMID 41549016). — [EUSTAR 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ard.2025.12.008)
- **SENSCIS placebo** (all patients had ≥10% fibrotic extent, so these are not "minimal" cases): FVC decline −112 mL/year in dcSSc and −74.5 mL/year in lcSSc (Assassi review, PMID 37534955; DOI 10.55563/clinexprheumatol/trcv91). — [Assassi 2023](https://doi.org/10.55563/clinexprheumatol/trcv91). In lcSSc (n=277), placebo decline was −74.5 mL/year vs −49.1 with nintedanib (PMID 37294870; DOI 10.1093/rheumatology/kead280). — [Allanore 2024](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/kead280). A SENSCIS placebo prediction model (n=288) had an AUC of only 0.63 overall, and 0.75 in the early/inflammatory/severe-skin subgroup (PMID 39242112; DOI 10.1136/rmdopen-2024-004240). — [SENSCIS model 2024](https://doi.org/10.1136/rmdopen-2024-004240)
- **focuSSced quantitative-CT subgroup**, which is the most direct trial data on mild extent (Roofeh, Arthritis Rheumatol 2021; PMID 33538094; DOI 10.1002/art.41668). 136/210 (65%) of early dcSSc patients had ILD; 77% of them had >10% QILD. In **placebo, the mild QILD group (>5–10%) lost 10.0 ± 2.6% FVC predicted over 48 weeks (n=11)**, vs −5.7% for moderate (10–20%) and −6.7% for severe (>20%). With tocilizumab the changes were −4.1%, +0.7% and +2.1%. — [Roofeh 2021](https://doi.org/10.1002/art.41668)
- **Early dcSSc open-label extension** (Khanna, AJRCCM 2022; PMID 34851799; DOI 10.1164/rccm.202103-0714OC). 61% had ILD on HRCT. 96-week FVC change was −4.1% (placebo→TCZ) vs −0.6% (continuous TCZ) among completers with ILD. — [Khanna 2022](https://doi.org/10.1164/rccm.202103-0714OC)
- **Small prospective cohort** (Sieiro Santos, Rheumatology 2025; PMID 40600932; DOI 10.1093/rheumatology/keaf361). n=74. ILD prevalence rose from 38% to 51% over 2 years. — [Sieiro Santos 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/keaf361)
- **Early SSc-ILD inception cohort** (Wangkaew, Clin Rheumatol 2020; PMID 32803568; DOI 10.1007/s10067-020-05346-3). n=75. HRCT score at 1 year: 34 worsened (45%), 17 stable, 24 improved. Worsening was associated with shorter disease duration and an increase in oesophageal diameter (OR 1.21). — [Wangkaew 2020](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10067-020-05346-3)
- **Radiographic progression is prognostic** (Volkmann, Chest 2022; PMID 34896093; DOI 10.1016/j.chest.2021.11.033). SLS I/II, n=82 and 90. A QILD increase of ≥2% at 12 or 24 months predicted worse long-term survival (log-rank p ≤0.01). — [Volkmann 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chest.2021.11.033)

### Inferences
- Pooled across sources, about 7% (Oslo, CT extent over 3 years) to about 25% (SPAR, FVC over 1 year) of mild or limited SSc-ILD progresses in a short window. Cumulative progression reaches about 40% at 3 years among untreated patients and about 40–46% at 5 years when progression definitions have no time limit.
- The highest-risk window is early disease (<2–3 years from first non-Raynaud symptom), diffuse skin, anti-Scl-70, high CRP, and male sex.
- In the small focuSSced placebo subgroup, mild-extent ILD in early inflammatory dcSSc declined as fast as or faster than more extensive ILD. "Mild extent" is therefore not low risk when systemic disease is early and active.

### Gaps
- No study found that follows a strictly <5% or <10% visual-extent SSc cohort with CT-based progression endpoints, analogous to ILA follow-up in the general population. The Oslo 1–20% stratum is the closest.
- Most natural-history data define progression by FVC, not CT. CT-extent progression rates in minimal disease are rarely reported.
- Without full texts, the "mild ILD" definitions in SPAR were not verified.

## Q3. CT features predicting progression in limited or early disease

### Takeaway
Baseline extent is the most consistent CT predictor, both of progression to >20% and of mortality. Pattern-level predictors are weaker and less consistent:
- Quantitative ground glass predicted DLCO decline in one small CALIPER study.
- Quantitative reticulation predicted mortality.
- Automated detection of GGO increase predicted later FVC decline better than visual scoring did.
- Irregular reticulation with traction bronchiolectasis signals UIP-like dense fibrosis (Fukuda 2025).
- A new early sign, FANO, often evolved into reticulation.

Other adjuncts: oesophageal dilatation, pleuroparenchymal fibroelastosis (PPFE), radiomics and lung ultrasound. Subpleural sparing as a prognostic sign in SSc was not identified in the retrieved literature.

### Cited Findings
- **Fukuda et al., Jpn J Radiol 2026;44(4):673–684** (epub 18 Dec 2025; PMID 41407994; PMCID PMC13038689; DOI 10.1007/s11604-025-01922-2). Retrospective; 28 SSc-ILD patients with surgical lung biopsy (2008–2018) and 79 biopsy specimens. Whole-lung HRCT was read by 2 radiologists, biopsy sites by 2 more, histology by 3 pathologists, followed by joint correlation.
  - NSIP was the predominant pattern on HRCT (75%) and on pathology (61%).
  - At biopsy sites, GGO was seen in 92% and reticulation in 84%. Reticulation was almost always accompanied by GGO, reflecting diffuse fibrosis.
  - **Irregular reticulation with traction bronchiolectasis = dense fibrosis with severe architectural destruction (UIP-like features)**, proposed as a marker of risk for progression.
  - **New sign, FANO (faint amorphous nodular opacity)**: a small, faint nodular opacity on amorphous GGO within 1 cm of the pleura, in a band-like distribution parallel to the pleura. Seen in 18/28 (64%), mainly in the **anterolateral upper lobes**. Pathologically it corresponded to perivenular fibrosis and peribronchiolar metaplasia, with or without mucostasis. It is proposed as an early-stage CT finding.
  - **Longitudinal follow-up in 14 patients (median 32.5 months): progression in 71%. Half of these showed coalescence of FANO into subpleural curvilinear opacities with reticulation.**
  - The abstract does not use Fleischner ILA terminology.
  — [Fukuda 2025/2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11604-025-01922-2)
- **Baseline extent → progression.** In Oslo, baseline fibrosis (OR 1.3) and FVC predicted reaching >20%. ACA and DLCO predicted remaining fibrosis-free (PMID 25916462). — [Hoffmann-Vold 2015](https://doi.org/10.1002/art.39166). Extensive fibrosis predicted progression in untreated SSc-ILD (PMID 38199606). — [RMD Open 2024](https://doi.org/10.1136/rmdopen-2023-003658)
- **Zonal location** (van Doorn-Hogervorst, Semin Arthritis Rheum 2025; PMID 39914260; DOI 10.1016/j.semarthrit.2025.152650). n=185 with "early ILD"; semi-quantitative 5-level scoring. **Disease extent at the apical level and reticulation at mid-upper levels predicted 1-year change in TLC.** Effects were small (adjusted R² about 0.02). — [van Doorn-Hogervorst 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.semarthrit.2025.152650)
- **Quantitative CT, GGO vs reticulation.**
  - CALIPER (Ferrazza, Clin Rheumatol 2020; PMID 31940114; DOI 10.1007/s10067-020-04938-3), prospective, n=66. Mean extents: normal lung 87.4%, GGO 2.8%, reticular 4%, honeycomb 1%. **GGO ≥4.5 predicted 12-month DLCO worsening >10% (AUC 0.75; RR 6.8).** Reticulation correlated with baseline PFTs. — [Ferrazza 2020](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10067-020-04938-3)
  - CALIPER mortality (Amorim, PLoS One 2024; PMID 39331602; DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0310892), n=71. ILD extent ≥6.32% and **reticular pattern ≥1.41%** were associated with worse survival. Reticular pattern was an independent predictor (Exp 2.70). — [Amorim 2024](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0310892)
  - Automated vs visual (Motta, Rheumatology 2026; PMID 42426553; DOI 10.1093/rheumatology/keag346), n=33. **Only the software detected GGO increase.** An early GGO increase >50 mL (8.2%) predicted later functional progression (sensitivity 81%, specificity 77%). Visual scoring did not. — [Motta 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/keag346)
  - Imbio LTA in the focuSSced data (Zou, ERJ Open Res 2023; PMID 37868144; DOI 10.1183/23120541.00210-2023). Only weak correlations of qCT metrics with FVC slope (|r| 0.25–0.33 in SSc), and marginal incremental value after adjustment. — [Zou 2023](https://doi.org/10.1183/23120541.00210-2023)
  - Radiomics LTA (Rheumatology 2026; PMID 42658665; DOI 10.1093/rheumatology/keag463), n=267. Pulmonary vessel volume predicted mortality (aHR 1.21). Baseline radiomic features were "overall comparable" between progressors and non-progressors (abstract truncated). — [Radiomics 2026](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/keag463)
  - qRISSc radiomics (Schniering, Eur Respir J 2022; PMID 34649979; DOI 10.1183/13993003.04503-2020). Zurich n=90 / Oslo n=66. A 26-feature score predicted progression-free survival beyond clinical parameters. — [Schniering 2022](https://doi.org/10.1183/13993003.04503-2020)
  - Densitometry (Saldana, AnnalsATS 2020; PMID 32191491; DOI 10.1513/AnnalsATS.201910-741OC), n=170, 503 CTs. qCT changes were associated with survival but not independently of PFT change. — [Saldana 2020](https://doi.org/10.1513/AnnalsATS.201910-741OC)
- **Pattern transitions under treatment** (Kim, Ann Rheum Dis 2016; PMID 26757749; DOI 10.1136/annrheumdis-2015-208929). SLS I, n=83, quantitative CT. In the most severe zone QILD fell 3.9% with cyclophosphamide and rose 4.2% with placebo (p=0.01). There were more fibrotic→GGO/normal transitions with cyclophosphamide and the reverse with placebo. — [Kim 2016](https://doi.org/10.1136/annrheumdis-2015-208929)
- **PPFE** (Bonifazi, Eur Respir J 2020; PMID 32299855; DOI 10.1183/13993003.02135-2019). n=359 (UK and Italy). PPFE prevalence 18% (11% extensive). PPFE predicted worse survival independently of ILD severity (HR 1.89). — [Bonifazi 2020](https://doi.org/10.1183/13993003.02135-2019)
- **Oesophageal dilatation on HRCT.** The widest oesophageal diameter was associated with total ILD score cross-sectionally (n=270; PMID 27033049). — [Richardson 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.semarthrit.2016.02.004). An increase in oesophageal diameter predicted HRCT worsening in early SSc-ILD (PMID 32803568). — [Wangkaew 2020](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10067-020-05346-3). It did *not* distinguish FVC decliners over 5 years (n=138; PMID 34266985; DOI 10.3899/jrheum.210533), where anti-Scl-70 drove a 5-year FVC decline of −6.33%. — [Showalter 2021](https://doi.org/10.3899/jrheum.210533)
- **HRCT pattern in CTD-ILD** (meta-analysis of 23 studies, n=2,020; PMID 40943925; DOI 10.3390/jcm14176164). NSIP predominates in SSc. — [Drimus 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/jcm14176164)
- **Lung ultrasound as a screening alternative.**
  - Gargani, Rheumatology 2022 (PMID 34698807; DOI 10.1093/rheumatology/keab801), n=69, same-day HRCT. **>10 B-lines on the whole chest or >1 B-line on the postero-basal chest gave 97% sensitivity even for very early ILD (SLS-I score 1)**, rising to 100% with pleural-line changes. B-lines correlated with SLS score (R=0.754). — [Gargani 2022](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/keab801)
  - Fairchild, Arthritis Care Res 2025 (PMID 40320831; DOI 10.1002/acr.25567), n=95 (SSc and myositis). LUS-ILD-24 sensitivity 92.4–95.5% and specificity 82.8–86.2%; κ 0.92. — [Fairchild 2025](https://doi.org/10.1002/acr.25567)
  - Gutierrez, Tomography 2024 (PMID 38668398; DOI 10.3390/tomography10040041). 133 asymptomatic SSc patients: **59.4% had US signs of ILD** vs 4.8% of controls. Sensitivity 91.2% and specificity 88.6% vs HRCT. Over 48 weeks, 37.9% of US-positive patients showed a change in US score. — [Gutierrez 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/tomography10040041)
  - Mercier, J Clin Med 2024 (PMID 38792426; DOI 10.3390/jcm13102885). **12/30 (40%) SSc patients with normal HRCT and normal PFT had an ultrasound interstitial syndrome**, with slightly lower FVC and TLC. Whether this is a pre-CT stage or a false positive is unknown. — [Mercier 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/jcm13102885)
  - Perazzi, J Autoimmun 2026 (PMID 41764846; DOI 10.1016/j.jaut.2026.103537). n=72. <5 B-lines excluded ILD (100% sensitivity); >15 B-lines gave sensitivity 88.9% and specificity 82.5%. — [Perazzi 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaut.2026.103537)
- **Risk score to target repeat HRCT** (ILD-RISC; Bruni, Rheumatology 2025; PMID 40811030; DOI 10.1093/rheumatology/keaf445). Derivation n=533, validation n=247. AUC 79.1%; a score ≥0.3 gave sensitivity 85.6%. Of 819 patients with negative baseline HRCT, 170 developed ILD. — [Bruni 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/keaf445)
- **Molecular and functional imaging in early SSc-ILD** (small exploratory studies).
  - 18F-FDG PET: higher uptake in dorsobasal lungs and with ≥20% extent (n=15 ILD; PMID 39702262). — [Broens 2024](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13075-024-03447-x)
  - 68Ga-FAPI-46: volume-based uptake was higher in INBUILD progressors (n=22; PMID 41627399). — [Tai 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00259-025-07710-4)
  - Dual-energy CT perfusion: abnormal in 26% of patients with no or mild (≤10%) ILD (n=101; PMID 35881181). — [Dupont 2022](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00330-022-09016-7)
- **Non-CT predictors in mild ILD.** SPAR (SpO2 after 6MWT, arthritis). — [Wu 2018](https://doi.org/10.1136/annrheumdis-2018-213201). Nailfold capillaroscopy disorganisation predicted FVC decline (β −0.75) (PMID 40600932). — [Sieiro Santos 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/keaf361)

### Inferences
- In limited SSc-ILD, CT extent, together with early disease and anti-Scl-70, is the best-validated imaging predictor.
- Pattern-specific signals (GGO burden or increase, reticulation burden, traction bronchiolectasis or UIP-like features, PPFE) come mostly from small single-centre studies and need validation.
- Fukuda's FANO, an anterolateral upper-lobe, juxtapleural (within 1 cm) faint nodular/GGO band, is the only retrieved paper that explicitly describes an "early-stage" CT lesion with radiologic–pathologic correlation and longitudinal evolution. It is anatomically "subpleural" and pathologically fibrotic (perivenular fibrosis). Under Fleischner it would most plausibly map to "subpleural non-fibrotic" ILA on CT appearance. That mapping is my inference and was not done by the authors.
- Lung ultrasound is a highly sensitive rule-out tool, but its specificity for minimal disease and its prognostic value are not established.

### Gaps
- No retrieved SSc study tested **subpleural sparing** (an NSIP hallmark) as a predictor of progression.
- No SSc study was found comparing traction bronchiectasis vs none specifically *within* minimal or limited disease as a progression predictor.
- Fukuda's longitudinal subset was small (n=14) and consisted of biopsied, likely more advanced patients. Its applicability to screening-detected minimal disease is uncertain.

## Q4. Has any SSc study used the Fleischner ILA categories or "ILA" terminology?

### Takeaway
**No.** I found no primary SSc study that applied the Fleischner ILA definition or its subtypes (non-subpleural / subpleural non-fibrotic / subpleural fibrotic) to SSc patients, or that used "ILA" as the label for minimal CT abnormalities in SSc. PubMed returned only 2 records for "interstitial lung abnormalities" AND "systemic sclerosis": a review and an expert survey, neither SSc-specific. Broader searches found ILA discussed in CTD reviews, but the data cited there were from RA, not SSc. The SSc literature instead uses its own terms: "subclinical ILD", "limited/extensive" (Goh), "mild/moderate/severe" by % extent or QILD, and "early ILD".

### Cited Findings
- PubMed query `"interstitial lung abnormalities" systemic sclerosis` returned **2 records only**:
  - A CTD HRCT review that lists "CTD-related interstitial lung abnormalities" as a topic (Curr Probl Diagn Radiol 2021; PMID 34579993; DOI 10.1067/j.cpradiol.2021.07.007). — [Review 2021](https://doi.org/10.1067/j.cpradiol.2021.07.007)
  - An ILA expert-survey consensus in Chest 2021 (PMID 34197782; DOI 10.1016/j.chest.2021.06.035). Experts agreed that honeycombing and traction bronchiectasis/bronchiolectasis indicate potentially progressive ILD. — [Chest 2021 survey](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chest.2021.06.035)
- Broader query (ILA / Fleischner AND scleroderma / SSc / CTD / rheumatic), first 20 records screened: the only abstract mentioning both ILA and SSc was Yoo et al. (Eur J Radiol Open 2022; PMID 35445144; DOI 10.1016/j.ejro.2022.100419). It is a review. It cites ILA in **RA** at about 20–60% prevalence, with progression in 35–45% and subpleural distribution and greater extent as risk factors. It gives no SSc ILA data. — [Yoo 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejro.2022.100419)
- The 2025 ATS clinical statement on ILA (AJRCCM; PMID 40387336) does not mention SSc in its abstract. — [ATS 2025 ILA statement](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40387336/)
- A web search for "interstitial lung abnormalities" "systemic sclerosis" Fleischner subpleural fibrotic returned only the Fleischner position paper and general SSc-ILD background. No SSc ILA cohort turned up. — [Fleischner position paper (CDC stacks copy)](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/104325)
- Fukuda 2025/2026 (the radiologic–pathologic SSc paper above) uses the new term "FANO" and NSIP/UIP-like descriptors, not ILA, at least in the abstract. — [Fukuda](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11604-025-01922-2)
- **Tangential, MCTD not SSc.** The Norwegian MCTD cohort defined lung fibrosis by "reticular pattern changes according to the Fleischner Society CT criteria". This means the Fleischner *glossary*, not the ILA classification (Gunnarsson, Rheumatology 2015; PMID 26320136; DOI 10.1093/rheumatology/kev300). — [Gunnarsson 2015](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/kev300)
- Severity schemes actually used in SSc:
  - Goh limited/extensive: 20% threshold, FVC 70% for indeterminate 10–30% (PMID 18369202). — [Goh 2008](https://doi.org/10.1164/rccm.200706-877OC)
  - QILD mild >5–10%, moderate 10–20%, severe >20% (focuSSced, PMID 33538094). — [Roofeh 2021](https://doi.org/10.1002/art.41668)
  - Australian mild <10%, moderate 10–30%, severe >30% (PMID 31740961; DOI 10.1093/rheumatology/kez532). — [Australian burden 2020](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/kez532)
  - Oslo none / 1–20% / >20% (PMID 25916462). — [Hoffmann-Vold 2015](https://doi.org/10.1002/art.39166)
  - Conceptual framework of subclinical vs clinical vs progressive ILD, tested with 44 experts on 80 profiles (Roofeh, Rheumatology 2023; PMID 36173318; DOI 10.1093/rheumatology/keac557). — [Roofeh 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/keac557)
  - Subclinical vs clinical ILD and low vs high risk, as a practical classification (Khanna, Arthritis Rheumatol 2021; PMID 34313399; DOI 10.1002/art.41933). — [Khanna 2021](https://doi.org/10.1002/art.41933)

### Inferences
- The Fleischner ILA framework is by definition for *incidental* findings in people without suspected ILD. SSc patients are screened precisely because ILD is suspected, which likely explains why SSc researchers have not adopted ILA terminology. The field instead labels any HRCT-detected interstitial change in SSc as SSc-ILD, possibly "subclinical".
- Even so, the SSc data roughly parallel ILA findings:
  - Extent predicts progression and mortality.
  - "Normal-PFT" CT abnormalities still carry excess mortality (Nor-SSc).
  - Fibrotic features (traction bronchiolectasis, reticulation) are linked to worse outcome.
- No one has tested the fibrotic vs non-fibrotic subpleural split in SSc. That is a genuine evidence gap and could be a research opportunity.

### Gaps
- I could not search Embase, conference abstracts (ACR/EULAR/ERS/RSNA) or the Fleischner/ATS full texts. An SSc ILA abstract may exist outside PubMed-indexed full papers.
- I did not check whether the ATS 2025 ILA statement explicitly excludes patients with known CTD (abstract only).

## Q5. Treatment effects on early disease: does treatment confound natural history?

### Takeaway
Yes, substantially. Treatment use has risen sharply: in EUSTAR, immunosuppressive treatment at first evaluation went from 13.6% to 57.4%, while progression rates fell from 21.3% to 12.1%. Trials show that MMF, cyclophosphamide, tocilizumab and nintedanib slow decline, including in mild-extent and early disease. Patients who stay untreated are selected for limited extent and ACA positivity. Yet early immunosuppression did not prevent new ILD in the EUSTAR/Nijmegen analysis. Natural-history estimates from contemporary cohorts therefore mix untreated low-risk patients with treated high-risk ones.

### Cited Findings
- **EUSTAR treatment trends** (Arthritis Rheumatol 2026; PMID 41568546; DOI 10.1002/art.70043). n=1,409. Immunosuppressive treatment at first evaluation rose **13.6% (≤2006) → 57.4% (≥2017)**; MMF rose 7%→57%. **12-month ILD progression fell from 21.3% (2007–2011) to 12.1% (≥2017)**, defined as FVC ≥5% or DLCO ≥10%. — [EUSTAR 2026 treatment](https://doi.org/10.1002/art.70043)
- **Selection into "untreated"** (PMID 38199606). Limited extent (OR 2.39), ACA (OR 6.75), longer disease duration and higher DLCO predicted no treatment. Untreated patients still had 39.9% 3-year progression. — [RMD Open 2024](https://doi.org/10.1136/rmdopen-2023-003658)
- **Early immunosuppression and ILD prevention** (Velauthapillai, Rheumatology 2025; PMID 39037917; DOI 10.1093/rheumatology/keae375). EUSTAR plus Nijmegen, n=1,052 ILD-free at start of immunosuppression. ILD incidence was 46.6% after a mean of 3.6 years. Early (≤3 years) vs late start: **HR 1.13 (0.93–1.38), meaning no preventive effect**. Most patients were on methotrexate. Confounding by indication applies. — [Velauthapillai 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/keae375)
- **Mild QILD in a trial** (focuSSced; PMID 33538094). Tocilizumab −4.1% vs placebo −10.0% FVC predicted in the mild (>5–10%) group over 48 weeks, so treatment benefit was present across extents. — [Roofeh 2021](https://doi.org/10.1002/art.41668)
- **Cyclophosphamide reverses the direction of quantitative-CT change** (SLS I; PMID 26757749). — [Kim 2016](https://doi.org/10.1136/annrheumdis-2015-208929)
- **Nintedanib on background MMF** (SENSCIS; PMID 33412120; DOI 10.1016/S2213-2600(20)30330-1). 48% of patients were on MMF. FVC decline was −40.2 vs −66.5 mL/year with vs without nintedanib in MMF users. — [Highland 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2213-2600(20)30330-1)
- **Trial enrichment vs real life** (EUSTAR; PMID 36244404; DOI 10.1016/j.chest.2022.09.044). Of 2,258 SSc-ILD patients, 67.7% met no major trial criteria. 12-month FVC change was −0.1% overall vs −3.7% in focuSSced-eligible patients. — [Hoffmann-Vold 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chest.2022.09.044)
- **Clinician behaviour** (PMID 34918033). About 52% would treat or consider treating subclinical SSc-ILD. ≥70% would treat if diffuse SSc, anti-Scl-70, duration <18 months, **ground-glass opacities**, desaturation or progression were present. — [Hoa 2022](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/keab929)
- Early immunosuppression or anti-reflux exposure and incident ILD (CSRG). Gastroprotective agents: weighted HR 0.86 (0.52–1.41), NS (PMID 34089970). — [Hurtubise 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rmed.2021.106482). A related CSRG analysis of immunosuppression and incident ILD exists (Chest 2021; PMID 34153341; DOI 10.1016/j.chest.2021.06.014), but its abstract was not available, so no results are reported here. — [Hoa 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chest.2021.06.014)

### Inferences
- Contemporary cohort progression rates probably *underestimate* the untreated natural history of high-risk early disease and *overestimate* the stability of "untreated limited" disease as a general category, because of selection.
- GGO is an explicit treatment trigger for clinicians. GGO-predominant minimal disease is therefore preferentially treated, which further confounds pattern-specific natural history.
- The Oslo 2015 serial-HRCT cohort and SLS-I placebo arm are among the cleanest sources on untreated course. Even Oslo patients may have received treatment; this was not verified from the abstract.

### Gaps
- No RCT has enrolled solely minimal (<10%) SSc-ILD to test whether treatment changes CT progression. The CONQUEST platform trial (early active SSc-ILD; PMID 39544897) is ongoing. — [CONQUEST design](https://doi.org/10.1177/23971983241278079)
- Treatment status within the Oslo 2015 and SPAR mild-ILD cohorts was not available from abstracts.
