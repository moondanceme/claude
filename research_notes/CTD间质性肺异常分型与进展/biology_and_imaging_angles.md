# Biology and imaging angles: general-population ILA vs early CTD lung involvement

All sources were retrieved from PubMed in this session (date of search: 2026-10-28). Each citation gives PMID and DOI. Abstract-level reading only (no full texts were fetched), so statements about specific figures, tables or discussion sections are limited to what the abstracts say.

## Q1. Genetic and biological contrasts (MUC5B, telomeres, immune mechanisms, reversibility): can findings from ILA in the general population be applied to CTD?

### Takeaway
The MUC5B promoter variant links general-population ILA and RA-ILD, especially the UIP pattern, but large meta-analyses show it has no association with SSc-ILD. Short telomeres are found in both RA-ILD and SSc-ILD, yet in CTD they look more like markers of severity or "hyper-aging" than of susceptibility. Under immunosuppression, quantitative CT in SSc-ILD shows that some "fibrotic" voxels change back to ground glass or normal lung. So the general-population idea that "subpleural fibrotic = irreversible, progressive" can be applied partly to RA (UIP-like, MUC5B-driven) and poorly to SSc, myositis or other NSIP/OP-dominant CTD.

### Cited Findings
**General-population ILA: genetics and natural history**
- In Framingham (n=2633 volumetric CTs), 7% had ILA. Each copy of the MUC5B rs35705950 minor allele raised the odds of ILA (OR 2.8, 95% CI 2.0–3.9) and of definite CT fibrosis (OR 6.3). Smoking did not modify the association, which was stronger above age 50. — Hunninghake 2013, NEJM, PMID 23692170, [DOI 10.1056/NEJMoa1216076](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1216076)
- In MESA (n≈4500), the MUC5B T allele was associated with a +2.60% absolute rise in high-attenuation areas (HAA, −600 to −250 HU) per 10 years. The effect was stronger when telomeres were short (<5th age-adjusted percentile; interaction p=0.008). Each 1%/year rise in HAA carried RR 1.07 for death and RR 1.34 for ILD. — Kim 2022, Thorax, PMID 36690926, [DOI 10.1136/thorax-2021-218139](https://doi.org/10.1136/thorax-2021-218139)
- Fleischner position paper: ILA is present in 4–9% of smokers and 2–7% of non-smokers. It is associated with radiological progression, mortality and complications of interventions. The subpleural fibrotic subtype is singled out as the one more likely to progress and to be associated with mortality. — Hatabu 2020, Lancet Respir Med, PMID 32649920, [DOI 10.1016/S2213-2600(20)30168-5](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2213-2600(20)30168-5)
- Long-term screening cohort (median follow-up 11.3 y): 80.5% of ILA progressed (median 3.2 y) and 17.3% progressed to UIP (median 11.8 y). Fibrosis extent on deep-learning quantification was an independent risk factor. — Park 2023, AJRCCM, PMID 37590877, [DOI 10.1164/rccm.202303-0410OC](https://doi.org/10.1164/rccm.202303-0410OC)

**RA-ILD: shares the MUC5B/UIP biology**
- MUC5B rs35705950 was associated with RA-ILD versus controls (combined adjusted OR 4.7) and with ILD within RA (OR 3.1), particularly with a UIP pattern. — Juge 2018, NEJM, PMID 30345907, [DOI 10.1056/NEJMoa1801562](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1801562)
- European replication: MUC5B combined adjusted OR 2.9 for RA-ILD (883 RA patients). The Japanese GWAS hit RPA3-UMAD1 rs12702634 did not replicate. — Juge 2024, Rheumatol Adv Pract, PMID 38854416, [DOI 10.1093/rap/rkae059](https://doi.org/10.1093/rap/rkae059)
- In Asian RA the variant is rare (MAF 0.37% in Korean RA) but associated specifically with UIP (meta-OR 3.51), not with RA-ILD overall. — Joo 2022, RMD Open, PMID 36581384, [DOI 10.1136/rmdopen-2022-002790](https://doi.org/10.1136/rmdopen-2022-002790). In Japanese RA, OR 10.66 for UIP. — Higuchi 2025, Rheumatology, PMID 39110542, [DOI 10.1093/rheumatology/keae422](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/keae422)
- MUC5B status did **not** predict survival in RA-ILD (VARA, n=263; aHR 0.97), including when stratified by UIP vs non-UIP. — Klein 2025, Rheumatology, PMID 39504460, [DOI 10.1093/rheumatology/keae615](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/keae615)
- Air pollution (NO2, PM2.5, PM10) was independently associated with RA-ILD. Its effect was attenuated in carriers of the MUC5B T allele, which suggests that environmental and genetic routes are partly different. — Sesé 2026, Respirology, PMID 41486088, [DOI 10.1002/resp.70181](https://doi.org/10.1002/resp.70181)
- Population bridge: in MESA/COPDGene, RA-risk HLA alleles were not associated with ILA. Higher serum IgA-RF and anti-CCP were associated with ILA (OR ~1.2), and IgM-RF predicted incident ILA among smokers. These associations did not differ by MUC5B status. — Kim 2025, Ann Am Thorac Soc, PMID 39405163, [DOI 10.1513/AnnalsATS.202403-238OC](https://doi.org/10.1513/AnnalsATS.202403-238OC)
- Telomeres in RA-ILD: 43% of 101 RA-ILD patients had short leukocyte telomere length (<10th percentile), which was associated with lower FVC/DLCO and faster 12-month decline. — El Husseini 2025, Eur Respir J, PMID 40967766, [DOI 10.1183/13993003.00587-2025](https://doi.org/10.1183/13993003.00587-2025). In Japanese RA, telomeres were *longer* in RA with airway disease. — Higuchi 2025, BMC Rheumatol, PMID 41402949, [DOI 10.1186/s41927-025-00605-9](https://doi.org/10.1186/s41927-025-00605-9)

**SSc-ILD: different genetic architecture**
- In the largest meta-analysis (10 European cohorts; 2363 SSc-ILD, 3526 SSc without ILD, 15,076 controls), MUC5B rs35705950 was **not** associated with SSc-ILD (SSc-ILD vs SSc-noILD OR 1.05, p=0.588), and neither were 903 nearby variants. The authors conclude that the pathogenic mechanisms "may only partially overlap" with IPF or RA-ILD. — Rosa-Baez 2025, RMD Open, PMID 40803819, [DOI 10.1136/rmdopen-2025-005754](https://doi.org/10.1136/rmdopen-2025-005754)
- Review: HLA-DRB1*11 is linked to ILD in European SSc, whereas MUC5B is linked to IIP. — Furukawa 2015, PMID 26056507, [DOI 10.4137/CCRPM.S23283](https://doi.org/10.4137/CCRPM.S23283)
- Short telomeres in SSc are associated with the presence and severity of ILD and PH and with worse event-free survival, not with skin disease. — Yang 2026, Arthritis Rheumatol, PMID 40964771, [DOI 10.1002/art.43400](https://doi.org/10.1002/art.43400). Review describing SSc as "hyper-aging": telomere attrition, senescence, cGAS-STING/type-I IFN, and accelerated epigenetic clocks especially in SSc-ILD. — Waseem 2026, Curr Opin Rheumatol, PMID 42559700, [DOI 10.1097/BOR.0000000000001181](https://doi.org/10.1097/BOR.0000000000001181)
- SSc-ILD pathogenesis involves the inflammasome, NETosis and the IL-17/23 axis, and immunosuppression (CYC, MMF, rituximab, aHSCT) halts progression. The review notes that "inflammation in SSc is mostly subclinical". — Ahmed 2018, Mediterr J Rheumatol, PMID 32185315, [DOI 10.31138/mjr.29.3.140](https://doi.org/10.31138/mjr.29.3.140)

**Reversibility with treatment: does "fibrotic" on CT carry the same meaning?**
- Scleroderma Lung Study I, computer-aided quantitative CT: quantitative ILD fell by 3.2% (whole lung) on cyclophosphamide and rose by 2.2% on placebo (p=0.03). Transition analysis showed **more voxels changing from a fibrotic pattern to ground glass or normal** on cyclophosphamide, and the reverse on placebo. — Kim 2016, Ann Rheum Dis, PMID 26757749, [DOI 10.1136/annrheumdis-2015-208929](https://doi.org/10.1136/annrheumdis-2015-208929)
- Review of CTD-ILD and ILA: in RA, ILA occurs in ~20–60% and CT progression in ~35–45%. Subpleural distribution and higher baseline extent predict progression. CTD histology is diverse (NSIP, UIP, OP, apical fibrosis, DAD, LIP), with NSIP predominant in SSc, DM/PM and MCTD. — Yoo 2020, Eur J Radiol Open, PMID 33364263, [DOI 10.1016/j.ejro.2020.100311](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejro.2020.100311)
- HRCT pattern by CTD (n=99): NSIP was the most common pattern overall. UIP was most frequent in RA, OP in PM/DM, and LIP only in pSS. — Li 2025, BMC Med Imaging, PMID 40197179, [DOI 10.1186/s12880-025-01655-8](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12880-025-01655-8)
- Lung MRI in SSc-ILD over 6 months of treatment: in 2 progressing patients, MRI "inflammation decreased while fibrotic features increased", so inflammatory and fibrotic compartments moved in different directions. — Di Battista 2026, Clin Exp Rheumatol, PMID 42370521, [DOI 10.55563/clinexprheumatol/utoe2k](https://doi.org/10.55563/clinexprheumatol/utoe2k)

### Inferences
- What carries over from general-population ILA depends on the disease. **RA** (older, more often male and smoking, MUC5B-enriched, UIP-prone) is the CTD where ILA rules (subpleural fibrotic = higher risk) are most plausibly transferable. In **SSc, IIM, MCTD and pSS**, the null MUC5B result and the NSIP/OP predominance argue against transferring ILA risk stratification wholesale.
- In ILA, "subpleural fibrotic" (reticulation ± traction bronchiectasis) is used as a proxy for an irreversible, UIP-trajectory process. In CTD, fine reticulation within NSIP can coexist with active inflammation, and the SLS-I voxel transitions show that some CT "fibrosis" regresses under immunosuppression. The same CT label may therefore carry different biology and prognosis in the two settings.
- Telomere shortening appears in both settings. In SSc it tracks severity (ILD + PH) rather than an IPF-like susceptibility signal, so it could serve as a stratifier in CTD imaging studies but not as an ILA-equivalent risk factor.

### Gaps
- No retrieved study genotyped MUC5B against *ILA-type minimal CT abnormalities* within SSc, IIM or pSS cohorts. Whether MUC5B modifies the UIP-like subset of SSc-ILD is unresolved.
- No retrieved data on reversibility of minimal (<5% extent) CTD abnormalities specifically. The SLS-I data come from established SSc-ILD.
- MUC5B data in IIM (anti-synthetase, MDA5) and pSS-ILD were not retrieved.

## Q2. Imaging features of early CTD-ILD (NSIP, subpleural sparing, GGO vs reticulation, dependent opacities, prone imaging, expiratory CT, pSS cysts)

### Takeaway
Early CTD lung involvement is characterised by more than subpleural reticulation. It includes basal GGO/NSIP (often dorsobasal), OP, small-airway disease shown as air trapping on expiratory CT (RA, pSS, ACPA-positive), and cysts/LIP in pSS. Air trapping is a discriminator: it was seen in 75% of CTD-ILD versus 12% of IPF. Prone imaging matters because the nonfibrotic GGO component of minimal abnormalities is the least reproducible between positions.

### Cited Findings
- Expiratory HRCT: air trapping in 27/36 (75%) of CTD-ILD (17 of these RA) versus 4/34 (12%) of IPF (p<0.0001). Centrilobular nodules were more common in CTD-ILD, while interlobular thickening and traction bronchiectasis were more common in IPF. Air-trapping extent correlated with closing volume/VC and closing capacity/TLC. — Tokura 2014, Acta Radiol, PMID 24252818, [DOI 10.1177/0284185113512300](https://doi.org/10.1177/0284185113512300)
- RA (n=83): ACPA-positive patients had more centrilobular nodules and air trapping. Air trapping and bronchial wall thickening correlated with ACPA titre "regardless of physiologic airflow limitation", which implies airway disease attributable to RA autoimmunity independent of ILD. — Park 2016, Lung, PMID 26608346, [DOI 10.1007/s00408-015-9828-1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00408-015-9828-1)
- RA (n=45): the mean expiratory air-trapping score was 21%. Mosaic attenuation was associated with lower FEF25–75 and PEF, and air trapping was proposed for detecting early airway obstruction "even in patients with infiltrative lung disease only". — Chung 2004, Yonsei Med J, PMID 15227731, [DOI 10.3349/ymj.2004.45.3.443](https://doi.org/10.3349/ymj.2004.45.3.443)
- Review: bronchiolocentric fibrosis and constrictive bronchiolitis show air trapping and mosaic attenuation on expiratory HRCT, and CTD (especially RA) is a recognised cause. — Cordier 2007, Thorax, PMID 17600295, [DOI 10.1136/thx.2004.031005](https://doi.org/10.1136/thx.2004.031005)
- Sjögren disease (n=83 symptomatic or PFT-abnormal): 31.3% had ILD on HRCT. SjD-ILD was associated with higher ESSDAI, lymphoma history and focus score. The study explores a link between ILD and small-airway dysfunction (FEF25–75, oscillometry R5–R20). — Goules 2026, J Autoimmun, PMID 41980284, [DOI 10.1016/j.jaut.2026.103556](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaut.2026.103556)
- Reduced FEF50 occurred in 17–22% of RA, SSc and MCTD patients, mostly without obstructive ventilatory disorder (a small-airway signal). — Frappa 2026, Respir Med Res, PMID 42849321, [DOI 10.1016/j.resmer.2026.101327](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resmer.2026.101327)
- pSS cystic disease and LIP: a case of diffuse thin-walled cysts with LIP on biopsy as the first manifestation of pSS. — Rai 2026, BMJ Case Rep, PMID 42320947, [DOI 10.1136/bcr-2026-271994](https://doi.org/10.1136/bcr-2026-271994). The differential of cysts in SS includes nodular amyloidosis with calcified nodules (Jin 2025, PMID 40999413, [DOI 10.1186/s12890-025-03876-7](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12890-025-03876-7)) and light-chain deposition disease (Pendyala 2026, PMID 41668818, [DOI 10.12890/2026_006086](https://doi.org/10.12890/2026_006086)). These are case-level evidence only.
- LIP was seen only in pSS in a comparative CTD-ILD CT series, and OP mainly in PM/DM. — Li 2025, PMID 40197179, [DOI 10.1186/s12880-025-01655-8](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12880-025-01655-8)
- FDG uptake in early severe dcSSc was mainly increased in the **dorsobasal** lung fields, and GGO with or without reticulation corresponded to increased uptake. — Broens 2024, Arthritis Res Ther, PMID 39702262, [DOI 10.1186/s13075-024-03447-x](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13075-024-03447-x)
- **Prone vs supine (ILA, quantitative):** in 38 same-day supine and prone CTs analysed with deep-learning qCT, total ILA extent was 1.76% supine versus 1.39% prone (p=0.02). Agreement was moderate for the fibrotic component (CCC 0.770) and **poor for the nonfibrotic/GGO component (CCC 0.431)**, with the lowest reproducibility in **dependent lung zones**. The 95% limits of agreement for total extent were −1.49% to +2.23%. — Ahn 2026, Eur Radiol, PMID 42091660, [DOI 10.1007/s00330-026-12595-4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00330-026-12595-4)
- Prone HRCT is described as "essential for early diagnosis of ILD" but limited by radiation. Low-dose prone CT with deep-learning reconstruction cut dose by 63.7% (1.22 mSv) with comparable overall image quality, though bronchiolectasis visualisation was weaker with the strong bone kernel. — Zhao 2025, BMC Med Imaging, PMID 40830838, [DOI 10.1186/s12880-025-01871-2](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12880-025-01871-2)
- In RA, subpleural distribution and higher baseline extent predict ILA progression. — Yoo 2020, PMID 33364263, [DOI 10.1016/j.ejro.2020.100311](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejro.2020.100311)

### Inferences
- The main early CTD findings (dorsobasal GGO/NSIP, posterior subpleural changes) sit exactly where dependent atelectasis occurs, and that is where supine/prone quantitative reproducibility is worst (Ahn 2026). This makes prone acquisition, or at least prone confirmation, a methodological requirement for any minimal-CTD-ILD study, more so than for fibrotic ILA in smokers.
- An imaging definition of "early CTD lung involvement" should probably be broader than the Fleischner ILA definition. It would need an **airway axis** (expiratory air trapping, centrilobular nodules, bronchial wall thickening: RA/ACPA, pSS) and a **cystic/lymphoid axis** (pSS), neither of which the ILA framework captures. This is inferred from the pattern spectrum in Tokura, Park, Li and Goules.

### Gaps
- No retrieved study directly quantified the frequency of "subpleural sparing" in early (minimal) CTD-ILD. The feature is well known from NSIP literature but no PubMed abstract was retrieved to cite it here.
- No retrieved CTD-specific (SSc/RA) prone-vs-supine study. Ahn 2026 is in ILA, not CTD.
- No quantitative expiratory-CT (parametric response mapping) study in early RA/pSS was retrieved.

## Q3. Quantitative/AI CT, LUS, MRI (UTE/functional) and PET for detecting and tracking minimal CTD-ILD; reproducibility at minimal extent

### Takeaway
Deep-learning qCT already separates early RA from healthy controls at very low extents (median 3.7% vs 1.6% interstitial abnormality). However, at around 1–2% extent, the variability from positioning alone (about ±1.9%) is the same size as the signal. LUS is very sensitive with a high negative predictive value for SSc-ILD but samples only subpleural lung. UTE and other MRI sequences detect SSc-ILD with ~93% sensitivity but poor inter-reader kappa (0.26). FDG-PET (Broens 2024) shows activity in dorsobasal GGO, which could separate active from inactive minimal disease, but the evidence comes from n=20 SSc patients.

### Cited Findings
**Quantitative and AI CT**
- SAIL-RA (preprint, medRxiv): 200 early RA patients (median RA duration 8.3 months) versus 104 non-smoking COPDGene controls. Deep-learning QCT found 3.7% (IQR 2.1–6.1) interstitial abnormality versus 1.6% (0.8–2.4), p<0.0001, and early RA had 9.3% less normal lung after adjustment for age and sex. **Not peer-reviewed.** — McDermott 2026, medRxiv, PMID 41757183, [DOI 10.64898/2026.02.20.26346723](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.02.20.26346723)
- SLS-I computer-aided qCT detected treatment response and pattern transitions (see Q1). — Kim 2016, PMID 26757749, [DOI 10.1136/annrheumdis-2015-208929](https://doi.org/10.1136/annrheumdis-2015-208929)
- MESA HAA densitometry (−600 to −250 HU) captured longitudinal interstitial change linked to MUC5B, death and ILD. — Kim 2022, PMID 36690926, [DOI 10.1136/thorax-2021-218139](https://doi.org/10.1136/thorax-2021-218139)
- CALIPER-based lung texture analysis in SSc (n=186): ILD presence AUC 0.846 on low-dose photon-counting CT versus 0.772 on energy-integrating CT. For extensive ILD (>20%), EID-CT did better (0.978 vs 0.842), so detector technology affects quantitative output. — Happe 2025, Eur J Radiol, PMID 41401537, [DOI 10.1016/j.ejrad.2025.112605](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejrad.2025.112605)
- CALIPER (GGO, reticulation, honeycombing, pulmonary vascular volume) predicted 5-year mortality in SSc (n=71). — Amorim 2024, PLoS One, PMID 39331602, [DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0310892](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0310892)
- Review of SARD-ILD imaging, from visual assessment to HAA, QLF, CALIPER and data-driven AI: "operational thresholds for HRCT-defined progression remain ill defined", and guidelines "lack standardized, reproducible assessment protocols". — Chang 2026, J Rheum Dis, PMID 42516722, [DOI 10.4078/jrd.2025.0161](https://doi.org/10.4078/jrd.2025.0161)
- Italian lung cancer screening (n=500, CTDI <2 mGy): ILA prevalence 6.8%. Inter-radiologist agreement was almost perfect and visual–DL concordance substantial, but **visual assessment gave higher extent than software**. — Cozzi 2026, J Clin Med, PMID 42122926, [DOI 10.3390/jcm15093193](https://doi.org/10.3390/jcm15093193)
- Asian ILA review: quantitative CT thresholds of 5% in at least one zone (sensitivity 67.6%, specificity 93.3%) and 1.8% area (reported 100%/99% in Korea). Standardised protocols are still required. — Jin 2023, Tuberc Respir Dis, PMID 38111097, [DOI 10.4046/trd.2023.0117](https://doi.org/10.4046/trd.2023.0117)
- Positional reproducibility of minimal ILA qCT: about 1.9% measurement variability, with GGO CCC 0.431 (see Q2). — Ahn 2026, PMID 42091660, [DOI 10.1007/s00330-026-12595-4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00330-026-12595-4)
- Chest-radiograph DL plus serum SP-D/KL-6 screening for fibrosis in a health-check population. — Nishikiori 2025, BMC Pulm Med, PMID 41408239, [DOI 10.1186/s12890-025-04062-5](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12890-025-04062-5)

**Lung ultrasound**
- SSc, two centres (n=108; ILD defined as ≥10% on HRCT): ≥10 B-lines gave sensitivity 90.5% and specificity 84.4%. Fairchild pleural criteria gave 93.2%/93.8% (AUC 0.935). The OR rule reached sensitivity 95.9% and NPV 89.7%. — Mohammad Reza Beigi 2026, Clin Rheumatol, PMID 42240790, [DOI 10.1007/s10067-026-08191-y](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10067-026-08191-y)
- In SSc (n=82), LUS B-lines and the pleural-line irregularity score correlated with automated qCT ILD, GGO and reticulation volumes in both **surface and core** lung. This addresses the "superficial-only" limitation. — Mohammad Reza Beigi 2025, Rheumatology, PMID 40504021, [DOI 10.1093/rheumatology/keaf328](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/keaf328)
- Handheld point-of-care LUNA score in systemic autoimmune disease (n=88): AUC 0.968, kappa 0.810, sensitivity 97.2% and specificity 92.3% at ≥38. — Fumanal Idocin 2026, Med Clin (Barc), PMID 41903274, [DOI 10.1016/j.medcli.2026.107415](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.medcli.2026.107415)
- Fewer than 5 B-lines excluded SSc-ILD with 100% sensitivity, and more than 15 B-lines gave sensitivity 88.9% and specificity 82.5%. — Perazzi 2026, J Autoimmun, PMID 41764846, [DOI 10.1016/j.jaut.2026.103537](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaut.2026.103537)
- In SARD-ILD (n=195), B-lines matched GGO (κ=0.606) and pleural-line abnormalities matched reticulation (κ=0.743). A modified LUS score correlated with Warrick score and ILD-GAP. — Chen 2026, Rheumatology, PMID 40973473, [DOI 10.1093/rheumatology/keaf501](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/keaf501)
- In CTD-ILD (n=117; SS 32, SSc 18, RA 16, IIM…), the LUS-B score correlated with HRCT severity. — Du 2026, Sci Rep, PMID 41580474, [DOI 10.1038/s41598-026-36874-8](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-36874-8)
- In RA/SSc (n=71), 56% had <5% HRCT extent. B-lines correlated with extent at Rho 0.58–0.61. — Cajas Santana 2025, Arch Rheumatol, PMID 40964926, [DOI 10.5152/ArchRheumatol.2025.10999](https://doi.org/10.5152/ArchRheumatol.2025.10999)
- B-lines track nintedanib treatment (175→121 over 12 months, p=0.005), changes with rehabilitation, and nailfold capillary loss. — Di Battista 2024, Rheumatology, PMID 38048612, [DOI 10.1093/rheumatology/kead642](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/kead642); de Alegria 2025, PMID 40407619, [DOI 10.3390/arm93030009](https://doi.org/10.3390/arm93030009); Elziaty 2025, PMID 40008078, [DOI 10.1177/11795441251318050](https://doi.org/10.1177/11795441251318050)

**MRI**
- Respiratory-triggered UTE spiral VIBE at 1.5 T in SSc (n=51): sensitivity 92.8% and specificity 75.0% for ILD. **Intra/inter-reader kappa for ILD presence was only 0.56/0.26**, but extent CCC was high (0.92/0.89). Three of 54 scans were discarded for quality. — Landini 2022, J Thorac Imaging, PMID 35482025, [DOI 10.1097/RTI.0000000000000637](https://doi.org/10.1097/RTI.0000000000000637)
- MRI scores predicted SSc-ILD progression with AUC 0.86 versus CT 0.83 and correlated with FVC. — Hochhegger 2022, Rheumatology, PMID 35258556, [DOI 10.1093/rheumatology/keac148](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/keac148)
- Multimodal follow-up (T2, T1 post-contrast, T2*), n=14: 6-month MRI and HRCT agreed in only 64.3% (κ=0.25), yet the **6-month MRI matched 2-year HRCT outcome in 13/14**. T1 contrast correlated with LUS pleural irregularity and DLCO. — Di Battista 2026, PMID 42370521, [DOI 10.55563/clinexprheumatol/utoe2k](https://doi.org/10.55563/clinexprheumatol/utoe2k)
- Reviews: MRI "can detect the presence of SSc-ILD with high accuracy", gives functional information, and can be paired with molecular probes. — Montesi 2019, Curr Rheumatol Rep, PMID 31025121, [DOI 10.1007/s11926-019-0826-9](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11926-019-0826-9). More than 60% of SSc patients with ILD on HRCT had normal PFTs, so PFT screening performs poorly, and US/MRI were "promising but further work is needed". — Molberg 2016, Curr Opin Rheumatol, PMID 27387267, [DOI 10.1097/BOR.0000000000000323](https://doi.org/10.1097/BOR.0000000000000323)

**FDG-PET and molecular imaging**
- Broens 2024 (prospective; 15 SSc-ILD, 5 SSc without ILD, 7 controls): FDG uptake was increased in dorsobasal fields in SSc-ILD (p=0.03 vs SSc-noILD; p<0.001 vs controls), higher with ≥20% extent (p=0.04), and correlated with DLCO% (R=−0.59). GGO ± reticulation corresponded to uptake. — PMID 39702262, [DOI 10.1186/s13075-024-03447-x](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13075-024-03447-x)
- Tissue correlate: GLUT1- and FAPα-positive cells were most prevalent in areas with **early fibrosis**, with GLUT3 in myeloid cells. This suggests FDG and 68Ga-FAPI PET show different aspects of activity. — Broens 2025, Respir Res, PMID 41044547, [DOI 10.1186/s12931-025-03363-x](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12931-025-03363-x)
- Rationale review for PET in aHSCT-eligible dcSSc: selecting rapid progressors, predicting response and understanding mechanism. — Broens 2022, Front Immunol, PMID 35865521, [DOI 10.3389/fimmu.2022.923869](https://doi.org/10.3389/fimmu.2022.923869)

### Inferences
- **Reproducibility at minimal extent is the binding constraint.** With ILA extent around 1.4–1.8% and supine/prone limits of agreement of about ±2%, and visual readers systematically giving higher extent than software, any longitudinal "progression" claim at the ILA scale needs repeat-scan or positional variability estimates, standardised dose/kernel/detector, and fixed software versions (Happe 2025 shows detector dependence).
- LUS is the best-validated radiation-free *screen* (high NPV). Its weakness is minimal and deep disease, though Beigi 2025 shows some correlation with core lung. MRI (UTE) is attractive for *characterisation*, separating inflammation (T2/T1-contrast) from fibrosis, which is exactly the CTD-specific question, but reader agreement for presence is poor.
- FDG-PET adds a dimension that ILA research lacks: *activity*. Combined with CT voxel-level GGO-to-fibrosis transitions, it could test whether minimal CTD abnormalities are "active-reversible" or "fibrotic-fixed".

### Gaps
- No retrieved deep-learning ILA detector validated in SSc, IIM or pSS early cohorts. SAIL-RA (RA only) is a preprint.
- No retrieved functional MRI (hyperpolarised gas, oxygen-enhanced, Fourier decomposition) studies in early CTD.
- No test–retest (same-day rescan) qCT reproducibility data specific to CTD were retrieved.
- FDG-PET evidence in early CTD is small and SSc-specific. No retrieved PET data in RA-ILA or minimal disease below the HRCT threshold.

## Q4. Reader agreement on minimal abnormalities and the role of prone imaging

### Takeaway
Agreement on minimal or borderline interstitial findings is moderate at best. Clinical reports of "ILA" were downgraded about one-third of the time on research reads, and UTE-MRI inter-reader kappa for presence was 0.26. Prone imaging reduces dependent-zone confounding, but quantitative GGO measurements remain unstable between positions.

### Cited Findings
- At a tertiary centre, of 125 clinically reported "ILA" CTs, only 62% were confirmed as ILA on research reads, 7% were equivocal and **31% had no ILA**. Most cases were very mild (1–25% of lung), and non-thoracic radiologists essentially did not use the term. — Escalon 2023, Clin Imaging, PMID 37862912, [DOI 10.1016/j.clinimag.2023.109996](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clinimag.2023.109996)
- Distinguishing post-COVID residual abnormalities from ILA (15 international radiologists): median AUC 0.85 (range 0.67–0.92), **κ=0.56 (moderate)**. — Lee 2024, Eur Radiol, PMID 39311916, [DOI 10.1007/s00330-024-11075-x](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00330-024-11075-x)
- Reticular opacity on chest radiographs: inter-reader κ=0.517 for readers alone (improved with DL assistance). — Kim 2021, AJR, PMID 34668769, [DOI 10.2214/AJR.21.26682](https://doi.org/10.2214/AJR.21.26682)
- Cozzi 2026 reported almost-perfect inter-radiologist agreement for ILA presence in LCS, but visual extent was systematically higher than DL extent. — PMID 42122926, [DOI 10.3390/jcm15093193](https://doi.org/10.3390/jcm15093193)
- UTE-MRI inter-reader κ=0.26 for SSc-ILD presence. — Landini 2022, PMID 35482025, [DOI 10.1097/RTI.0000000000000637](https://doi.org/10.1097/RTI.0000000000000637)
- Prone versus supine qCT: the nonfibrotic component had poor positional agreement (CCC 0.431) and the dependent zones were least reproducible. — Ahn 2026, PMID 42091660, [DOI 10.1007/s00330-026-12595-4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00330-026-12595-4). Low-dose prone CT with DLR is feasible at about 1.2 mSv. — Zhao 2025, PMID 40830838, [DOI 10.1186/s12880-025-01871-2](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12880-025-01871-2)

### Inferences
- In CTD, where the earliest findings are posterior basal GGO, reader disagreement and positional artefact work together. A study of early CTD lung involvement should use mandatory prone acquisition (low dose plus DLR), multi-reader adjudication with explicit "indeterminate/dependent" categories, and should report kappa separately for GGO and reticulation.

### Gaps
- No retrieved study reported inter-reader agreement specifically for minimal CTD-ILD (<5%) or for applying Fleischner ILA categories to CTD cohorts.
- Fleischner's own handling of dependent opacities and prone imaging was not verifiable from the abstract (full text not fetched).

## Q5. Open imaging questions explicitly called for by authors (research agendas, "future studies should…")

### Takeaway
Authors repeatedly call for: (1) standardised, reproducible quantitative thresholds for progression in SARD-ILD; (2) validation of radiation-free LUS and MRI for early detection; (3) PET to capture disease activity and predict treatment response; (4) work on the genetic architecture of SSc-ILD beyond MUC5B; and (5) prognostic factors beyond MUC5B in RA-ILD. The most promising intersection for a research question is **multimodal, activity-aware phenotyping of minimal CTD abnormalities**. That means characterising, beyond counting, whether ILA-scale findings in CTD are inflammatory-reversible or fibrotic-progressive, using prone low-dose qCT with voxel-transition analysis anchored to LUS/MRI/PET activity.

### Cited Findings
- "Operational thresholds for HRCT-defined progression remain ill defined"; guidelines "lack standardized, reproducible assessment protocols". The review argues that quantitative evaluation is "pivotal". — Chang 2026, PMID 42516722, [DOI 10.4078/jrd.2025.0161](https://doi.org/10.4078/jrd.2025.0161)
- There is no consensus on HRCT screening frequency or intervals in SSc patients at risk. — Khanna 2022, J Scleroderma Relat Disord, PMID 36211204, [DOI 10.1177/23971983211064463](https://doi.org/10.1177/23971983211064463)
- The Fleischner position paper explicitly addresses "the role of quantitative evaluation; and future research needs". — Hatabu 2020, PMID 32649920, [DOI 10.1016/S2213-2600(20)30168-5](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2213-2600(20)30168-5)
- "The role of quantitative CT as a screening tool for ILA requires further validation and standardized imaging protocols." — Jin 2023, PMID 38111097, [DOI 10.4046/trd.2023.0117](https://doi.org/10.4046/trd.2023.0117)
- Ultrasound and MRI "showed promise as tools for early ILD detection; but further work is needed". — Molberg 2016, PMID 27387267, [DOI 10.1097/BOR.0000000000000323](https://doi.org/10.1097/BOR.0000000000000323)
- MRI and ultrasound techniques "hold promise for translation into clinical care and clinical trials". — Montesi 2019, PMID 31025121, [DOI 10.1007/s11926-019-0826-9](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11926-019-0826-9)
- FDG-PET suggests "potential utility … in the early detection of ILD (progression) and aiding in risk stratification". — Broens 2024, PMID 39702262, [DOI 10.1186/s13075-024-03447-x](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13075-024-03447-x). The authors also "provide several suggestions for future research" on PET tracers and aHSCT mechanisms. — Broens 2022, PMID 35865521, [DOI 10.3389/fimmu.2022.923869](https://doi.org/10.3389/fimmu.2022.923869). FDG and FAPI may visualise "different aspects of ILD activity". — Broens 2025, PMID 41044547, [DOI 10.1186/s12931-025-03363-x](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12931-025-03363-x)
- SSc-ILD genetics: "highlights the need for further studies regarding their genetic architecture". — Rosa-Baez 2025, PMID 40803819, [DOI 10.1136/rmdopen-2025-005754](https://doi.org/10.1136/rmdopen-2025-005754)
- RA-ILD: "Further studies are needed to identify other genetic and non-genetic prognostic factors". — Klein 2025, PMID 39504460, [DOI 10.1093/rheumatology/keae615](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/keae615)
- The optimal CT follow-up interval for ILA was previously unknown. Long-term data now give a median of 3.2 years to progression, with fibrosis extent as the driver. — Park 2023, PMID 37590877, [DOI 10.1164/rccm.202303-0410OC](https://doi.org/10.1164/rccm.202303-0410OC)
- In the ILA/CTD review, the "identification of progressive ILA may enable appropriate surveillance and commencement of treatment". — Yoo 2020, PMID 33364263, [DOI 10.1016/j.ejro.2020.100311](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejro.2020.100311)
- LUS limitations to address: it "traditionally explores only superficial lung regions". — Beigi 2025, PMID 40504021, [DOI 10.1093/rheumatology/keaf328](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/keaf328)

### Inferences (candidate research angles, ranked)
1. **Reversible versus fixed minimal abnormalities in CTD (most promising).** Apply Fleischner ILA subcategories to newly diagnosed CTD cohorts (RA, SSc, IIM, pSS) with prone low-dose CT. Use deep-learning qCT with *voxel-wise transition analysis* (in the style of SLS-I) at baseline, 6–12 months and after treatment, stratified by MUC5B and telomere length. The question is whether "subpleural fibrotic" ILA in CTD behaves like general-population ILA. This addresses Chang 2026 (thresholds), Kim 2016 (transitions) and Rosa-Baez 2025 (biology differs by disease).
2. **Activity imaging for minimal disease.** Test whether FDG-PET or MRI T2/T1-contrast signal in ILA-scale CTD abnormalities predicts the voxels that progress or regress (extends Broens 2024 to sub-threshold disease, and Di Battista 2026).
3. **Radiation-free surveillance.** Calibrate LUS (and UTE-MRI) against prone qCT at the <5% extent level, including the dependent posterior zones. Repeatability studies are needed (Landini 2022 κ=0.26; Beigi 2025/2026).
4. **Airway axis.** Quantitative expiratory CT for small-airway involvement as a separate early phenotype in RA/ACPA and pSS (Tokura 2014, Park 2016, Goules 2026), which the ILA framework does not cover.
5. **Measurement science.** Positional, test-retest and detector variability of qCT at 1–3% extent in CTD (Ahn 2026, Happe 2025, Cozzi 2026).

### Gaps
- Abstract-level evidence only. The explicit "future research" sections of the Fleischner 2020 paper and of the Broens 2022/2024 papers were not read in full text, so their specific agenda items are not itemised here.
- No retrieved EULAR/ACR or ATS CTD-ILD screening guideline, or a CTD-specific ILA consensus, which would be the primary source for formally stated research agendas.
- No retrieved paper explicitly calls for "ILA in CTD" as a distinct construct, apart from Yoo 2020 (review). This absence is itself a potential gap to exploit.
