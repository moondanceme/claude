# Framework and Methods: Progression Prediction in CTD-ILD (Burden vs Activity) and NVC Methodological Standards

Search scope: PubMed via MCP (about 12 queries, roughly 90 abstracts screened) plus targeted web searches and one full-text fetch (the ERS/EULAR 2025 guideline on PMC, first ~100k characters only). Every item below carries a PMID/DOI or URL. Anything not retrieved in this session is listed under Gaps and is not cited as fact.

## A1. Known risk factors for progression in SSc-ILD and other CTD-ILD

### Takeaway
Risk factors for SSc-ILD progression are fairly consistent across registries, trial cohorts and the 2025 ERS/EULAR guideline: male sex, older age, diffuse cutaneous subset or high mRSS, anti-topoisomerase I (ATA/Scl-70), short disease duration (early disease), raised CRP/ESR (inflammatory activity), raised KL-6, low baseline FVC/DLCO, extensive HRCT disease, and reflux/GERD. Each one has modest effect sizes, and the effect varies by subgroup. For RA-ILD, IIM-ILD and SjD-ILD the guideline lists disease-specific sets: UIP pattern in RA, anti-MDA5/anti-synthetase status in IIM, and male sex/CRP/anti-Ro52 in SjD.

### Cited Findings
**Guideline level**
- **ERS/EULAR CTD-ILD guideline (Antoniou KM, … Hoffmann-Vold AM; Eur Respir J 2026;67(1):2402533; parallel publication in Ann Rheum Dis; PMID 40907995; DOI 10.1183/13993003.02533-2024).** 5 PICO and 10 narrative questions produced 50 recommendations. For SSc, narrative Q8 lists male sex, older age, more extensive skin fibrosis (mRSS), higher CRP/ESR, low FVC, low DLCO and more extensive ILD on HRCT as progression risk factors. Table 3 adds longer disease duration, smoking, raised KL-6, ATA positivity and dcSSc. For RA-ILD: UIP/probable UIP pattern, HRCT extent, low FVC/DLCO, older age, male sex, smoking, ESR, anti-CCP/RF, articular activity. For IIM-ILD: raised CRP/ESR, anti-synthetase syndrome, clinically amyopathic DM, skin involvement, arthritis; baseline anti-MDA5/anti-synthetase/anti-Ro52 testing is recommended. For SjD-ILD: older age, male sex, raised CRP, anti-Ro52, extra-pulmonary involvement. **Monitoring:** PFT every 3–6 months during the first 3–5 years of SSc-ILD (first 1–2 years for RA-ILD; first year for IIM/SjD), then every 6–12 months. Repeat HRCT after 1–2 years; in IIM at risk of rapid progression, after 3–6 months and then annually for 2 years. The guideline itself lists **"how to define CTD-ILD progression"** as a research priority (Table 1d). The visible text does not mention nailfold capillaroscopy. — [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12824641); [ERJ page](https://publications.ersnet.org/content/erj/67/1/2402533)
- A 2026 Nat Rev Rheumatol overview (Del Galdo et al.) places the 2023 EULAR SSc treatment recommendations, the BSR guidelines, the **2023 ACR/CHEST** guidelines and the **2025 ERS/EULAR** CTD-ILD guidelines side by side as the current recommendation landscape. — [PMID 42552374, DOI 10.1038/s41584-026-01416-z](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41584-026-01416-z)
- The 2023 EULAR SSc treatment update (22 recommendations; most new ones concern skin and ILD: MMF, nintedanib, rituximab, tocilizumab, etc.). — [PMID 39874231, DOI 10.1136/ard-2024-226430](https://doi.org/10.1136/ard-2024-226430)

**EUSTAR registry analyses**
- **Hoffmann-Vold et al., Ann Rheum Dis 2021 (EUSTAR, n=826 SSc-ILD).** Over 12±3 months, 27% had progressive ILD (FVC decline 5–10% moderate, >10% significant). In each 12-month period 23–27% progressed, but few progressed in consecutive periods. 58% of progressors declined slowly, only 8% declined rapidly and continuously, and 33% never had an FVC decline over about 5 years. The strongest predictors of FVC decline were **male sex, higher mRSS and reflux/dysphagia**. — [PMID 32988845, DOI 10.1136/annrheumdis-2020-217455](https://doi.org/10.1136/annrheumdis-2020-217455)
- EUSTAR GERD analysis (n=5462 SSc-ILD; 80.6% with GERD). GERD was associated with dcSSc (OR 1.44), lower FVC and lower DLCO. Within the GERD group, female sex (HR 1.39) and older age (HR 1.02/yr) predicted progression (FVC ≥10% relative decline, or 5–9% plus DLCO ≥15%, over 12±3 mo). — [PMID 39775478, DOI 10.1093/rheumatology/keaf016](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/keaf016)
- EUSTAR incident ILD (CP 133; n=5331 without ILD on baseline HRCT). Incidence was 3.83/100 person-years and new cases kept appearing up to 10 years. Risk factors were NYHA ≥2, muscle weakness, **high inflammatory markers** and SSc-specific autoantibodies, but not disease duration. Incident ILD progressed less often than prevalent ILD but almost doubled mortality. — [PMID 41549016, DOI 10.1016/j.ard.2025.12.008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ard.2025.12.008)
- EUSTAR anti-Ro/SSA ± RF (n=4221). Anti-Ro/SSA independently predicted ILD (OR 1.24), and Ro/SSA+RF+ was linked to more severe lung involvement. — [PMID 42191555, DOI 10.1016/j.ejim.2026.106898](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejim.2026.106898)
- EUSTAR early SSc (≤3 yr, n=1301, prospective). Synovitis (HR 1.26), tendon friction rubs (HR 1.32), dcSSc (HR 1.30) and ATA (HR 1.25) predicted overall organ progression. — [PMID 25165035, DOI 10.1136/annrheumdis-2014-205295](https://doi.org/10.1136/annrheumdis-2014-205295)

**SENSCIS (trial cohort)**
- Post hoc analysis of the placebo arm (Maher/Distler group, RMD Open 2023). Mean FVC decline was −93.3 mL/yr overall versus **−167.8 mL/yr in early SSc (<18 mo since first non-Raynaud symptom)**, −100.7 mL/yr with **elevated inflammatory markers (CRP ≥6 mg/L and/or platelets ≥330×10⁹/L)**, −121.7 mL/yr with mRSS 15–40 and −131.7 mL/yr with mRSS ≥18. — [PMID 36796874, DOI 10.1136/rmdopen-2022-002859](https://doi.org/10.1136/rmdopen-2022-002859)

**Biomarkers (see also A3)**
- In the SLS-II biomarker substudy (n=92 of 142), 21% developed PPF by 24 months. The **change in KL-6** over 12 months predicted PPF (OR 1.4, p=0.0002) on top of a baseline model of sex, reflux severity and CXCL4. — [PMID 39711034, DOI 10.1002/acr.25485](https://doi.org/10.1002/acr.25485)
- In the prospective KORAIL RA-ILD cohort (n=136; PPF criteria), 34.6% progressed over a median 3 years. Higher baseline KL-6 (HR 1.37) and SP-D (HR 1.51), top-quartile MMP-7 (HR 2.60), and especially a 1-year rise in KL-6 (HR 2.00) predicted progression. — [PMID 39814449, DOI 10.3899/jrheum.2024-0713](https://doi.org/10.3899/jrheum.2024-0713)
- In a plasma proteomics study (discovery n=45, replication n=157, plus a Tokyo/Milan cohort n=113), SP-D was associated with SSc-ILD and **MMP-8 independently predicted SSc-PPF**, adding to clinical variables. — [PMID 42493308, DOI 10.1016/j.ard.2026.06.037](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ard.2026.06.037)
- A review notes converging evidence that CRP, KL-6 and CCL18 are prognostic biomarkers in SSc-ILD. — [PMID 31436584, DOI 10.1097/BOR.0000000000000656](https://doi.org/10.1097/BOR.0000000000000656)
- A meta-analysis of anti-MDA5 DM (27 cohorts, n=2579) found RP-ILD predictors of older age, male sex, fever, skin ulcers, high CRP/LDH/ferritin/KL-6, anti-Ro52, high-titre anti-MDA5 and lymphopenia. — [PMID 42334960, DOI 10.1093/rheumatology/keag311](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/keag311)

**Imaging**
- A deep-learning HRCT UIP probability score (SOFIA; n=522 SSc-ILD) was associated with FVC decline (p=0.008) and 15-year survival, independent of baseline FVC and Goh stage. — [PMID 39418195, DOI 10.1093/rheumatology/keae571](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/keae571)
- In quantitative CT (CALIPER; n=71 SSc), the baseline reticular pattern (Exp 2.70) and ≥5% absolute FVC decline at 24 months (Exp 15.0) predicted 5-year death. Baseline FVC did not. — [PMID 39331602, DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0310892](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0310892)
- In SSc-ILD (n=45), [18F]FDG PET background lung uptake (HR 4.2) and the ILD-GAP index (HR 3.9) independently predicted survival, and combining them improved stratification. — [PMID 40441891, DOI 10.2967/jnumed.125.269497](https://doi.org/10.2967/jnumed.125.269497)

### Inferences
- The risk-factor list naturally falls into two groups. One group is **static or "burden"** variables: low FVC/DLCO, HRCT extent, UIP pattern, age. The other is **dynamic or "activity/driver"** variables: early disease, CRP/platelets, rising mRSS, rising KL-6, ATA. The SENSCIS subgroup data show the activity group selects the fastest decliners. This supports framing a study around "burden vs activity".
- Because progression in SSc-ILD is mostly intermittent and slow (EUSTAR), a one-off baseline burden measure will predict who progresses in a given year only modestly. That leaves room for a dynamic or vascular activity marker.

### Gaps
- The primary 2023 ACR/CHEST screening/monitoring and treatment guideline papers were not retrieved. I found only secondary references (PMID 39822854; the J Bras Pneumol reference list 41417310), so I cannot quote their risk-factor tables.
- The full ERS/EULAR guideline text beyond the first ~100k characters (narrative sections for SjD/MCTD progression) was not read.
- I found no CTD-wide (non-SSc) pooled estimates of risk-factor effect sizes.

## A2. Existing prediction models and their discrimination

### Takeaway
Existing tools mostly predict **mortality** from **severity/burden** variables: ILD-GAP (sex, age, FVC, DLCO), Goh staging (HRCT extent/FVC), SADL (smoking, age, DLCO) and CT radiomics nomograms. They reach C-indices of about 0.65–0.88 for death. Models that predict **progression** itself perform worse: the SENSCIS placebo-arm model had an apparent AUC of 0.63 overall and 0.75 in an "early/inflammatory" subgroup. There is a lot of room to improve short-term (12–24 month) progression prediction.

### Cited Findings
- **SENSCIS progression model (RMD Open 2024; placebo n=288).** Endpoint was absolute FVC%pred decline >5% or death at 52 weeks. Apparent AUC was **0.63** overall and **0.75** in the early/inflammatory/severe-skin subgroup (n=155; predictors DLCO%, time since first non-Raynaud symptom, mRSS, ATA, MMF use). The authors conclude that different subgroups may need different approaches. — [PMID 39242112, DOI 10.1136/rmdopen-2024-004240](https://doi.org/10.1136/rmdopen-2024-004240)
- **SADL (Morisset et al., Chest 2017).** Smoking, age and DLCO predict SSc-ILD mortality. C-index was 0.88 in derivation and 0.84 in validation; the points version reached 0.82 with 3-year low/moderate/high risk groups. — [PMC5812750](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5812750)
- **ILD-GAP in Chinese CTD-ILD (Respir Res 2022, n=675, 23.4% died).** ILD-GAP C-index **0.652** versus 0.767 for a machine-learning nomogram. The authors question whether ILD-GAP applies to Chinese CTD-ILD. — [Respir Res 2022, DOI 10.1186/s12931-022-01925-x](https://respiratory-research.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12931-022-01925-x); [PMC8742429](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8742429/)
- **Computer-based CT versus GAP in CTD-ILD (UCL).** ILD-GAP AUC 0.72; a stratified CT-GAP model reached AUC 0.89 for 1-year mortality. — [UCL Discovery PDF](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10054264/)
- **CT radiomics nomogram for ILD-GAP stage in CTD-ILD (Front Immunol 2023; two centres, n=245).** AUC 0.887 training, 0.885 internal and **0.85 external**. The endpoint is a GAP *stage* (a severity surrogate), not progression. — [PMID 37868980, DOI 10.3389/fimmu.2023.1213008](https://doi.org/10.3389/fimmu.2023.1213008)
- **RA-ILD radiomics + KL-6 nomogram (n=177).** AUC 0.948 training and 0.923 test for low-risk ILD-GAP group. Single centre, severity endpoint. — [PMID 39148737, DOI 10.3389/fimmu.2024.1417156](https://doi.org/10.3389/fimmu.2024.1417156)
- **Machine-learning 2-year mortality model in ILD (n=670, 47.9% CTD-ILD, single centre).** Nine algorithms; adds nutritional indices to ILD-GAP; internal validation only. — [PMID 41851885, DOI 10.1186/s12931-026-03631-4](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12931-026-03631-4)
- **Goh staging versus Warrick score (n=33 SSc-ILD, retrospective).** Compares sensitivity for radiological progression. Goh progression can only be assessed in patients with limited disease at baseline (24/33). — [PMID 42457349, DOI 10.3899/jrheum.2026-0185](https://doi.org/10.3899/jrheum.2026-0185)
- **CT densitometry CII (SSc-ILD, n=119 derivation).** AUC 0.77–0.78 for Goh-extensive ILD. Goh-extensive ILD (OR 2.94) and CII ≤−0.96 predicted death. — [PMID 38302169, DOI 10.3899/jrheum.2023-0415](https://doi.org/10.3899/jrheum.2023-0415)
- **Modified lung ultrasound score (n=195 SARD-ILD).** Correlates with Warrick (r=0.863) and ILD-GAP (r=0.613). — [PMID 40973473, DOI 10.1093/rheumatology/keaf501](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/keaf501)

### Inferences
- The published C-indices of 0.8–0.9 mostly apply to **mortality** or to **severity-stage classification**, which are burden endpoints. For **12-month progression** the best externally anchored figure is SENSCIS at AUC 0.63–0.75 (apparent, not validated). A grant can reasonably say that progression prediction in CTD-ILD is "moderate at best (AUC ≈0.6–0.75)".
- Chinese CTD-ILD cohorts show ILD-GAP transfers poorly (C≈0.65). This is a local justification for new models.
- Most ML and radiomics models are single- or dual-centre with severity endpoints and no external validation. A prospective multicentre design with a PPF-based endpoint would fill a recognised gap.

### Gaps
- Primary papers for Goh 2008 (Am J Respir Crit Care Med) and Ryerson 2014 ILD-GAP (Chest) were not retrieved, so the original C-indices (and the Goh thresholds: >20% HRCT extent, or 10–30% indeterminate resolved by FVC <70%) are not verified in this session. The ≥20% HRCT figure does appear as a threshold in PMID 40600932.
- du Bois index: no retrieved source.
- No retrieved model combines NVC with HRCT to predict ILD progression. This looks like a true gap (see A3).

## A3. Is "severity/burden vs activity/driver" an established framework? Where does NVC fit?

### Takeaway
SSc has an established distinction between **disease activity** (EUSTAR Activity Index, 2017), **damage** (SCTC Damage Index) and **severity** (Medsger scale), and recent cohorts measure them separately. In ILD specifically, "activity" is mostly operationalised through dynamic biomarkers (CRP, rising KL-6, CXCL4, MMP-8) and early disease. ILD guidelines do not explicitly frame it as "burden vs activity", so the framing is novel but defensible. NVC is consistently described as a microvascular marker whose "active/late" patterns and capillary loss predict organ progression. A 2025 prospective study links baseline NVC disorganisation and the late pattern to FVC/DLCO decline and to KL-6 rise.

### Cited Findings
- **EUSTAR Activity Index (Valentini et al., Ann Rheum Dis 2017;76:270–276).** A weighted 10-point index derived from 97 expert-rated charts. Items: Δskin, mRSS, digital ulcers, tendon friction rubs, CRP, DLCO. A score ≥2.5 identifies active disease. — [ARD 76/1/270](https://ard.bmj.com/content/76/1/270); [UCL AAM](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1516569/)
- A cohort study that measured **activity (EUSTAR-AI), severity (Medsger) and damage (SCTC-DI)** separately defined persistent activity as EUSTAR-AI ≥2.5 at ≥50% of visits and moderate-severe damage as SCTC-DI ≥6. (Reported in the web search summary; the primary record was not opened.) — [Padova research record](https://www.research.unipd.it/handle/11577/3564984)
- **Sieiro Santos et al., Rheumatology 2025 (prospective, n=74 SSc, 2-year follow-up).** ILD rose from 38% to 51% of the cohort and ≥20% HRCT involvement from 32% to 43%. Baseline **capillary disorganisation predicted ΔFVC% (β −0.75) and ΔDLCO% (β −0.24)** and mRSS worsening. **The late NVC pattern predicted a larger KL-6 rise (β 0.18), DLCO decline (β −0.38) and mRSS worsening.** Baseline SCTC-DI predicted HRCT fibrosis progression (β 0.32). EUSTAR 2017 AI and SCTC-DI were both measured. This is the closest prior work to an "NVC + CT" design. — [PMID 40600932, DOI 10.1093/rheumatology/keaf361](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/keaf361)
- **Vanhaecke et al., Rheumatology 2022 (EUSTAR, 7 centres, n=334; standardised EULAR SG reading).** Normal capillary density was protective against new or progressive severe organ involvement (OR 0.77). The active/late pattern predicted overall progression (OR 2.14) and skin progression (OR 1.70). The study was underpowered for single organs such as the lung. — [PMID 35176132, DOI 10.1093/rheumatology/keac079](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/keac079)
- **Systematic review (Best Pract Res Clin Rheumatol 2026; 14 longitudinal studies, all centrally read with the EULAR SG framework, QUIPS low/moderate RoB).** Capillary density <7/mm and active/late patterns consistently predicted progression, and were linked with skin fibrosis, DU, PAH and ILD. The review describes reduced density as reflecting "a globally more severe phenotype". — [PMID 41826085, DOI 10.1016/j.berh.2026.102130](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.berh.2026.102130)
- **SENSCIS NVC substudy (RMD Open 2025).** NVC at baseline and week 52 (density, giants, shapes, % fingers with microhaemorrhages). Nothing changed overall. In patients with risk factors for rapid FVC decline (n=38), density fell numerically on placebo and stayed stable on nintedanib. The ILD-progressor subgroup was only n=11. This shows NVC density as a dynamic measure that may respond to treatment, though with very small numbers. — [PMID 41107055, DOI 10.1136/rmdopen-2025-005704](https://doi.org/10.1136/rmdopen-2025-005704)
- **Tocilizumab cohort (n=13).** Capillary density rose at 6 months, and the rise correlated strongly with improvement in PFTs. HGF correlated with density. — [PMID 41664492, DOI 10.1111/exd.70220](https://doi.org/10.1111/exd.70220)
- **12-year NVC evolution (Sulli et al., n=34 starting from the "early" pattern).** 76% changed pattern. Transitions were faster with nucleolar ANA or Scl-70. Organ involvement increased from early to active to late. — [PMID 31750929, DOI 10.1093/rheumatology/kez374](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/kez374)
- **Cross-sectional (n=70).** Advanced NVC patterns were associated with ILD (OR 3.73, robust to adjustment for ATA, duration and mRSS). AUC for ILD was 0.688. — [PMID 42063790, DOI 10.3389/fmed.2026.1808581](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmed.2026.1808581)
- **NVC and outcomes (n=360, median 11.7 years).** Avascular areas predicted major cardiovascular events (HR 2.24) and mortality (HR 2.09). Microhaemorrhages were protective (HR 0.35). — [PMID 41076942, DOI 10.1016/j.semarthrit.2025.152845](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.semarthrit.2025.152845)
- **Myositis.** The EULAR SG review of IIM found reduced density and scleroderma-like pattern associated with **active disease**. — [PMID 35421608, DOI 10.1016/j.autrev.2022.103087](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.autrev.2022.103087). In juvenile myositis, AI-quantified NVC features tracked disease activity and changed by 3 months after treatment. — [PMID 42681940, DOI 10.1002/ctm2.70789](https://doi.org/10.1002/ctm2.70789)
- **SARD-ILD miRNA study (n=24).** miR-483-5p was higher with microvascular (NVC) involvement, which carried a distinct miRNA signature. — [PMID 41350880, DOI 10.1186/s12931-025-03392-6](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12931-025-03392-6)
- **Activity-type ILD biomarkers.** ΔKL-6 predicted PPF in SLS-II ([PMID 39711034](https://doi.org/10.1002/acr.25485)) and in KORAIL ([PMID 39814449](https://doi.org/10.3899/jrheum.2024-0713)). MMP-8 predicted SSc-PPF ([PMID 42493308](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ard.2026.06.037)). In the SENSCIS data, inflammatory markers (CRP/platelets) identify fast decliners ([PMID 36796874](https://doi.org/10.1136/rmdopen-2022-002859)).

### Inferences
- A grant can cite the EUSTAR-AI / SCTC-DI / Medsger triad as precedent for separating activity, damage and severity in SSc. Applying it to the lung (HRCT extent and FVC/DLCO as "burden", NVC and inflammatory or epithelial markers as "activity/driver") extends that framework; it has not been described before.
- NVC carries both signals. The **active pattern, giants and microhaemorrhages** are dynamic, "activity-like" features: in the n=360 cohort microhaemorrhages were even protective for mortality. **Capillary loss, avascular areas and the late pattern** are closer to cumulative microvascular **damage**. Analysing NVC components separately, rather than one global pattern, would let the study test which one adds to CT burden.
- Sieiro Santos 2025 (n=74, single centre) is the key prior study. It suggests an effect but is small, uncontrolled for multiplicity and limited to SSc. A multicentre CTD-wide study with AI-quantified NVC would be a clear step forward.

### Gaps
- MDAAT/MITAX (the myositis activity/damage tools) and the original SCTC-DI paper (Ferdowsi et al.) were not retrieved.
- I found no ILD guideline that explicitly uses "burden vs activity" wording. Report it as a novel framing.
- No data were retrieved on NVC in RA-ILD, SjD-ILD or MCTD-ILD progression (non-SSc CTDs).

## A4. Progression definitions usable as endpoints, and expected event rates

### Takeaway
Endpoint options:
- **2022 ATS/ERS/JRS/ALAT PPF** (2 of 3 within 1 year: symptoms, radiological progression, physiological decline of absolute FVC ≥5% or DLCO ≥10%).
- **OMERACT/EUSTAR SSc-ILD progression** (relative FVC ≥10%, or 5–9% plus relative DLCO ≥15%, over about 12 months).
- **Minimal clinically important FVC worsening.**
- **Erice criteria.**

Annual progression rates in SSc-ILD cohorts run about 20–27% per 12 months. Over 2–3 years, PPF rates are about 21% (SLS-II, 24 months) to 35% (RA-ILD, 3 years).

### Cited Findings
- **2022 ATS/ERS/JRS/ALAT PPF.** At least 2 of 3 within the past year with no alternative explanation: worsening symptoms; radiological progression; physiological progression (absolute FVC decline ≥5% and/or absolute DLCOc decline ≥10%). It applies to non-IPF fibrotic ILD. Commentary notes the absolute ≥5% FVC threshold is more sensitive than a relative 10% threshold. — [Front Med 2023, DOI 10.3389/fmed.2023.1106560](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medicine/articles/10.3389/fmed.2023.1106560/full); [PMC9952875](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9952875)
- **EUSTAR/OMERACT-type definition** as used in EUSTAR: relative FVC decline ≥10%, or 5–9% with relative DLCO decline ≥15%, over 12±3 months. — [PMID 39775478](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/keaf016)
- **Concordance and prognostic value of four definitions (AJRCCM 2024; n=245 SSc-ILD; 24-month progression; 60-month mortality, 26 SSc deaths).** Progression was linked to mortality under MCID-FVC (HR 2.27), OMERACT (HR 2.90) and Erice (HR 2.69). The link was weak with disease duration ≥3 yr, mild impairment or PASP ≥40 mmHg. Erice performed better in long-duration, lcSSc and PH subgroups. — [PMID 39078207, DOI 10.1164/rccm.202311-2153OC](https://doi.org/10.1164/rccm.202311-2153OC)
- **Event rates:**
  - EUSTAR SSc-ILD: 27% progressed over 12±3 mo (23–27% per 12-month window) ([PMID 32988845](https://doi.org/10.1136/annrheumdis-2020-217455))
  - SLS-II: 21% PPF by 24 mo ([PMID 39711034](https://doi.org/10.1002/acr.25485))
  - KORAIL RA-ILD: 34.6% PPF over median 3 yr ([PMID 39814449](https://doi.org/10.3899/jrheum.2024-0713))
  - Sieiro Santos: ILD prevalence 38%→51% over 2 yr ([PMID 40600932](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/keaf361))
  - SENSCIS NVC substudy: only 11 progressors (FVC >5% or death) at 52 wk among NVC participants ([PMID 41107055](https://doi.org/10.1136/rmdopen-2025-005704))
  - EUSTAR incident ILD: 3.83/100 person-years ([PMID 41549016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ard.2025.12.008))
- **ERS/EULAR 2025** states that the definition of CTD-ILD progression still needs work (research agenda). — [PMC12824641](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12824641)

### Inferences
- For power calculations, assume about 20–30% progression at 12 months and about 30–40% at 24 months for a CTD-ILD cohort. Use the PPF criteria as primary, with OMERACT/EUSTAR and an FVC-slope analysis as sensitivity analyses. The intermittent progression pattern (EUSTAR) argues for 24-month follow-up and for counting repeated events or using time-to-event analysis.
- The radiological domain of PPF needs standardised HRCT reading (central reading, or quantitative CT/AI), because the CT burden variable and the endpoint share that modality. Bias from this overlap should be handled explicitly, for example by using a physiological-only endpoint as a sensitivity analysis.

### Gaps
- The original OMERACT SSc-ILD progression paper (Khanna et al.) and the Erice working-group paper were not retrieved directly; they are only referenced through PMID 39078207.
- The ACR/CHEST 2023 definition of progression was not verified.

## B. NVC methodological standards and technical advances for a rigorous multicentre prospective study

### Takeaway
A rigorous multicentre NVC protocol should:
- Follow the EULAR Study Group on Microcirculation (SG MC/RD) standardised definitions and the 33-item reporting checklist (Ingegnoli 2021).
- Image **all 8 fingers excluding thumbs**, ideally 4 fields/32 images per patient. Sensitivity falls when fewer fingers are imaged, and pattern disagreement reaches 27–42% with partial sampling.
- Report quantitative density (capillaries/mm), dilations, giants, haemorrhages and abnormal shapes, plus the Fast Track/pattern classification.
- Use **central blinded reading** plus validated automated software. Options include the Manchester deep-learning system (AUC 0.95–0.97, works on low-cost microscopes), the Zurich ViT (AUC about 0.82–0.93 by feature), and CAPI-Score/CAPI-Detect (accuracy about 0.91 for SSc vs non-SSc; external validation for structural abnormalities).

Most AI tools are validated for *diagnosis or pattern* classification, not for *longitudinal change* or prognosis.

### Cited Findings
**Standardisation and reporting**
- **EULAR SG / SCTC Delphi reporting checklist (Ingegnoli et al., Rheumatology 2021).** A systematic review of 319 articles, then a 3-round Delphi with 80 experts from 31 countries, produced a **33-item checklist**: patient preparation (15 items), device description (5) and examination details (13). Methods reporting in prior literature was highly heterogeneous. — [PMID 32984894, DOI 10.1093/rheumatology/keaa457](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/keaa457)
- **Reliability of simple EULAR SG capillary definitions (Cutolo et al., Rheumatology 2018; letter).** — [PMID 29361155, DOI 10.1093/rheumatology/kex460](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/kex460) (abstract unavailable; kappa values not retrieved)
- **EULAR SG Fast Track algorithm.** Used to separate "scleroderma pattern" (giants and/or capillary loss with abnormal shapes) from "non-scleroderma" patterns, as applied in [PMID 40410857](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12969-025-01104-4) and described as an "easy-to-manage procedure" proposed by EULAR in the CAPI-Score paper ([PMID 38530791](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/keae197)).
- **Algorithm comparison (n=258 NVCs).** Density ≤8 capillaries/mm had AUC 0.911 and accuracy 87.9% for the scleroderma pattern. Giants had 98.1% specificity. Giants plus density ≤8/mm reached 91.1% accuracy. — [PMID 40757426, DOI 10.55563/clinexprheumatol/0igmnr](https://doi.org/10.55563/clinexprheumatol/0igmnr)
- **Italian Delphi on clinical NVC reporting (52 experts, 23 items)**, built on the EULAR SG framework. — [PMID 41537537, DOI 10.55563/clinexprheumatol/2z4j95](https://doi.org/10.55563/clinexprheumatol/2z4j95)
- **Worldwide standardised paediatric NVC (13 centres, 413 subjects, 6474 images, 16 images/patient acquired locally and read centrally).** A precedent for a multicentre central-reading design. — [PMID 36005889, DOI 10.1093/rheumatology/keac487](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/keac487)
- **Smith V et al. review (Best Pract Res Clin Rheumatol 2023).** Covers practical acquisition and analysis, standardised terminology and the EULAR SG consensus reporting framework, and stresses the predictive value of capillary loss for new organ involvement. — [PMID 37419757, DOI 10.1016/j.berh.2023.101849](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.berh.2023.101849)
- **Structured training.** An Italian 2-day course for 82 trainees improved NVC skills. This is relevant to rater training in multicentre studies. — [PMID 42860915, DOI 10.4081/reumatismo.2026.2250](https://doi.org/10.4081/reumatismo.2026.2250)

**How many fingers or images**
- **Dinsdale et al., Rheumatology 2019 (2994 expert assessments of 1600 images from 173 participants).** Eight-finger sensitivity was 74.6%. A single finger gave 31.7–46.6%, both ring fingers 59.8%, and four fingers (ring + middle) 66.7%. Conclusion: **examine all 8 nailbeds (excluding thumbs)**. — [PMID 30247696, DOI 10.1093/rheumatology/key293](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/key293)
- **Guillén del Castillo et al., Med Clin 2026 (2387 NVCs, 12 hospitals).** Compared with 32 images (4 per finger), pattern disagreement was **26.7% / 36.6% / 41.5%** with 16 / 8 / 4 images. With 16 images, 18.5% of SSc-pattern calls flipped and more than 30% of subtypes changed. CAPI algorithms also showed discrepancies. Conclusion: all fingers except thumbs, four nailbed areas each. — [PMID 42013567, DOI 10.1016/j.medcli.2026.107426](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.medcli.2026.107426)

**Automated / AI analysis**
- **Manchester deep-learning system (Bharathi et al., Rheumatology 2023).** Detects each distal-row capillary and measures morphology, then combines fingers into a patient-level SSc probability. Trained on 111 subjects. Test AUC was **0.97** on high-resolution images (n=132) and **0.95** on a **low-cost USB microscope** (n=66), with equal sensitivity/specificity of 91% and 89%. Expert consensus reached sensitivity 82% and specificity 73%. — [PMID 36651676, DOI 10.1093/rheumatology/kead026](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/kead026)
- **Zurich ViT (Garaiman et al., Rheumatology 2023;62(7); EUSTAR + VEDOSS images, about 300 patients).** An off-the-shelf vision transformer had AUC 81.8–84.5% for microangiopathic features and 83.2% for the scleroderma pattern. In the reliability subset it reached 92.6% for giants and 90.2% for enlarged capillaries. It matched or beat 1 of 4 rheumatologists and is positioned as a screening aid. — [DOI 10.1093/rheumatology/keac541; PMC10321092](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10321092); correction [PMID 37040613](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/kead156)
- **Gracia Tello et al., external validation of a deep CNN (Clin Exp Rheumatol 2023).** 1164 images annotated by 5 capillaroscopists. At least 3 raters agreed on 86.9% of images, and the algorithm matched 75.8% of those consensus labels. — [PMID 37140670, DOI 10.55563/clinexprheumatol/m6obl3](https://doi.org/10.55563/clinexprheumatol/m6obl3)
- **CAPI-Score (Rheumatology 2024).** A software-quantified, Fast-Track-inspired algorithm built on 851 NVCs (21,957 images) with pattern consensus among 9 capillaroscopists. — [PMID 38530791, DOI 10.1093/rheumatology/keae197](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/keae197)
- **CAPI-Detect (Rheumatology 2025).** 1780 NVCs read by 3–4 observers; 24 automated capillary variables; CatBoost. Accuracy was 0.912 for SSc vs non-SSc, 0.812 across SSc patterns and 0.746 for normal vs non-specific. On the full-consensus set: 0.910 / 0.925 / 0.933. Outperformed CAPI-Score. — [PMID 39918978, DOI 10.1093/rheumatology/keaf073](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/keaf073)
- **Negative control.** A pretrained ViT with no fine-tuning had **0% clinical applicability**; two rheumatologists in consensus reached 94.2% accuracy (κ 0.827). Off-the-shelf models need domain training. — [PMID 42138864, DOI 10.1590/1806-9282.20251389](https://doi.org/10.1590/1806-9282.20251389)
- **AI pitfall case.** AI correctly called an "early scleroderma" pattern but missed intraloop neoangiogenesis that suggested dermatomyositis (anti-Mi-2+). Current algorithms are trained on SSc patterns. This matters for a CTD-wide (IIM-inclusive) cohort. — [PMID 42402485, DOI 10.1515/dx-2026-0062](https://doi.org/10.1515/dx-2026-0062)
- **AI in SSc review (Arthritis Care Res 2026).** NVC computer vision reaches "expert-level agreement for microvascular staging", but most tools are single-centre with limited external validation, a heavy annotation burden and small data. — [PMID 42007790, DOI 10.1002/acr.80068](https://doi.org/10.1002/acr.80068)
- **Other tools:**
  - ML multiclass Ctr/RA/SLE (600 images; macro AUC 0.96 train, 0.80 test) — [PMID 41781159](https://doi.org/10.1136/rmdopen-2025-006393)
  - CNN fuzzy ensemble (accuracy 85%) — [PMID 40054169](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compbiomed.2025.109975)
  - EfficientNet cascade transfer learning — [PMID 39814806](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-85277-8)
  - Automated density from a low-cost Dino-Lite USB microscope (COVID cohort) — [PMID 39961398](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.mvr.2025.104796)
  - Deep-learning perfused-capillary density in CKD — [PMID 42935699](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ekir.2026.107033)
- **Functional add-on.** Multispectral oxygenation imaging of nailfold capillaries (CAPoxy, n=22) is feasible. — [PMID 42585238, DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0329913](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0329913)

**Longitudinal behaviour and modifiability**
- NVC patterns evolve: 76% of patients starting from the "early" pattern changed over 12 years, faster with Scl-70 ([PMID 31750929](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/kez374)). In very early SSc (Hit Hard and Early trial, n=30), serial NVC was tracked for 3 years and 75% had an active pattern at baseline ([PMID 42370081, DOI 10.1016/j.ero.2026.02.006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ero.2026.02.006)).
- Density can change with treatment: it increased with tocilizumab ([PMID 41664492](https://doi.org/10.1111/exd.70220)) and stayed stable on nintedanib versus a numerical decline on placebo in high-risk SENSCIS patients ([PMID 41107055](https://doi.org/10.1136/rmdopen-2025-005704)). It also changed over 1 and 4 years of aminaphtone ([PMID 42445479](https://doi.org/10.2147/OARRR.S613596)).
- Non-specific dilations (30–50 µm) can precede the scleroderma pattern ([PMID 36197673, DOI 10.1136/rmdopen-2022-002449](https://doi.org/10.1136/rmdopen-2022-002449)).

### Inferences
Recommended protocol elements for a grant:
1. EULAR SG definitions and the 33-item reporting checklist.
2. A pre-specified device: 200× videocapillaroscope, with magnification and resolution recorded.
3. 8 fingers × 4 fields, 32 images per visit, with acclimatisation at room temperature for at least 15–20 min and no manicure or nailfold trauma. The patient-preparation items come from the Ingegnoli checklist; exact acclimatisation times were not verified in this session.
4. Two blinded central readers with a third adjudicating; report ICC/κ at baseline and on a re-read subset.
5. Parallel automated quantification (density/mm, apical width, giants, haemorrhage count, avascular areas) with an existing validated tool such as the Manchester system or CAPI-Detect, or a locally trained model with external validation across centres. Report agreement with central readers (Bland–Altman, ICC).
6. Repeat NVC at 6/12/24 months to model ΔNVC as a time-varying "activity" covariate.
7. Pre-specify handling of image quality, fingers that cannot be assessed, and non-SSc CTD morphologies (DM neoangiogenesis, SLE tortuosity) that SSc-trained AI may misclassify.

Device heterogeneity across centres is a key risk. The Manchester system's robustness on low-cost microscopes (AUC 0.95) supports using a harmonised low-cost device plus AI, but per-centre calibration is still needed.

### Gaps
- The primary **Smith V et al. 2020 Autoimmun Rev 102458** ("Standardisation of nailfold capillaroscopy for the assessment of patients with Raynaud's phenomenon and SSc") and the **Smith et al. 2019 Fast Track algorithm** paper (Ann Rheum Dis) were **not retrieved** in this session (exact-title PubMed queries returned nothing). They should be cited from the reference manager after verifying PMID/DOI. Their content here is known only indirectly (PMIDs 38530791, 40410857, 40757426).
- **Numeric inter- and intra-rater reliability** (κ/ICC for density, giants, pattern) of the EULAR SG definitions was not retrieved (the Cutolo 2018 letter has no abstract). The only agreement data found: expert consensus in ≥3 of 5 raters on 86.9% of images (PMID 37140670), full consensus in 515 of 1490 NVCs (PMID 39918978), and rheumatologist pair κ 0.827 (PMID 42138864).
- **Short-term test–retest variability** of quantitative density, needed to define a minimal detectable change for longitudinal analysis, was not found.
- No retrieved AI-NVC study validated automated measures for **predicting ILD progression** or for longitudinal change in a multicentre CTD-ILD population.
