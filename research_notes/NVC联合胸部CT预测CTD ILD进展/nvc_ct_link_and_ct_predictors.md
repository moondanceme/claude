# NVC–chest CT link in CTD-ILD, and chest CT predictors of CTD-ILD progression

Search method: PubMed (MCP PubMed tools), about 28 tool calls, searched October 2026. All items below were retrieved, and the abstracts were read. "Abstract-only" means the full text was not read, so covariates and full effect sizes may be missing. PMIDs and DOIs are given for each item.

---

## A1. Do NVC parameters/patterns correlate with HRCT extent, pattern, quantitative CT and CT vascular metrics? (redundancy vs complementarity)

### Takeaway
In SSc and DM, NVC capillary loss, avascular areas, microhaemorrhage and the late pattern are associated with the presence and severity of ILD on visual HRCT. The correlations that are reported are only moderate (|r| about 0.4–0.5 where given). I found **no study** that correlated NVC with quantitative CT density histograms (MLA, skewness, kurtosis, HAA, CII), CALIPER/AI texture output, or CT vascular metrics (PVV, BV5, PA diameter). The overlap between the two is therefore partial, and the literature has not measured it.

### Cited Findings
**Direct NVC–CT correlation studies (all cross-sectional, small):**
- De Santis et al. 2016, Auto Immun Highlights (PMID 26878864; DOI 10.1007/s13317-016-0077-y): n=44 SSc. Giant capillaries were **inversely** correlated with the extent of reticular pattern on HRCT (r = −0.5, p=0.04). Mean capillary density correlated with DLCO/VA (r=+0.4). Serum VEGF correlated with GGO and reticular extent (r=+0.4). — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13317-016-0077-y)
- Kotani group (Sci Rep 2020; PMID 32973255; DOI 10.1038/s41598-020-72752-7): DM-ILD, anti-MDA5 vs anti-ARS. Microhaemorrhage and capillary loss scores "correlated significantly with the total fibrosis scores of chest HRCT" (r not given in the abstract). Microhaemorrhage was higher in ILD-related deaths (P=0.02). Disorganization was higher in MDA5+ and tortuosity higher in ARS+. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-020-72752-7)
- Ji et al. 2018, Beijing Da Xue Xue Bao (PMID 29930420; no DOI; Chinese): n=71 SSc (45 ILD). ILD+ patients had higher scores for capillary loss (P=0.003), avascular area (P=0.006) and ramified capillaries (P=0.006). Ramified capillaries plus dcSSc were independent risk factors for ILD presence. Giant capillaries were **lower** in severe vs mild CT-scored ILD (P=0.019). — [Source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29930420/)
- Indian cross-sectional study (Indian Dermatol Online J 2022; PMID 35287403; DOI 10.4103/idoj.idoj_393_21): n=43 SSc, dermoscopic NFC. There was a significant association between NFC stage (early/active/late) and HRCT ILD severity (p=0.0003). 6 patients had NFC changes but no ILD. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.4103/idoj.idoj_393_21)
- Clin Rheumatol 2024 (PMID 38963466; DOI 10.1007/s10067-024-07049-5): n=95 ILD (46 CTD-ILD, 49 non-CTD-ILD). Tortuosity was higher in **UIP than NSIP** on CT (P=0.041). NFC abnormalities were also present in non-CTD-ILD. The authors suggest fibrotic lung disease itself may affect the microvasculature. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10067-024-07049-5)
- Matsuda et al. 2023 (ANCA vasculitis, not CTD; PMID 35816001; DOI 10.1093/rheumatology/keac402): microhaemorrhage scores "tended to correlate" with HRCT GGO and consolidation scores. Microhaemorrhage fell after 3 months of immunosuppression, which suggests that NVC captures activity/inflammation. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/keac402)
- Lung ultrasound study (Clin Med Insights Arthritis 2025; PMID 40008078; DOI 10.1177/11795441251318050): n=32 SSc. B-lines agreed with HRCT score (ICC 0.864) and were negatively correlated with NVC capillary density (r = −0.687). This is an indirect NVC–imaging link through LUS. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1177/11795441251318050)
- Spanish Scleroderma Study Group (Rheumatol Int 2018; PMID 29322341; DOI 10.1007/s00296-017-3916-x): n=595 SSc-ILD. In multivariable analysis, the factors associated with FVC<70% included **both** a ground-glass pattern on HRCT **and** an active NVC pattern (cross-sectional). — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00296-017-3916-x)

**NVC across HRCT-defined ILD categories (diagnostic, not prognostic):**
- Lee et al. 2023, Korean J Intern Med (PMID 37488834; DOI 10.3904/kjim.2022.358): n=81. The semi-quantitative NFC score was CTD-ILD 5.8 > IPAF 4.2 > IIP 3.0 (p<0.001). NSIP predominated in CTD-ILD/IPAF and UIP in IIP. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.3904/kjim.2022.358)
- Sabella et al. 2026, Reumatismo (PMID 42922420; DOI 10.4081/reumatismo.2026.2398): n=67. Total NVC score was IPF 3.2 < IPAF 4.5 < CTD-ILD 6.5 (p=0.001). A scleroderma pattern was seen in 95.2% of SSc-ILD, and 81.3% of IPF had nonspecific abnormalities. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.4081/reumatismo.2026.2398)
- Narrative review (Diagnostics 2025; PMID 39941292; DOI 10.3390/diagnostics15030362) states that "low capillary density was associated with the presence and severity of ILD and a reduction in FVC and DLCO" and that NVC can differentiate UIP from NSIP patterns. It also says more research is needed. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.3390/diagnostics15030362)

### Inferences
- The correlations are moderate at best (|r| about 0.4–0.5 for single parameters vs CT extent, where reported). Some directions are counter-intuitive: giant capillaries are *inversely* related to reticulation and severity, which fits the shift from the active to the late pattern. This argues against full redundancy. NVC appears to reflect microvascular stage and activity, while CT reflects accumulated parenchymal damage.
- Microhaemorrhage tracks inflammatory activity and responds to treatment. This suggests NVC may carry "activity" information that a single baseline CT extent does not.
- NVC abnormalities in IPF and non-CTD fibrotic ILD point to possible reverse causation or confounding by fibrosis or hypoxaemia. Any NVC–CT model should account for this.

### Gaps
- **No retrieved study correlated NVC with quantitative CT** (density histogram MLA/skewness/kurtosis/HAA, CII, CALIPER, AI texture) **or with CT vascular metrics (PVV, BV5, PA diameter)**. This is a clear empirical gap and a natural mechanistic hypothesis: peripheral microangiopathy vs pulmonary vascular pruning.
- No study reported NVC by NSIP vs fibrotic NSIP vs OP subpattern in SSc. The GGO-vs-reticulation split was reported only in small studies (De Santis; Matsuda in AAV).
- Most studies are single-centre, cross-sectional, n<100, and use heterogeneous NVC scoring.

## A2. Prediction or multivariable models that combine NVC with CT, KL-6 or autoantibodies; has NVC's incremental value been tested?

### Takeaway
Models of "NVC + other markers" exist only in partial form. NVC has been combined with autoantibodies (Leiden), with KL-6 (IIM-ILD and a prospective SSc cohort) and with HRCT measured at baseline. **No retrieved study tested whether NVC adds prognostic value on top of CT-based fibrotic burden** using a C-index increment, ΔAUC, NRI or IDI. Large longitudinal data (EUSTAR) show that baseline NVC pattern does **not** independently predict incident ILD. Worsening NVC over time does. Where NVC was adjusted for CT ILD extent (mortality), its effect weakened and lost significance. "NVC + chest CT to predict progression" is therefore **untested as a formal incremental-value question**, and the closest existing work covers only part of it.

### Cited Findings
- **Sieiro Santos et al. 2025, Rheumatology (Oxford) (PMID 40600932; DOI 10.1093/rheumatology/keaf361)**: prospective, n=74 SSc (38% ILD at baseline), 2-year follow-up. Baseline NVC, HRCT, PFT and KL-6/IL-18/IL-18BP were recorded. Capillary **disorganization predicted %FVC decline (β = −0.75, P=0.03)** and %DLCO decline (β = −0.24, P=0.03). The **late pattern was associated with a larger KL-6 rise (β=0.18, P=0.04)** and DLCO decline (β = −0.38, P=0.04). A higher SCTC-DI predicted progressive semi-quantitative HRCT fibrosis (β=0.32, P=0.003). The authors call NVC an "independent predictor", but the abstract does not say whether baseline HRCT extent was a covariate. No discrimination metrics (AUC/C-index/NRI) were reported. This is the closest existing study to "NVC + CT + KL-6 for progression", but it does not test incremental value. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/keaf361)
- **Sieiro Santos et al. 2025, Rheumatology (Oxford) (PMID 39658251; DOI 10.1093/rheumatology/keae669)**: retrospective, n=95 IIM. ILD was associated with avascular zones (P=0.004), capillary loss (P=0.04) and microhaemorrhages (P=0.04). Capillary loss correlated with %FVC (rs = −0.46) and enlarged capillaries with %FVC (rs = −0.57). sKL-6 correlated with avascular areas (rs=0.64). The predictors of ILD **presence** in multiple logistic regression were male sex, respiratory symptoms, %FVC, %DLCO, sKL-6, anti-Jo1 and NVC abnormalities. The outcome was diagnostic, not progression, and incremental metrics were not reported. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/keae669)
- **Markusse et al. 2017, Rheumatology (Oxford) (PMID 27940596; DOI 10.1093/rheumatology/kew402)**: Leiden cohort, n=287 SSc, cross-sectional. The association between NVC pattern and ILD (OR 1.3–1.4 per pattern step) was **independent of the specific anti-ENA antibody**. Title: "complementary value of NVC patterns and disease-specific autoantibodies". ILD here was a presence outcome, not progression. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/kew402)
- **EUSTAR longitudinal analysis 2025, J Scleroderma Relat Disord (PMID 39777215; DOI 10.1177/23971983241307692)**: n=771 SSc with a scleroderma NVC pattern, HRCT-confirmed absence of ILD and disease duration ≤5 years. The baseline NVC pattern did **not** predict 5-year incident ILD (adjusted HR 1.09, 95% CI 0.86–1.39). However, **worsening** of the NVC pattern was associated with higher annual ILD risk (OR 3.76, 95% CI 1.99–7.11). — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1177/23971983241307692)
- **Capillary loss and mortality (Clin Rheumatol 2017; PMID 29018976; DOI 10.1007/s10067-017-3869-1)**: n=170 SSc, mean follow-up 10.1 years, 73 deaths. The avascular score gave univariable HR 1.64 (1.22–2.19). After adjustment for age, skin score, sex, race, peripheral ischaemia **and extent of ILD** (from HRCT), the HR fell to 1.15 (0.80–1.65, p=0.445). This is the only retrieved study that adjusted an NVC prognostic effect for CT ILD extent. The NVC signal was largely absorbed, though the outcome was mortality, not ILD progression. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10067-017-3869-1)
- South Australian Register (Intern Med J 2010; PMID 20546058; DOI 10.1111/j.1445-5994.2010.02281.x): n=786. Increased nailfold capillary dropout showed only a "trend" toward worse survival. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1445-5994.2010.02281.x)
- Exploratory miRNA study (Respir Res 2025; PMID 41350880; DOI 10.1186/s12931-025-03392-6): n=24 SARD-ILD. Variables included NVC alterations, KL-6 and radiographic pattern. miR-483-5p was higher with microvascular (NVC) involvement, and several miRNAs were linked to a progressive phenotype over 18 months. This is hypothesis-generating only. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12931-025-03392-6)
- Practice context: a 2026 European MCTD survey (PMID 42791152; DOI 10.1016/j.ejim.2026.107199) found 74.1% use HRCT and 71.9% use NFC at baseline, and 35.6% did not repeat NFC after a scleroderma pattern. NVC and CT are routinely co-collected but not jointly modelled. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejim.2026.107199)
- Reviews state that the role of NVC in predicting ILD progression "remains to be established" (Autoimmun Rev 2017; PMID 28576600; DOI 10.1016/j.autrev.2017.05.019) and that "high-performance algorithms" for CTD-ILD progression are needed (Mediterr J Rheumatol 2023 protocol; PMID 38282947; DOI 10.31138/mjr.20230804.tr). — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.autrev.2017.05.019); [Source](https://doi.org/10.31138/mjr.20230804.tr)

### Inferences
- The question is **partially done**. NVC → FVC/DLCO decline has been shown in one small prospective SSc cohort (n=74). NVC + KL-6 has been shown for ILD presence in IIM. NVC + autoantibodies has been shown for ILD presence in SSc. The **formal incremental test is untested**: a baseline model with CT fibrotic burden (visual Goh extent or QCT) ± FVC, with NVC added, assessed by ΔC-index, NRI or IDI for PPF or FVC decline.
- The data run in opposite directions. Static baseline NVC adds little for incident ILD (EUSTAR) and for mortality after CT adjustment (Clin Rheumatol 2017). *Dynamic* NVC (pattern worsening) and specific features (disorganization, microhaemorrhage) carry signal. A credible design would test **change in NVC** or specific quantitative parameters, not only the baseline Cutolo pattern.

### Gaps
- No study reported C-index, AUC, NRI or IDI for NVC added to CT-based models.
- No NVC study was found in RA-ILD progression.
- No retrieved study modelled NVC + quantitative CT, NVC + SP-D, or NVC + CT vascular metrics together.
- The covariate set of Sieiro Santos 2025 (whether baseline HRCT extent was included) could not be confirmed from the abstract. The full text needs checking.

## B1. Which chest CT features predict FVC decline, PPF or mortality in CTD-ILD?

### Takeaway
Baseline **CT extent** (Goh 20% threshold) is the established prognostic anchor in SSc-ILD. **Quantitative CT** adds prognostic information in several studies: CALIPER pulmonary vessel volume (PVV) and vessel-related structures (VRS), histogram indices and CII, honeycombing on texture analysis, and early serial GGO change. The evidence on PVV is the most consistent: it independently predicts mortality in mixed CTD-ILD, RA-ILD and SSc-ILD. Prediction of *progression* (as opposed to mortality) from baseline CT is weaker. In one large 2026 radiomics study, baseline features were "overall comparable between progressors and non-progressors", and only honeycombing predicted progression.

### Cited Findings
**Visual extent / staging**
- **Goh et al. 2008, AJRCCM (PMID 18369202; DOI 10.1164/rccm.200706-877OC)**: n=215 SSc-ILD. HRCT extent was a powerful mortality predictor (P<0.0005), with an optimal threshold of 20%. The limited/extensive staging (with FVC 70% used in indeterminate 10–30% cases) gave HR 3.46 (2.19–5.46). This was better than HRCT 20% alone (HR 2.48) or FVC 70% alone (HR 2.11). Prognostic separation was lower for trainees (HR 1.87–2.60) than practitioners (HR 3.39–3.82). — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1164/rccm.200706-877OC)
- van Doorn-Hogervorst et al. 2025, Semin Arthritis Rheum (PMID 39914260; DOI 10.1016/j.semarthrit.2025.152650): n=185 SSc. Visually scored disease extent at the apices predicted change in TLC with **adjusted R² = 0.024**, and reticulation at mid-level gave R² = 0.026. Baseline CT features explained only about 2–3% of the variance in 1-year PFT change. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.semarthrit.2025.152650)
- Carnevale et al. 2020, Clin Rheumatol (PMID 32880053; DOI 10.1007/s10067-020-05375-y): n=129 SSc (58 with a 12–24-month interval). Change in semi-quantitative CT score correlated with ΔFVC (r = −0.487) and ΔDLCO (r = −0.298). Of 25 patients progressive by OMERACT PFT criteria, only 5 had ΔCT >5%. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10067-020-05375-y)

**Quantitative CT – CALIPER / texture / vessels**
- **Jacob et al. 2016, BMC Med (PMID 27876024; DOI 10.1186/s12916-016-0739-7)**: n=203 mixed CTD-ILD. In Cox analysis, the independent mortality predictors were age, smoking, KCO and **CALIPER pulmonary vessel volume** (P<0.0001). Automated CALIPER stratification predicted mortality more strongly than all CT and functional indices. Replacing PFT with CT strata in ILD-GAP kept the AUC (0.74 vs 0.72). The combined stratified CT-GAP model gave 1-year AUC 0.89 vs 0.87 and 2-year AUC 0.88 vs 0.86 for ILD-GAP. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12916-016-0739-7)
- **Jacob et al. 2019, Eur Respir J (PMID 30487199; DOI 10.1183/13993003.00869-2018)**: RA-ILD. The Goh scleroderma system gave HR 3.78, the Fleischner IPF-pattern system HR 1.98, and a CALIPER **VRS threshold of 4.4%** HR 3.10. Combining the visual systems into a "progressive fibrotic system" gave C-statistic 0.71 and identified 23% with IPF-like survival. Adding VRS gave C-statistic **0.77** and VRS independently predicted outcome. This is an example of a formal incremental test (ΔC) of a CT vascular metric over visual staging, and a template for testing NVC. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1183/13993003.00869-2018)
- **Iacovantuono et al. 2026, Rheumatology (Oxford) (PMID 42658665; DOI 10.1093/rheumatology/keag463)**: n=267 SSc-ILD, Imbio LTA radiomics, median follow-up 42 months, 65 deaths. **PVV% independently predicted mortality** (adjusted HR 1.21, 1.09–1.35; upper zones HR 1.28). In 160 patients with yearly visits, 50.6% had at least one progression episode. **Baseline radiomic features were overall comparable between progressors and non-progressors.** Only whole-lung **honeycombing** independently predicted progression (aOR 1.53, 1.17–2.01). — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/keag463)
- Li et al. 2025, Quant Imaging Med Surg (PMID 40235767; DOI 10.21037/qims-24-1770): n=168 CTD-ILD. AI artery/vein analysis found that arterial and venous **BV5** volume, density and fraction and vessel tortuosity differed across GAP stages 1–3. BV5 correlated with PFT (r 0.179–0.608). In a 12-patient longitudinal subset, BV5 fell as ILD worsened. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.21037/qims-24-1770)
- Cheng et al. 2026, J Thorac Dis (PMID 42306716; DOI 10.21037/jtd-2025-1-2705): n=84 CTD (51 ILD), inspiratory/expiratory QCT. BV5, BV10, total blood volume and vessel counts were reduced in ILD. A diagnostic nomogram (age, sex, DLCO%, FEV1%, expiratory HAA%, fSAD ratio, vessel count at 12 mm) gave AUC 0.963. This was diagnostic, not prognostic. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.21037/jtd-2025-1-2705)
- Yang et al. 2025, Acad Radiol (PMID 40382278; DOI 10.1016/j.acra.2025.04.012): n=348 PM/DM. MLD and HAA% were higher, and lung volume and intrapulmonary vessel volume lower, in ILD. A random forest gave test AUC 0.843 (diagnostic). — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.acra.2025.04.012)
- RA QCT studies (diagnostic or structural only): Pu 2023 (PMID 36847056; DOI 10.3233/XST-221329) measured PA diameter, PA/Ao and peripheral vessel cross-sectional area; peripheral vessel area had AUC 0.894 for RA lung change. Yang 2026 (PMID 42410211; DOI 10.1007/s10067-026-08273-x) found small-vessel volume (TVV5mm) and small-airway loss in RA (combined AUC 0.837). — [Source](https://doi.org/10.3233/XST-221329); [Source](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10067-026-08273-x)

**Quantitative CT – density histogram (Ariani; Bocchino/Rea CII)**
- Ariani et al. 2015, Mod Rheumatol (PMID 25736361; DOI 10.3109/14397595.2015.1016200): n=257 SSc, 6 centres. QCT indices correlated with visual extent (r −0.60 to 0.58) and PFT (r −0.54 to 0.52), and discriminated poor-prognosis groups. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.3109/14397595.2015.1016200)
- Ariani et al. 2017, Rheumatology (PMID 28160007; DOI 10.1093/rheumatology/kew480): n=146 SSc. Histogram QCT parameters discriminated high vs low mortality risk as defined by **ILD-GAP and du Bois** indices. This was a surrogate outcome, not observed mortality. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/kew480)
- Ariani et al. 2014 (PMID 25316138; no DOI): n=63. Skewness had the strongest correlation with visual extent (r = −0.378). Ariani 2013 (PMID 23949623; DOI 10.1007/s00296-013-2845-6) found kurtosis vs MLA rho 0.885, with a mean QCT time of about 1.85 min. — [Source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25316138/); [Source](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00296-013-2845-6)
- Ariani et al. 2016, Clin Respir J (PMID 26899794; DOI 10.1111/crj.12451): n=63. QCT indices discriminated exercise desaturation as well as PFT did. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1111/crj.12451)
- Sambataro et al. 2022, Clin Exp Rheumatol (PMID 36189912; DOI 10.55563/clinexprheumatol/7q5gkz): n=60 SSc. Skewness <1.85 identified high EUSTAR disease activity with sensitivity 79.5% and specificity 68.7%. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.55563/clinexprheumatol/7q5gkz)
- **Bocchino et al. 2019, Sci Rep (PMID 31263199; DOI 10.1038/s41598-019-45990-7)**: n=83 SSc, prospective. The CII was built by PCA of MLA, skewness and kurtosis. It discriminated ILD with AUC 0.77. **34% of patients with visually ILD-free CT had a CII below the cutoff**, and 67% of those had DLCO <80%. CII correlated with sIL-2Rα and CCL18. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-019-45990-7)
- **Rea et al. 2024, Sci Rep (PMID 39730381; DOI 10.1038/s41598-024-77328-3)**: this cohort is **IPF**, not CTD (n=73). CII was associated with survival (adjusted HR 0.35, 0.2–0.63, after GAP and antifibrotics). The paper states that CII "predicts mortality in systemic sclerosis patients", citing earlier work I did not retrieve. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-77328-3)

**Early serial CT change**
- Motta et al. 2026, Rheumatology (PMID 42426553; DOI 10.1093/rheumatology/keag346): n=33 SSc-ILD. An early (about 1-year) **automated GGO increase >50 mL (8.2%)** predicted subsequent functional progression with 81% sensitivity and 77% specificity. Visual scoring did not. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/keag346)
- SLS II (Volkmann 2023, Arthritis Care Res; PMID 36504432; DOI 10.1002/acr.25070): QILD and QLF in the lobe of maximal involvement were used as 2-year radiographic progression endpoints. Reflux symptom severity predicted QCT progression and esophageal dilation did not. Tocilizumab meta-analysis (PMID 37773003; DOI 10.1513/AnnalsATS.202301-056OC): QILD and QLF at 48 weeks favoured tocilizumab. Together these show QCT is in use as a trial endpoint. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1002/acr.25070); [Source](https://doi.org/10.1513/AnnalsATS.202301-056OC)

**AI reviews (2026)**
- Poellinger, Curr Opin Rheumatol 2026 (PMID 42712118; DOI 10.1097/BOR.0000000000001201): in SSc-ILD, "deep-learning UIP probability stratifies FVC decline and long-term survival". The review recommends human-in-the-loop use and says prospective multicentre validation, protocol harmonization and calibration are needed before AI outputs can trigger treatment. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1097/BOR.0000000000001201)
- Jiang, World J Radiol 2026 (PMID 42812907; DOI 10.4329/wjr.125041): QCT is "variably implemented across centers"; radiomics/ML is "predominantly investigational". — [Source](https://doi.org/10.4329/wjr.125041)

**Reproducibility / harmonization of QCT**
- Ahn et al. 2023, Acad Radiol (PMID 37516583; DOI 10.1016/j.acra.2023.06.008): n=194. The reconstruction kernel changed quantified reticulation and honeycombing (B30f underestimated, B50f overestimated; 95% LOA up to −5.0 to 12.7%). Deep-learning kernel conversion narrowed the LOA to −2.7 to 2.1%. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.acra.2023.06.008)
- Ahn et al. 2025, AJR (PMID 41474222; DOI 10.2214/AJR.25.34057): n=48, prospective, same-day scans on GE vs Siemens. Fibrosis score CCC was 0.953–0.998. The lung-B60f pair had a mean difference of −0.80% (LOA −3.4 to 1.8%), and the worst kernel pair −2.52%. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.2214/AJR.25.34057)
- An expert panel (Sambataro 2021; PMID 34280961; DOI 10.1515/jbcpp-2021-0110) supported QCT feasibility and validity in CTD-ILD, **except** for its ability to recognise elementary lesions. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1515/jbcpp-2021-0110)

### Inferences
- For a "CT + NVC" model, CT should be represented by visual extent (Goh) plus at least one QCT metric. PVV/VRS is the strongest replicated quantitative mortality predictor in CTD-ILD. Because PVV is itself a vascular metric, testing NVC against PVV would directly address the redundancy-vs-complementarity question.
- Histogram indices are cheap with open-source software but are mostly validated against surrogates (GAP, du Bois, disease activity) rather than observed progression in CTD-ILD.
- Kernel and vendor effects of about 1–3% fibrosis score are similar in size to clinically meaningful annual change. Multicentre QCT needs standardized protocols or kernel harmonization.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve a dedicated study of **PA diameter or PA/Ao predicting SSc-ILD progression**. My combined search failed and returned irrelevant results, so this remains unverified.
- I did not retrieve SSc-specific CII mortality data (cited in Rea 2024 but not fetched). Visual traction bronchiectasis and honeycombing studies in RA-ILD and IIM-ILD were also not retrieved individually.
- Inspiration-level (spirometric gating) effects on CTD-ILD QCT were not specifically retrieved.

## B2. Does baseline CT extent leave much unexplained variance? What do guidelines say about predictors of progression?

### Takeaway
Yes. Several lines of evidence show that baseline CT burden explains only a small share of subsequent progression. Baseline radiomics did not separate progressors from non-progressors in SSc-ILD. Visual CT features explained about 2–3% of PFT change. Progression in one year did not predict progression in the next. Guidelines endorse HRCT and PFT for monitoring and define PPF partly on CT, but none lists NVC as a validated progression predictor.

### Cited Findings
- Iacovantuono 2026 (PMID 42658665): "Baseline radiomic features were overall comparable between progressors and non-progressors". Only honeycombing predicted progression (aOR 1.53). — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/keag463)
- van Doorn-Hogervorst 2025 (PMID 39914260): the best HRCT predictors of TLC change had adjusted R² of about 0.02–0.03. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.semarthrit.2025.152650)
- **Hoffmann-Vold et al. 2025, Lancet Rheumatol (PMID 40381640; DOI 10.1016/S2665-9913(25)00026-8)**: n=231 SSc-ILD (Oslo/Zurich), validated in 121 (with Michigan). Progression by FVC ≥5% occurred in 31%, by ≥10% in 16%, by PPF in 17% and by PF-ILD in 39%. Progression from visit 1 to 2 did **not** predict progression from visit 2 to 3 (FVC≥5%: OR 0.28, 0.12–0.63; PPF: OR 0.93, 0.39–2.22). FVC decline ≥5% predicted mortality (HR 1.66, 1.05–2.62). The authors call for treating patients "at risk of progression", which needs better baseline predictors. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2665-9913(25)00026-8)
- SENSCIS subgroup analysis (Khanna 2023, RMD Open; PMID 36796874; DOI 10.1136/rmdopen-2022-002859): among patients with ≥10% fibrotic extent on HRCT (an entry criterion), FVC decline in the placebo arm varied by **clinical** factors. It was −167.8 mL/yr with early SSc (<18 months), −131.7 mL/yr with mRSS ≥18 and −100.7 mL/yr with elevated CRP or platelets, vs −93.3 mL/yr overall. In other words, rate of decline differed by non-CT factors among patients who all met the same CT extent threshold. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1136/rmdopen-2022-002859)
- EUSTAR incident ILD (Petelytska 2026, Ann Rheum Dis; PMID 41549016; DOI 10.1016/j.ard.2025.12.008): n=5331 with negative baseline HRCT. Incidence of new ILD was 3.83/100 person-years, and new cases kept appearing up to 10 years. Risk factors were NYHA ≥2, muscle weakness, high inflammatory markers and SSc-specific autoantibodies. Incident ILD carried about double the mortality of ILD-negative patients. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ard.2025.12.008)
- **2022 ATS/ERS/JRS/ALAT guideline (Raghu et al., AJRCCM; PMID 35486072; DOI 10.1164/rccm.202202-0399ST)**: PPF = at least two of three criteria within the past year (worsening symptoms, **radiological progression**, physiological progression) with no alternative explanation, in non-IPF ILD. It gives a conditional recommendation for nintedanib. The abstract does not list baseline predictors of progression. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1164/rccm.202202-0399ST)
- **2023 ACR/CHEST screening and monitoring guideline (Johnson et al., Arthritis Care Res 2024; PMID 38973729; DOI 10.1002/acr.25347)**: conditionally recommends PFTs and HRCT for screening at-risk SARD patients (RA, SSc, IIM, MCTD, SjD). For monitoring it conditionally recommends PFT, HRCT and ambulatory desaturation testing, and conditionally recommends against 6MWD, chest radiography and bronchoscopy. It gives guidance on ILD risk factors. The abstract does not mention NVC. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1002/acr.25347)
- **2025 ERS/EULAR CTD-ILD guideline (Antoniou et al., Ann Rheum Dis 2025, PMID 40912974, DOI 10.1016/j.ard.2025.08.021; co-published Eur Respir J, PMID 40907995)**: 25 PICO and 28 narrative questions across SSc, RA, IIM, SjD, SLE and MCTD. For **assessment of risk for ILD progression in MCTD, SjD and SLE**, the task force found **insufficient evidence to make recommendations**. Many recommendations rest on low-certainty evidence. The abstract does not mention NVC. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ard.2025.08.021)
- Esposito 2025, Chest review (PMID 41397518) on incorporating SSc-ILD guidelines into practice: title only retrieved, no content extracted.

### Inferences
- Baseline CT extent is necessary but not sufficient. A considerable share of the variance in progression is unexplained, and progression is episodic (Hoffmann-Vold). This justifies testing complementary, biologically distinct markers such as microvascular NVC and activity markers like KL-6, ideally with dynamic NVC change.
- None of the three guidelines, judging from their abstracts, positions NVC as a progression predictor. An "NVC + CT" study would address a recognised evidence gap, especially for MCTD, SjD and SLE, where ERS/EULAR explicitly lacks evidence.

### Gaps
- The full guideline texts were not read, so specific risk-factor tables (for example, ACR/CHEST risk factors by disease, or ERS/EULAR SSc progression risk factors) and any mention of NVC could not be verified.
- No retrieved study quantified the proportion of variance in progression explained by baseline CT extent in a large CTD-ILD cohort (for example, R² or C-index of extent alone for PPF).
